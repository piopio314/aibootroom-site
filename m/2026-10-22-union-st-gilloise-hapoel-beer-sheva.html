Union St. Gilloise play a 'home' tie in an empty stadium

A home game with no home

Union St. Gilloise's home tie in the Europa League is not at home at all. Leuven's mayor banned the game at Den Dreef for geopolitical reasons, and Union's own Stade Marien does not meet UEFA regulations, so the match is played behind closed doors in Düsseldorf [1]. Both clubs have said they regret it, with one club figure quoted saying: "It's really annoying. Football is for the fans." [2]

That matters because Union are the clear favourites and would normally have a crowd behind them. Union, coached by David Hubert [5], are unbeaten in their last five matches in all competitions and have four points from two Europa League games, which puts them 12th in the 36-team table. Beer Sheva have one point from two, sit 25th, and lost 1-2 at AZ Alkmaar last week.

The market makes Union 71.1% to win, more than six times Beer Sheva's 11.8%. The gap is wide enough that the empty stadium is a detail rather than the whole story — but it is the reason to be careful about how much the word "home" is worth here.

Union's numbers are better than their results

Union's five-match run reads three wins and two draws, with 10 goals scored and two conceded. The chance numbers are stronger than the scorelines: 2.18 expected goals created per game against 0.84 allowed. Against Real Sociedad in their last European outing they out-created their opponents 1.92 to 0.76 and still only drew 1-1 — the result undersold them.

Beer Sheva's run is three wins, one draw and one defeat, and it looks less solid underneath. They beat Hapoel Katamon 2-1 at home with 2.71 xG, but at AZ they gave up 2.75 xG in that 1-2 loss. Away from home that defensive number is the weak point, and here they do not even get a crowd to lean on. Both sides had four days between matches.

Two defenders out, three players reported back

Union are without defender K. Mac Allister, who has played 638 minutes this season at a 7.3 rating, and fellow defender O. Kricfalusi, 534 minutes at 7.27. Losing two regular defenders would worry most teams; Union have conceded twice in their last five games, so the unit has held. R. Schoofs is also out.

The better news is reported rather than confirmed: Circus Daily says three important players return for this tie [3], and Le Soir reports that Union trained in Düsseldorf on Wednesday evening with notable players back [4]. Beer Sheva's news runs the other way. Defender I. Rotman (506 minutes this season) and midfielder Lucas Ventura (492) are newly listed as doubtful — two of their most-used players, and part of the spine of the team.

Seventy-one per cent, and a line of a goal and a half

The win chances come from the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed: Union 71.1%, the draw 17.0%, Beer Sheva 11.8%. On the handicap the main line is Union -1.5, which means Union must win by two clear goals for that line to land. The fair line sits at -1.54 — the market's way of saying Union are about a goal and a half the stronger team, and that a two-goal win is close to a coin flip.

For goals, the main line is 3.25 and the under is marginally favoured: the over is 47.2%, and the fair total is 3.14 goals. That fits a Union side that scores plenty and concedes very little. The market has shifted slightly away from Union in recent days: Union's win chance has edged down by about a point, the fair handicap has moved from -1.61 to -1.54 goals, and the goals line has eased as well — a neutral, empty stadium and two Beer Sheva regulars short of full fitness both point the same way.

The football and the market agree

Both point the same way. Union create far more than they allow, they have not lost since mid-September, and Beer Sheva's away defence is the softest part of their game — so a chance near 71% and a line of a goal and a half are not a stretch. The empty stadium in Düsseldorf trims the home advantage, and the small drift in Union's numbers shows the market has taken that in. Even so, the gap in quality is wide enough that Union should control the game. Whether they win it by two is much closer, and a fair total near three goals says the chances will come at both ends.
