A cup story against a 0.08 night

Ferencvaros have won both of their Europa League league-phase matches and sit on six points from two games. On Thursday they host Torreense in Budapest, and the table already tells you which side is comfortable. [1]

Torreense's presence here is a genuine story. They qualified by winning the Portuguese Cup final against Sporting Lisbon while playing in the second division, and the club had spent a long spell outside Portugal's top tier, the BBC reported [8]. They travelled with a 25-man squad, according to A Bola, and trained on the Groupama Aréna pitch on Wednesday [9][13].

But the football gap is recent and clear. A week ago Torreense led Sunderland at home and lost 2-1 to a late Noah Sadiki goal [7]. In that match they produced 0.08 expected goals, the measure of chance quality, while allowing 1.82. That number sits behind almost everything else in this preview.

Unbeaten, but three draws in five

Ferencvaros are unbeaten in five: two wins and three draws. The draws matter. On Sunday they went to Kisvarda and drew 1-1 with 2.74 expected goals to 0.94, which says they created plenty and finished little. Their European form is sharper — a 2-1 home win over Plzen six days ago, with both goals from Zachariassen [1]. Across those five games they average 2.25 expected goals and allow 1.5.

Torreense have had the quieter month: a 0-1 league defeat at Tondela, then the Sunderland loss. Their best night was the 2-1 win away at Lillestrom in September, and a Hungarian preview notes they have looked more convincing in Europe this season than in their own league [6]. They sit 19th of 36 with three points, so a result in Budapest would leave them well placed. Ferencvaros played four days ago; Torreense have had seven days to prepare.

Nearly full strength at home

Ferencvaros' absentee list is long in names but light in weight. The biggest is forward L. Joseph, with 280 minutes and two goals across 11 appearances this season. N. Keita is doubtful after 137 minutes, and E. Botka and M. Bero have 69 and five minutes between them. No regular starter is missing, so the side that beat Plzen is close to the side that will start here.

Torreense have one doubt, A. Baradji, who has no minutes this season. Coach Luís Tralhão has a full group to choose from [10]. Ferencvaros coach Balázs Borbély said "there are no easy games at European level" ahead of the match, and Ádám Nagy also spoke about Torreense's progress [2][3]. The former Portugal international Carlos Simões warned that Ferencvaros should not treat Torreense as a second-division team [12].

The story is theirs, the chances are not

Ferencvaros are favoured because they win at home in Europe, because they create around two and a quarter expected goals a game, and because their absentees are squad players rather than starters. Torreense are outsiders because they created almost nothing against Sunderland and now must do it away, on four days' less rest than they would like, against a side that has not lost in five. The cup story is real, and the draw at Kisvarda shows Ferencvaros can be held when they waste chances. But the market's read and the football's read agree here: a home win is more likely than not, and by roughly a goal and a half.
