A 0-4, then Bologna

The first question about Cagliari this week is not about the table. It is about how a team answers a 0-4. Monza beat them on Sunday [1], and the unbeaten run that carried Cagliari through September ended in one afternoon.

The table still looks good for the hosts. Cagliari are seventh with 13 points from seven games [2], a strong start to the season. Bologna are 15th, with five points and one win.

And yet the bookmakers we compare make Bologna the favourites for Saturday: 38.0% to win, against 32.7% for the draw and 29.3% for Cagliari. That is the tension of this match. The season so far says one thing, the prices say another, and the distance between them is small enough that both can be right.

Points built on narrow margins

Cagliari's 13 points have arrived in a particular way. Their three wins this season were all by a single goal — 1-0, 2-1, 1-0 — and over their last five matches they have averaged 1.34 xG created and 1.68 allowed. Against Juventus they managed 0.52 xG and drew 1-1. At Udinese they won 1-0 while giving up 2.13 xG. The Monza afternoon fits that pattern more than it breaks it: Cagliari created 2.11 and allowed 2.10, and still lost 0-4.

Bologna's recent numbers are loose at the other end. They lost 1-3 at home to Inter on 17 October, their first defeat under Raffaele Palladino [9], and Inter out-created them 3.16 to 1.0. Their only league win came at Lecce, 2-1; at Napoli they produced 0.29 xG. Over the same five-match window they average 1.21 created and 1.72 allowed. Bologna have had seven days to prepare, Cagliari five — and the last two meetings finished 0-0 and 0-2, with Cagliari scoring in neither.

Bologna's attack thins out

Bologna's forward line is the story of the week. The club confirmed that Roberto Piccoli will be out for about three weeks after the injury he picked up against Inter [6]. Italian media report that Riccardo Orsolini is training separately and that his availability for Saturday is uncertain [7]. Cagliari News 24 also reports a morning session at Casteldebole with treatment for both players and their presence still to be assessed [8].

For Cagliari, Riyad Idrissi is out with a knee problem. There is better news elsewhere: Yael Trepy trained fully with the first team for the first time since his swimming pool accident last August, according to ANSA [3], and the club confirmed he is working with the squad [4]. Fabio Bazzani, a former member of Cagliari's staff, said he expects an aggressive home performance after the Monza defeat [5].

A fraction of a goal

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Bologna are 38.0% to win, the draw 32.7% and Cagliari 29.3%. Those are the numbers of a near-even game, and the handicap agrees: Cagliari are priced at +0.25, with a fair line of +0.15, so Bologna are rated a fraction of a goal better — not a full one.

The goals line is 2.25, with Over at 49.0% and both teams to score at 51.1%. The fair line has slipped since Sunday, from 2.27 to 2.22, and Over 2.25 has moved down 1.7 points. That is consistent with a Bologna attack that may be missing two starters, and with two recent meetings that produced two goals in total. The win prices have barely moved, with the draw up 0.4 points. Steady prices next to Cagliari's seventh place and Bologna's fifteenth are the market's way of saying it rates Bologna's squad above Cagliari's results.

Both can be right

The football points at Cagliari: more points, home advantage, the better season. The prices point at Bologna, but gently — a narrow favourite, a fraction-of-a-goal handicap, a goals line just above two. What connects the two is how Cagliari have collected those points, by single-goal margins while creating less than they allow, against a Bologna side that has just lost its striker and possibly its winger. A tight game with few goals, settled by one moment, is what the table and the prices both describe. Cagliari's chance is real. So is Bologna's. Neither is close to certain.
