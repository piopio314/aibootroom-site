A lunchtime meeting from two ends of the table

Bramall Lane has the early kick-off on Saturday, and the table makes the match look simple. Sheffield United are 11th with 15 points from 11 games and have won two of their last three in the Championship [1]. Derby are 23rd of 24 with six points and one league win all season. That gap is the story, and the market has priced it that way.

For Derby the trip is a chance to stop a run that is starting to shape their season. Four goals scored and nine conceded in their last five, no win in that spell, and a goal difference of -11 [8]. They did draw 1-1 at Burnley in September, so they have shown they can take a point on the road.

Sheffield United arrive the other way. They won 1-0 at Bristol City last Saturday [2], days after a 3-2 defeat at Portsmouth in which they played with 10 men [4]. Chris Wilder has said the long run of away games can now pay off, with Derby and Wrexham visiting Bramall Lane [3]. His side comes into the game in the better condition of the two.

Same chances created, different results

Sheffield United's last five read W-L-W-L-L: seven goals scored, six conceded, and expected goals (xG, the quality of the chances) of 1.06 for and 1.18 against per game. The 3-0 home win over Lincoln was kinder than the performance, at 0.68 xG for and 1.39 against. The 1-0 at Bristol City was the opposite: fully deserved at 1.41 to 0.67. They have had seven days to recover.

Derby's run is L-L-D-D-L, with four scored and nine conceded, and averages of 1.06 xG for and 1.44 against. That attacking number is exactly the same as Sheffield United's, which is the interesting part. Derby's best display of the run was the 1-1 at Burnley, where they created 1.75 and allowed 0.45 and still did not win. Creating is not their main problem. Finishing is, and the defence leaks at the other end.

Derby's problems are all at the back

Derby's injury list falls almost entirely on the defence. Goalkeeper J. Widell Zetterstrom, who has played 725 minutes across eight league games at a rating of 7.27, is out. So are defenders S. Langas (917 minutes, an ever-present this season), C. Taylor (774) and C. Forsyth. The club added Jack Robinson, a former Sheffield United player, on a three-month deal [6] — cover that knows the ground.

John Eustace said he hopes the injury Lewis Travis picked up against Stoke is not serious, according to Yahoo Sports [5], and Derby have been described as injury-hit before the trip [7]. Sheffield United's own list is short: F. Seriki (287 minutes) and J. Shackleton (101 minutes) are out, so Wilder is close to a full squad.

Half a goal, and a line that says narrow

Across the ten bookmakers we compare, Sheffield United are 52.0% to win, the draw 23.9% and Derby 24.1% — the home chance is more than double the away one. The handicap explains it better than a percentage. The fair line is Sheffield United -0.6, with the main line at -0.5, so the market rates them about two-thirds of a goal the better team. A one-goal home win is the middle of that picture, not a big margin.

The goals side agrees with the team news. The fair line is 2.62, Over 2.5 is at 52.7% and both teams to score at 54.8% — a slightly open game, which fits a patched-up defence in front of a stand-in keeper. Those numbers have held steady all week, consistent with injuries that were already known. The caution sits in the history: Derby won 3-1 at Bramall Lane in November 2025, and the last three meetings have all gone to the away side.

Favourites for a win, not for a rout

Put the two halves together and they point the same way. Sheffield United are at home, in better form, against a side 23rd in the table whose goalkeeper and three regular defenders are missing. That is why the market makes them 52.0% favourites and rates them about 0.6 goals better. But the same numbers say the margin should be small: a goals line of 2.62 and a handicap of -0.6 describe a controlled home win rather than a rout, and Derby have won at this ground before. Sheffield United are the more likely winners. They are not certain ones.
