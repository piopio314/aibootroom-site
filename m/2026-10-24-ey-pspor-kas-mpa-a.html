A bottom side with no goals, a top-half side with one defeat

Eyüpspor are bottom of the Süper Lig and have lost their last five league matches without scoring a single goal. The aggregate score in that run is 0-19. On Saturday morning they host Kasımpaşa, a side seventh in the table with one defeat all season.

The table shows the size of the problem. Eyüpspor have three points from eight games, one win and seven defeats, and a goal difference of -20. The 0-8 at Fenerbahçe is the loudest number in that run, but every week has been heavy. A visit from a top-half team is not the fixture a side with no goals would choose.

Kasımpaşa come from the other direction. Emre Belözoğlu's team beat Samsunspor 4-1 at home on Matchday 8 [4], a result that took them to 13 points, and they have been beaten once in eight league games. They have also won three of the last four meetings between the clubs, including a 1-0 in March.

The chance data agrees with the results

Eyüpspor's run is no accident. Over their last five matches they created 0.93 expected goals a game and allowed 2.84. Even the 0-4 defeat to Göztepe [1] came with 1.33 xG created against 3.15 allowed — they made chances that afternoon and still lost by four. The problem sits at both ends, which is why five straight defeats have followed.

Kasımpaşa's last five read W-L-D-W-D, with 1.17 xG created and 1.42 allowed per game. The 4-1 win over Samsunspor was built on 2.41 xG, but the 0-0 with Konyaspor came with only 0.59 created against 1.22 allowed. That is the shape of this team: hard to beat, rarely dominant. Both had a full week to prepare, seven days' rest for Eyüpspor and six for Kasımpaşa.

Short lists on both sides

Eyüpspor's absentee list is thin. Y. Barasi is doubtful with an injury after 293 minutes across eight appearances this season, without a goal. He is a rotation forward rather than a fixed starter, so his likely absence thins the options without reshaping the attack — the scoring problem runs deeper than one player.

For Kasımpaşa, M. Ouanes is doubtful with a muscle injury after 78 minutes this season, while K. Corekci (Achilles) and G. Frimpong are out. Jesurun Rak-Sakyi, on loan from Crystal Palace, has impressed for the club, according to Read Crystal Palace [6].

Clear favourites, but only a goal better

The bookmakers we compare — ten of them, with the margin removed — make Kasımpaşa 60.6% to win. Eyüpspor are 15.9% and the draw 23.5%, so the visitors are rated almost four times more likely than the hosts. The handicap says the same thing in goals: Eyüpspor get a head start of one, and the fair line of 0.92 means the market expects a Kasımpaşa win by a little under one goal rather than a rout.

That gap between the table and the goal line is the interesting part. Kasımpaşa have drawn four of their eight games, and a team that draws half the time is not rated two goals better than anyone. The goals line leans under: over 2.5 is 47.0%, with the fair total at 2.39, and both teams to score is 46.6%. This week's moves were small and pointed the same way — the fair handicap line came in from 0.98 to 0.92, Kasımpaşa's win chance slipped 1.2 points, and the fair goals total fell from 2.46 to 2.39. A side that has not scored in five matches pulls the expected goals down, even as the underdog.

Our read

Everything here points one way except the size of the win. Eyüpspor cannot score — five games, no goals, 0-19 — and Kasımpaşa are clear favourites, beaten once in eight and winners of three of the last four meetings. But the market stops at about one goal, because Belözoğlu's team draws too often to be rated a heavy winner, and because a match with one side starving for a goal tends to stay small. Our read: Kasımpaşa are more likely than not to take the points, by a margin of roughly a goal, in a game that may not produce many.
