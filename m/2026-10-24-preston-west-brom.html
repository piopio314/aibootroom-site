Bottom against fifth at Deepdale

Saturday at Deepdale puts 24th against 5th. Preston have four points from 11 matches, one win and a goal difference of minus 15. West Brom have 20 points, six wins and a place in the promotion spots. After 11 rounds, the gap between the two clubs is 16 points.

The pressure sits on Graham Alexander. On Monday the club parted ways with coaches Peter Murphy and Andy Liddell [3], and Alexander told BBC Radio Lancashire that improvement "doesn't happen overnight" [1]. Preston are also looking at free-agent signings, with room for one more in their 25-man squad, as reported by the Lancashire Evening Post [6]. These are the moves of a club trying to stop a slide.

West Brom's week looked very different. They were two goals down at home to Blackburn on Saturday and won 3-2 [8]. Isaac Price said the squad believes in its depth after a second straight winner from a substitute [9], and BBC fan writer Chris Hall called them the Championship's comeback kings this season [10].

Who is available, and who is not

Preston's team news is mostly about who can play at all. Alexander has given an injury update on defenders Lewis Gibson and Liam Lindsay, reported by the Lancashire Evening Post [4], and the club is still looking at free agents [6]. The details of those two defenders' fitness have not been confirmed, so the shape of the back line may only be clear when the teams are named.

For West Brom the concern is Toby Collyer, who limped off with a hamstring problem on Saturday and is being assessed, according to the Express & Star [7]. He is a midfield option rather than a guaranteed starter, and West Brom came from two goals down to beat Blackburn 3-2, according to the BBC [8]. Price's words about the bench [9] suggest the visitors can cover one absence without changing how they play.

Our read

The football and the market tell one story. Preston are bottom with four points, create 0.8 xG a game and have just changed their coaching staff; West Brom are fifth, have won two in a row and came back from two goals down last week. So the market makes West Brom about twice as likely as Preston to take the points — a reflection of a 16-point gap after 11 games, and the half-goal handicap is a modest description of it. Over 2.5 goals at 51.0% says an open game is more likely than a tight one. West Brom are favourites, not certainties — a home crowd and a doubt in the visitors' midfield can change an afternoon. Even so, the evidence points to the away side.
