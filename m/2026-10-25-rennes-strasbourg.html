A favourite with a worry up front

Rennes host Strasbourg at Roazhon Park on Sunday afternoon with the table still kind and the mood around the club unsettled. Rennes lost 4-3 at Paris FC last weekend, in a match featuring four penalties, and missed the chance to go top of Ligue 1 [5]. They stay sixth, which is not a crisis, but the manner of the defeat was chaotic.

The bigger question is up front. Estéban Lepaul scored all three of Rennes' goals from the penalty spot at Paris FC and came off injured late on, and L'Équipe reports doubts over his availability for this game [2]. Off the pitch the noise has been about the coach: Daniel Riolo said on RMC's After Foot that Franck Haise 'ne maîtrise plus rien du tout' at Rennes [3]. According to foot01, Haise keeps the club's backing and most of the criticism is aimed at sporting director Loïc Désiré [4].

Strasbourg arrive with problems of their own. They have lost three Ligue 1 matches in a row, the latest 1-3 at home to PSG after taking the lead [6], and they sit 14th with seven points from seven games. Two clubs with difficult weeks, then, but only one of them is still rated a clear favourite.

Strasbourg's away numbers are the story

Rennes' last five matches have produced 19 goals, and the split is strange for a side near the top: nine scored, ten conceded. Four went in at Lyon, four more at Paris FC. Their expected goals averages in that run, 2.21 created and 2.16 allowed, describe a team that plays open games and does not hide it.

Strasbourg's numbers point the other way. Over their last five they average 1.26 xG created and 1.80 allowed, and away from home the attack has almost disappeared: 0.24 expected goals at Paris FC and 0.63 at Nice. Their one win in the run was a wild 6-2 at Troyes in early September. The three defeats came against PSG, Nice and Paris FC, which is a fair excuse, but the away chance creation is the part that matters here.

Lepaul waits, Yassine travels

Lepaul is the name to watch before kick-off. He has been the focal point of the Rennes attack, with seven goals in nine appearances this season, and a late fitness call on him changes the home side more than any other single decision. The club has not confirmed anything either way, and L'Équipe's report leaves it open [2].

Strasbourg's bright spot is Gessime Yassine, who has two goals in seven Ligue 1 games. Africa Foot reports that Chelsea are monitoring the youngster closely [8]. For a side with little to show on the road, his running between the lines is one of the few routes forward.

Three-quarters of a goal, and a line above three

The win probabilities are Rennes 54.5%, the draw 21.7%, Strasbourg 23.8%, so Rennes are clearly favoured over their visitors. The handicap line of -0.75 says the same in goals, rating Rennes about three-quarters of a goal better, so a one-goal home win would only half settle it in their favour while a two-goal win would settle it fully.

The goals line is 3.25, with over at 51.7% and both teams to score at 64.6%, and the market expects an open afternoon. The fair goals line has edged up in recent days, from 3.25 to 3.33, with over 3.25 moving only slightly, which is consistent with a Rennes team that has conceded four goals twice in its last five. Despite the Lepaul doubt and the noise around Haise, Rennes remain clear favourites at 54.5%, more than twice Strasbourg's 23.8%.

The doubt we flagged on Thursday

Earlier this week we wrote about Rennes before their Europa League trip, and the main point was the same one: their top scorer had gone off injured at Paris FC, with seven goals in nine appearances, and his availability was in question. Three days later the question has not been answered, and L'Équipe's report has not been replaced by anything firmer. It is still the first thing to check on the team sheet.

The read

The case for Rennes is simple. Strasbourg have created almost nothing away from home, and the handicap of three-quarters of a goal is built on exactly that gap. The case against a comfortable afternoon is Rennes' own defence, which has shipped four goals twice in five matches, and a goals line above three that expects both teams to find the net. Lepaul's fitness is the one thing that could tilt the shape of the afternoon, and it is the reason to look at the line-up before kick-off. Rennes are favourites, and an open game is the likelier picture.
