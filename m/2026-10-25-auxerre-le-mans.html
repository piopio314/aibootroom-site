Two moods at the Abbé-Deschamps

Two teams arrive at this game in very different moods. Auxerre won 3-1 at Le Havre on Sunday, a result their coach Will Still called a reference victory [1]. Le Mans lost 0-3 at home to Toulouse on Friday [6].

The table says the gap is small. Le Mans are 16th with 6 points from seven games, which is the play-off place, and as a promoted club they are already in a fight near the bottom [5]. Auxerre are 12th on 9 points, after two away trips in a row [2]. Before the win at Le Havre, they were the team in the play-off spot themselves [3] — one good afternoon moved them out of it, and one bad one can move them back.

Sunday also has a human note. Auxerre will pay tribute before kick-off to a long-time season-ticket holder who died during an amateur match [4]. Then the football starts, with the home side trying to win a third game in four.

Wins that look better than the numbers

Auxerre's run is good but not clean: three wins and two defeats in five, with 8 goals scored and 9 conceded. Both defeats came away, 1-4 at Rennes and 1-3 at Lyon. Their last five matches give them 1.57 expected goals for and 1.65 against, so the results are slightly better than the chances they create and allow. Their home wins were tight: 2-1 over Brest and 1-0 over Nice.

Le Mans have one win in five (LLWDD) and have conceded 10 goals in that run. They give up more chances than Auxerre, an average of 1.95 expected goals against in their last five, and the 0-3 to Toulouse fits that picture. Even so, there is something to work with: they drew at Nice in September and drew 2-2 with Lens while creating 2.59 expected goals. Both squads have had a full week — nine days for Le Mans, seven for Auxerre — so freshness is not a factor. The two most recent Ligue 2 meetings, in 2019 and 2020, both went Auxerre's way.

Bourabaa out for weeks

Le Mans have the clearer problem. Coach Patrick Videira said Adil Bourabaa is likely to be absent for several weeks and that the team must move on without him [7]. That is a change for more than one match, and it lands while the defence is already giving up chances at almost two expected goals a game.

Auxerre's week has been about the win rather than about missing players. The 3-1 at Le Havre was their best attacking afternoon of the run, with 2.7 expected goals, and Still described it as a reference victory [1]. He takes that group into a home game for the first time in three matches.

A quarter of a goal, and a lean to goals

The win chances, with the bookmakers' margin removed, are Auxerre 45.4%, the draw 25.7% and Le Mans 28.9%. The home side are the most likely winner, but the away chance is close to three in ten, so this is not a one-sided match on paper. The handicap says the same: Auxerre are on -0.25, a quarter of a goal, with the fair line at -0.33, so the market rates them a little over a quarter of a goal the better side. In plain terms, the market expects Auxerre to come out ahead, but by a small margin, and a draw is a real outcome.

The goals line sits at 2.75, with the over at 51.8%, the market leaning slightly towards three goals or more rather than two or fewer. The fair line has moved from 2.72 to 2.82. That is consistent with the numbers: Le Mans allow 1.95 expected goals a game, and the two teams' last matches produced four and three goals. Both teams to score is at 59.0%.

The football and the price agree

Join the two pictures and the price makes sense. Auxerre are at home, four places and three points better off, and they arrive from their best win of the run. But their expected goals say those wins were narrow, so the market holds them back. Le Mans, for all their leaking defence, drew at Nice and out-created Lens at home. That is why the home chance stops at 45.4% and why the handicap is only a quarter of a goal.
