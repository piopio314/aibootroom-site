A response, or a fourth game without a win

Sevilla come home on Sunday evening with one point from their last three league games. The last of those was a 1-4 defeat at Real Madrid in which Kylian Mbappe scored twice [1], and the club has since lodged a formal complaint to LaLiga about refereeing decisions in that match [2]. Sixth place and 14 points still read well, but Osasuna in tenth are only three points behind.

Osasuna travel with a lighter mood. According to Marca, they had gone a month and a half without a win before beating Racing Santander 2-0 on Sunday, with Budimir and Osambela scoring [7], and the same outlet reports that the result has brought a smile back to the squad under coach Arrasate [8].

The situation is simple. A Sevilla win keeps them in the top six; a Sevilla defeat would leave the two clubs level on 14 points, with Sevilla's goal difference -2 and Osasuna's -8.

Sevilla's problem is at the back, Osasuna's is everywhere

Sevilla's run is easy to misread. Two 1-0 wins in September came before a 1-3 home defeat to Barcelona in which they created 0.4 xG and allowed 3.47, a 3-3 draw at Levante with 2.2 xG created, and the 1-4 at Madrid. Across those five matches they average 1.0 xG created and 1.56 allowed — the attack is modest, the defence is the issue. Ten goals conceded in five says the same thing more plainly.

Osasuna's numbers are worse in defence and better going forward. Twelve goals conceded in five, an average of 1.92 xG against, including 3.99 in the 0-4 at Atletico and 2.89 in the 2-5 at Betis. Their 1.53 xG created per match is better than Sevilla's, and the 2-0 over Racing was the strongest display of the run: 3.65 xG for, 0.28 against. Both sides had eight days between matches, so freshness is not a difference here.

Forwards fit, one midfielder in question

Sevilla's team news is mostly about who is available. ABC reports that coach Luis Garcia Plaza will have all three of his forwards available for this match if nothing changes [4], which matters for a side that has scored seven goals in its last five. The one question is in midfield, where Mundo Deportivo reports that Giorgi Kochorashvili has been missing from training this week [3]. That is a reported training absence, not a confirmed injury.

Osasuna have spent the week on the training ground: according to Pamplona Actual, four more sessions are planned before Sunday, open to the public from Thursday to Saturday [9]. Their defence is the part of Osasuna's game that has hurt them: 12 goals conceded in five and 1.92 xG against per match.

Half a goal better, and a line right on the edge

Sevilla are 48.5% to win at home, the draw 28.6% and Osasuna 22.9%, with the margin removed, from the ten bookmakers we compare. That makes Sevilla clear favourites without making them certain — the home win is closer to a coin flip than to a promise, but it is more than double Osasuna's chance. The Asian handicap sits at -0.5: Sevilla are rated just under half a goal the better side, so a Sevilla win by any score clears that line while a draw or an Osasuna win does not.

The goals line is 2.25, with Over at 49.6%, so the market sees a controlled game rather than an open one; both teams to score is 48.9%, on the same side of the coin. That sits oddly beside the recent scorelines, since the last five matches of each team averaged 3.4 goals. The week's moves were small: Under 2.25 gained 0.8 percentage points and the draw 0.6, while Osasuna and the Sevilla -0.5 handicap each lost 0.4.

Favourites, not certainties

Sevilla are the better team and the market says so: 48.5%, roughly half a goal of superiority, against an Osasuna side that has conceded 12 goals in five and gave up 3.99 xG at Atletico. But Sevilla's own defence has conceded 10 in five, and their recent meetings with Osasuna have all finished as a draw or a one-goal win, the last of them a 2-1 Osasuna victory in April. Sevilla are favourites, not certainties — which is exactly why the total sits at 2.25 goals rather than higher, and why the half-goal handicap is the right shape for this game. The defence under the most pressure on Sunday is Osasuna's.
