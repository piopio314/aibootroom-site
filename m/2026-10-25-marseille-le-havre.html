The bottom club comes to the Vélodrome

Le Havre arrive in Marseille on Sunday bottom of Ligue 1: two points from seven matches, no win, five defeats. Marseille are ninth with nine points and come off a 5-2 win at Angers, a result reported as a much-needed one that pulled them away from the relegation places [3].

The timing matters for both. Before Angers, Marseille had lost three of five in all competitions, two of them in the Europa League, and the win lifted them back into mid-table [4]. Le Havre are moving the other way. Seven matches without a win, and their coach Didier Digard is reported by news.maxifoot.fr to be worried about a week that brings Marseille and then PSG [8].

The days around the game have also been unsettled in Marseille. The club's store on the Canebière was looted and vandalised during protests, and the club condemned the acts "with the greatest firmness" [1]; Marseille and Lille closed their official stores [2]. Sunday's fixture was maintained "at this stage" after authorities banned protests in several French cities including Le Havre [11].

Marseille's open games, Le Havre's missing finish

The 5-2 at Angers came with 2.71 expected goals for Marseille and 2.21 against, so even a big win was an open afternoon. Their last five average 1.68 xG created and 2.06 allowed; the 1-2 home defeat by PSG alone saw 4.33 xG against them. They have had seven days since Angers to prepare for this one.

Le Havre's numbers sit strangely for a bottom side. They average 1.67 xG per game, essentially the same as Marseille's 1.68, and created 2.34 at Toulouse where they still lost 2-3. Last weekend it was 1.4 against 2.7 in a 1-3 home defeat by Auxerre [9], and at Lille they lost 0-1 with the chances almost level. Four league goals all season is the story of their year: the build-up works, the finishing does not.

A worried coach and a noisy week

Le Havre travel with morale as low as the table suggests. Digard is described as worried about facing Marseille and PSG [8], which fits a team with one point from their last five league games and a winless run stretching through seven matches.

Around Marseille the news has been off the pitch. Le Figaro reports an adjusted internal organisation and a project for leaving the Commanderie training centre [5], after the damage at the club's city-centre store [1]. That is background rather than team news, but it has been a loud week before a home match.

A one-goal advantage, and three goals in the air

The chances below come from the ten bookmakers, with their margin removed. Marseille are 60.6% to win — about three times Le Havre's 19.7%, with the draw rated level with the visitors. That is consistent with the table, with Marseille's five goals at Angers and with a Le Havre side that has not won a game.

The handicap is where the market is more careful. Marseille are rated at -1, a fair line of -0.98, so a home win by roughly one goal: a clear advantage rather than a rout. Three of Le Havre's last four defeats came by a single goal, which explains the caution. The goals line tells a different story. It sits at 3.0 and has moved up: over 3.0 is now 51.4% after 49.1% a few days ago, and the fair line went from 2.96 to 3.05. That is consistent with a weekend in which Marseille's game had seven goals and Le Havre's four. Both teams to score is 57.6%, more likely than not, and Marseille have kept one clean sheet in their last five.

What we wrote three days ago

Before Marseille's Europa League trip to Sturm Graz we wrote that they still had no points in Europe and were rated 47.5% to win there, against 26.6% for the Austrian side. We also noted the 5-2 at Angers, reported by supersport.com [3], as their second straight Ligue 1 win. That league form is why they arrive here as clear favourites; the European record is the reminder of how quickly their games can open up.

Same chances, different finishing

Le Havre's problem is not the build-up. They create at the same rate as Marseille, 1.67 expected goals against 1.68, and have four league goals to show for it. Marseille, ninth and off a five-goal afternoon, do finish what they make. That is the through-line: it is why the home chance is 60.6%, why both teams to score is 57.6% — Le Havre are more likely than not to create their share — and why the goals line sits at three and has moved up. The handicap of one goal says the same thing another way. Le Havre lose by small margins; the market expects another one, with more goals in it.
