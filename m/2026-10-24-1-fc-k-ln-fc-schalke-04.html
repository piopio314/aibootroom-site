Two points apart, two different problems

Köln are 12th on seven points, Schalke 15th on five, and only two points and three places separate them after six Bundesliga games. Schalke arrive in Cologne three games without a win [7], while Köln have lost three of their last five. Saturday afternoon at 13:30 is the kind of fixture that sets the mood of a season before the winter.

Köln's problem is at the top of the pitch. Thijs Dallinga, Ragnar Ache and Imad Rondic are all injured, according to tag24.de, which leaves the home side short of a senior centre-forward for a match in which they are nonetheless the favourites [6]. Malek El Mala could come into the Bundesliga squad for the first time [6].

Schalke's problem runs through the whole final third. They have scored four goals in their last five league games and managed only 0.43 expected goals at home to Mainz on Saturday. BILD reports that coach Miron Muslic has 'once again' been shipwrecked after that defeat [9], and the club icons Rüdiger Abramczik and Klaus Fischer have spoken of their alarm at what they are watching [12].

Köln create, Schalke don't

Köln's run reads badly — one win, one draw and three defeats in their last five — but the chances tell a calmer story. They average 1.61 xG per game, and in the 1-4 defeat at Stuttgart they created 2.86 xG; the scoreline was heavy, the performance was not. Even the 0-1 at Frankfurt last week finished with 0.8 xG to 1.8.

Schalke's numbers have the opposite shape. They average 0.90 xG per game and managed 0.32 at Freiburg and 0.43 against Mainz in their last two matches. They did win 3-1 at Union Berlin with 2.52 xG in September, so the threat is real, but it has not appeared since. Köln have had eight days to prepare for this one, Schalke seven.

No number nine, and a Schalke squad stretched

The Köln team news is all about the centre-forward position. sport.de reports that Dallinga and Ache are out through injury [2], though BILD reports new hope over Ache before the Schalke game [4]. BILD also says defensive midfielder Joel Schmied is missing [4], and reports a surprise guest at Köln's training who could be available [3]. Köln legend Pierre Littbarski, meanwhile, told BILD the club has 'too much mediocrity' [5].

Schalke's list is longer but less certain. express.de reports up to five absences around an ex-Köln player for the trip to Cologne [10]. sportschau.de says it is unclear whether Edin Džeko can play, and describes a headache at the club over their lack of goal threat [11]. With four league goals in five games, an unavailable senior striker would sharpen the problem rather than create it.

Just under half a goal better

The market rates Köln at 47.4% to win, the draw at 24.1% and Schalke at 28.5%. That makes a home win the single most likely result, but it is still less than a coin-flip — and the draw and the away side together carry more than half the probability.

The handicap line explains the size of the gap: the main line is Köln -0.5, with a fair line of -0.42, so the market rates the home side just under half a goal the better team. A Köln win by a single goal would settle it. The goals line sits at 3.0 with over at exactly 50%, and both teams to score is priced at 62%. That fits a Köln side that has conceded in each of their last five league games and gives up 1.8 xG per match — and it fits a home favourite whose creation gap over Schalke, 1.61 xG to 0.90, is the clearest split between the two teams.

The read

Köln are favourites because they make chances and Schalke do not: that is the whole case, and it holds even with Dallinga and Rondic out and Ache doubtful, though BILD reports new hope over him [4]. It is a thin kind of favouritism, though. Less than a coin-flip of a home win, a handicap of only half a goal and a three-goal line that splits exactly in the middle all point the same way — a close game with goals in it, decided by whichever side finds a finisher on the day. Köln have the better case, and they have the weaker attack to go with it.
