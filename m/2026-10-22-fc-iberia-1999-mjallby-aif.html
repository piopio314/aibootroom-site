A team that only wins in Europe

Europe is the one place Mjällby's season is working. A week ago they won their first ever match in a European main tournament, 2-0 against Inter Club d'Escaldes, and they sit 5th of 36 in the Conference League table [5]. On Thursday they play Iberia in Tbilisi, a home side who lost their opener and sit 34th of 36 [1].

The contrast is the story. In Sweden, Mjällby have gone eight Allsvenskan matches without a win [2], and the mood around the trip is low. Tom Pettersson said in a direct quote that right now he is not "in the mood" to travel to Georgia given the team's situation [4].

Iberia come from the other direction. They won 2-0 away at Dila on Sunday in their domestic league, three days before this game, so the home side arrives with a win behind it while the visitors arrive with questions.

The chances are better than the results

Mjällby's last five matches read LWDLL, but the chance data tells a kinder story: 1.42 expected goals for and 1.02 against, more created than allowed. The 1-1 at home to Degerfors is the clearest example — 2.31 xG for and 0.75 against, and only one point.

The latest game was a 0-1 defeat at Västerås, where a goal for Mjällby was disallowed [3]. Iberia's last five read WLWLW, and their one European outing was a 0-2 loss at St. Truiden in which they created 0.15 xG. That single number explains a lot: at this level Iberia have produced almost no attacking threat. Iberia had three days of rest before this match, Mjällby four.

Mood, not missing names

The news around Mjällby this week has been about tone rather than absentees. Pettersson's comment to SVT Sport is the one that travelled beyond Sweden [4], and it fits a squad that has lost three of its last five games in all competitions and has not won in the league since well before this trip.

The other half of the picture is the coach's view. Andreas Brännström said after the 2-0 win over Inter Club d'Escaldes that it was very important to win [6], and Jacob Bergström and Axel Norén scored that night [5]. The same group that cannot win at home in Sweden has won its European match, and that is why the prices keep them ahead.

A favourite by a fraction of a goal

On the average of the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Mjällby win 39.7% of the time, Iberia 31.3%, and the draw takes the rest at 29.0%. The gap between the sides is small, and the handicap says the same: the main line gives Iberia +0.25 goals, so a draw counts as half a win for them, and the fair line the prices point to is only 0.16 goals. Mjällby's edge is a fraction of a goal, not a clear one.

The goals line is 2.5 and it is level, over and under both at 50.0%, with both teams to score at 55.1% — an ordinary goal game is expected. The move in the last day went against the favourite: Mjällby's chance drifted from 41.8% to 39.7% while Iberia gained 2.0 points and the home +0.25 handicap gained 2.5. That is consistent with eight Allsvenskan games without a win [2] and a player saying publicly that he does not want to make the trip [4].

What the price is really saying

Mjällby are the more likely winners because they create more than they allow, and because Iberia made almost nothing in their only European match. But the price is not a verdict on the Swedish season — it is a verdict on a close game, one in which a quarter of a goal separates the teams and the goal line sits level. The side with the better European record and the worse recent mood has to prove it on a long trip to Tbilisi, and the market has that as a narrow lead, not a comfortable one.
