A sacking two days before kick-off

Braga play a European night with a new man in the dugout. Carlos Vicens was sacked this week [1] and Franclim Carvalho was named in his place [2], two days before Gent arrive in the Conference League. The club president, Salvador, called it 'a cut, not a transition' [3] — the language of people who believe something has broken.

Carvalho has had almost no time on the training ground. He said he had always wanted to sit in the Braga dugout [5], and he will need that appetite: the team he inherits has won once in five and lost its Conference League opener 0-3 at Copenhagen. Gent, the visitors, won theirs.

The situation is simple and awkward. Braga sit 35th of 36 in the league phase after one game, with a goal difference of -3; Gent are 13th with three points. A second straight defeat would leave the Portuguese side chasing the rest of the table from a long way back.

One win in five against an unbeaten run

Braga's last five read LLLDW. They scored three goals in that run and let in eight, and the chance data is mixed: they created 1.15 expected goals a game and allowed 1.17, though their xG at home to Gil Vicente was 1.39 to 1.54. At home to Sporting CP they made more chances (1.5 expected goals to 0.95) and still lost 1-3. According to Goal.com, the eight goals conceded across those five matches is the pattern the new coach inherits [6].

Gent's run is DWWWD, with nine scored and five conceded, and a healthier profile underneath: 1.48 expected goals for and 0.76 against per game. Their 1-0 win over Aarhus came with 2.24 expected goals to 0.23 — the scoreline was far closer than the match. They also had four days' rest to Braga's three, after a 3-3 cup tie at Rochefort, with the Belgian Cup exit to Union Rochefortoise confirmed earlier in the week, according to Voetbalkrant [8].

A new voice on the bench

The significant team news at Braga is the bench. Carvalho arrives with no time to install new ideas, so the shape and the names are likely to look familiar; the first thing that usually changes is the mood and the level of risk. Vicens, according to A Bola, paid for the home defeat to Gil Vicente [4].

Gent travel close to full strength. According to Voetbalkrant, Rik De Mil has an almost fully available squad for the trip, with a few names missing and one defender back in the selection [7]. De Mil said the match would be a challenge and called Braga a great team [10], and his side do not expect an easy night despite the change in the home dugout [12].

Favourites by about half a goal

Take the bookmakers' margin out of the prices from the ten books we compare and you get Braga 51.3%, the draw 26.1%, Gent 22.6%. Braga are favourites, more than twice as likely as Gent on those numbers, though that is not a wide gap for a home side in Europe. The handicap is 0.5 goals in Braga's favour, which in plain terms means Braga must win the match for that side of the line to land, while a draw is enough for Gent.

The week has moved against the home side. Braga's fair win chance fell 5.2 points, Gent's rose 5.1 points, and the 0.5 handicap gave back 5.5 points. The fair line went from 0.78 goals to 0.56: Braga were rated close to three-quarters of a goal better, now roughly half. That is consistent with a team that has lost three of five hosting one that has not lost at all, and with a coaching change nobody could plan for.

The goals market sits at 2.25, with Over at 50.6% against a fair total of 2.28 — no strong lean either way. Both teams to score is priced at 47.7%, a shade against, which fits Gent's 0.23 expected goals against in their opener as much as it fights Braga's eight conceded in five.

The Gil Vicente night we called wrongly

Four days ago we previewed Braga at home to Gil Vicente. We called them clear favourites at around 63%, and Gil Vicente won 1-2. Braga have now lost three of five and changed coach; this time the price has moved, and it has moved toward the visitors.

Our read

The football points to Gent as the better team this week: unbeaten in five, stronger at both ends in chance terms (1.48 expected goals for, 0.76 against), and already with three Conference League points. The market still makes Braga favourites at 51.3%, more than twice Gent's 22.6%, with their home ground, squad and Gent's travel tilting the night their way, but less than a few days ago, with the home 1X2 down 5.2 points. Add a new head coach, Franclim Carvalho, per Record [2], after a home defeat to Gil Vicente reported by A Bola [4], and a defence that has conceded eight goals in five matches, per Goal.com [6], and the picture points to a favourite whose main advantage is the fixture rather than the form. That is why the 0.5 handicap and 2.25 goals line suggest a comfortable night in Braga is not expected.
