Seven from seven, against a five-game run

Manchester City have won all seven of their Premier League games and lead the table with 21 points, three clear of Arsenal [8]. On Saturday lunchtime they go to Villa Park, where the team they meet has not lost in five matches in any competition.

Aston Villa's run includes a 6-0 Champions League win over Fenerbahçe and a 1-0 win at Newcastle last weekend [4]. Even so, Unai Emery's side sit 12th with eight points from seven league games and a goal difference of minus four. That gap between the last month and the season as a whole is what makes this fixture awkward for both teams.

The recent meetings point the other way. Villa have won three of the last four against City, including a 2-1 win at the Etihad in May. The week also brought different news. The BBC reported that City could face a major points deduction, with the FRC looking into the club's accounting [9], and The Guardian reported David Kogan, chair of the Independent Football Regulator, saying the watchdog has powers to force City's owners to sell if a verdict is upheld [10].

What the chances say about both runs

Villa have scored 15 and conceded five across their last five, with an average of 2.05 expected goals for and 1.36 against. The 6-0 over Fenerbahçe came with 3.15 xG, so the scoreline and the chances agreed. The 3-2 win at Tottenham did not: Villa created 1.16 xG and allowed 1.56 and won anyway, while the 2-2 with Brentford was the reverse — 2.28 xG against 1.06, two points the chances said should have been three.

City's last five have produced 18 goals for and 10 against, with 2.62 xG for and 1.85 against. They create more than Villa have faced recently, but they give chances away too: Sunderland scored three in City's 5-3 win at the Etihad on 20 September, when City conceded 3.57 xG. Freshness is the hosts' clearest edge on the pitch. Villa have had seven days since Newcastle; City played on Tuesday, their third match in seven days and only four days after Ipswich.

Two out for Villa, Haaland ready

Villa lose Aaron Wan-Bissaka for at least three weeks with the hamstring injury he picked up at Newcastle, according to The Athletic [1]. Johan Manzambi, who scored the only goal in that 1-0 win [4], is also out, though Ian Maatsen has returned to training [2]. Losing a right-back matters more than usual against City's wide forwards, and losing the match-winner takes away Emery's most direct answer on the counter. Emery's own response was that the injuries are 'no excuse' [3].

City's news is better. Erling Haaland was an unused substitute in Tuesday's 5-1 Champions League win over AEK Athens, and Enzo Maresca said of him: 'He's okay to start on Saturday' [6][11]. Phil Foden scored a hat-trick in that game [7], so City scored five without their main scorer and can bring him back rested. Antoine Semenyo and Nico O'Reilly are reported as injury questions for Saturday [11].

The read: favoured, but not comfortable

City arrive with 21 points from seven games, a striker who did not play in midweek, and five goals scored without him. That is why they are favourites away from home. But Villa have won three of the last four meetings, and the hosts are the fresher team, with a week's rest and a 6-0 win behind them. The market makes City favourites, but not by much: a three-quarter-goal start for Villa and a goals line at 3.0. More likely than not City win, and more likely than not both teams score — the leader is favoured, the ground keeps it close.
