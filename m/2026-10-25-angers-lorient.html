Two clubs going opposite ways

Angers host Lorient on Sunday with the two clubs in very different moods. Angers have conceded five goals in each of their last two matches — 2-5 at home to Marseille [2] and 1-5 at Brest. Lorient beat the league leaders Monaco 1-0 on the same weekend [7], their third win in five league games.

That contrast has shaped this fixture all week. Lorient arrive as the favourites, and the gap between the two sides has grown since the Marseille result. For Angers, the game is a chance to stop a run that is pulling them towards the bottom of the table — they sit 15th on seven points, four places and four points behind their visitors.

There is a personal edge too. Alexandre Dujeux, now Lorient's coach, is Angers' former coach and faces his old club, according to Le Télégramme [8]. The away bench knows this squad and this ground very well.

The chances matched the scorelines

Angers' heavy defeats were not bad luck. They allowed 2.71 expected goals against Marseille and 3.82 at Brest, so the quality of the chances they gave up matched the goals they conceded. Across their last five matches Angers average 2.08 xG against, the clear weak point of their season so far.

Going forward they are not empty. Angers average 1.62 xG created, and they made 2.25 in a 2-0 home win over Troyes and 1.66 in a narrow 1-2 loss to Rennes. Lorient's numbers are quieter: 1.16 xG for and 1.07 against across their last five, and their 1-0 win over Paris FC came with only 0.55 xG created. They have won lately by defending well — two clean sheets in a row, four goals conceded in five.

Both teams had seven days between matches. Lorient took two days off after Monaco and have four sessions to prepare for this trip, according to Le Télégramme [11].

Angers short in midfield again

Haris Belkebla, who missed the Marseille defeat through injury, was still absent from Angers training four days before kick-off, Ouest-France report [3]. He is normally a central midfielder, and he was already missing when Angers conceded five at home — so this is a continuing problem rather than a fresh one for the price.

There is better news in the same area. Ousmane Camara, absent against Marseille, has returned to training with the squad, according to Ouest-France [4]. The same outlet reports that Emmanuel Biumla is training away from the first-team group and is doubtful for Sunday [5]. Lorient's week has been calm: rest, then four sessions, and no absences reported.

A level fixture that tilted away

Take out the bookmakers' margin — the prices come from the ten bookmakers we compare — and Lorient are 39.3% to win at Angers, with the home side on 31.6% and the draw on 29.1%. That makes Lorient narrow favourites, not clear ones: the two win chances are separated by less than eight points.

The handicap says the same thing in another language. Angers start with a quarter-goal head start (+0.25), so a draw counts as half a win for Angers and half a loss for Lorient. The fair line has moved from -0.05 to +0.15 — a fifth of a goal — which means a fixture first rated level is now a shade in Lorient's favour.

The week's biggest moves all point the same way. Lorient's price fell from 2.81 to 2.46 and Angers drifted from 2.61 to 3.06; the away win gained 5.4 percentage points and the home win lost 4.9. The change landed in the hours after Angers' home defeat to Marseille, and every major book moved at the same time — consistent with a broad re-pricing rather than one stale number.

The goals line sits at 2.5, with Over priced at 47.2% — a slight lean towards under three goals, and the fair line is 2.4. That fits Lorient's two clean sheets, and sits oddly beside the 13 goals scored in Angers' last two matches.

Form over home history, but only just

The market has turned this into a Lorient match, and the football supports it: the form side, the higher table position, and a defence that has conceded four goals in five. But the price keeps it honest. At 39.3% Lorient are more likely than not to win only in the loosest sense — Angers still hold close to a third of the chance, and they still create at 1.62 xG a game.

The home record in this fixture cuts the other way. The last three meetings all went to the home side, including Angers' 2-0 win at this ground last October. Form says Lorient; the history of this ground says be careful. Our read is Lorient, but only narrowly.
