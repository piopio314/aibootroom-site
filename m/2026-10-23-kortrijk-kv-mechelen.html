A bottom-five Friday night

Eight games, three points, one win. Kortrijk open the ninth matchday in 17th place of 18 with a goal difference of minus 16, and they do it at home to KV Mechelen. Both clubs sit in the bottom five, and VoetbalNieuws describes Friday's game as one with a lot riding on it for both teams [8].

Kortrijk's week has been about change rather than points. The newly appointed Daniel Beichler has announced a 'new' Kortrijk for Friday, reported by VoetbalNieuws [2], and according to voetbalfocus.be he is counting on a more collective and more aggressive team after a difficult debut at Genk [1]. The sporting staff is still moving as well: Voetbalkrant reports that Markus Pflanz is leaving and Kurt Bataille is taking another role, barely a week after a new head coach and assistant arrived [4].

Mechelen travel with a different feeling. Their first league win of the season was 4-3 at home to St. Truiden, and on Sunday they followed it with a 5-1 cup win over Eupen [5]. Even so, Voetbalkrant reports that they are still fighting near the bottom [6] — they are 14th on six points — so neither dressing room can treat this as a normal Friday.

One good afternoon, two wild wins

Kortrijk's single win came on 20 September, 1-0 at home to SK Beveren, and it was built on 2.25 expected goals to 0.42. That is the only match this season in which they clearly out-created the opponent. Around it sit defeats: 0-3 at Genk on 10 October with 0.65 xG against 1.78, 0-3 at RAAL La Louvière while allowing 3.58 xG, and 0-2 at home to Zulte Waregem. Across their last five matches they average 1.06 xG created and 1.74 allowed.

Mechelen's run needs a second look. The 4-3 over St. Truiden came with just 0.74 xG created and 1.45 allowed, a wild game they won without controlling it. Their most solid display was arguably the 0-0 at Lommel, 1.31 xG to 0.48, and they lost 0-1 at home to Anderlecht despite a 1.23 to 0.91 count. They average 0.96 created and 1.31 allowed over their last five, and the calendar also splits the two sides: Kortrijk have not played since 10 October, 13 days, while Mechelen turn around in five.

The unknown is on the home bench

Beichler's promise of a 'new' Kortrijk, reported by VoetbalNieuws, is the biggest unknown of the night [2]. Two weeks on the training ground are enough to change how a team presses and how it stands, and he has spoken of a more collective, more aggressive side, according to voetbalfocus.be [1]. The question for Friday is how far that goes in one game. The target is clear enough: Kortrijk's one strong performance this season came from creating 2.25 xG, and they have matched that number once in eight games.

Mechelen's story is a player more than a system. Nineteen-year-old midfielder Marco Decherf has convinced his coach and his teammates and has become the engine of the midfield, according to Voetbalprimeur and HBVL [7]. In a match the market treats as even, a midfielder who is playing well is often what separates a draw from a win.

Level, and why it is level

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Kortrijk are 37.4% to win, the draw 27.2% and Mechelen 35.4%. That is close to a coin-flip between the two clubs, and Kortrijk's home ground is the only thing in their favour: on results they are three points and three places worse off than the visitors. The handicap is set at level (+0), which in plain words means neither team gives a goal and the market regards them as equals on the pitch. The goals line sits at 2.75, with over priced at 47.5%, and both teams to score at 57.7%.

The movement this week is small but it points one way: Kortrijk's win chance eased by about 1.4 points and Mechelen's rose by the same amount, with the fair handicap line drifting from -0.1 to -0.04. That is the market taking a little of the home advantage away — a side coming off a difficult debut at Genk, according to voetbalfocus.be [1], against one that has just won twice and scored nine goals doing it, per VoetbalNieuws [5].

Goals in a game nobody can call

Put the two pictures together and the level line makes sense. Kortrijk have the fixture, the rest and a new coach's first home game; Mechelen have the results and the stronger defensive numbers over their last five, 1.31 xG allowed against 1.74, even if Kortrijk have created slightly more, 1.06 against 0.96. What the market will not do is make either side a clear favourite, because Mechelen's wins were high-scoring and not fully controlled, and Kortrijk's season contains exactly one good afternoon. Both teams to score at 57.7% fits that argument best: a tight result, with goals in it.
