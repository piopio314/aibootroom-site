Zwolle go to Utrecht without a manager — and with a derby win

A derby win, then a sacking

PEC Zwolle played their best afternoon of the season on Sunday, beating Go Ahead Eagles 4-1 in the IJsselderby [8]. Two days later they sacked head coach Henry van der Vegt [7]. On Saturday they walk into Galgenwaard with no permanent manager and no successor in place.

Utrecht come from the other direction. Their 2-1 win at Twente was the first home defeat Twente have suffered this season [1], and it made it three wins in four league games for Utrecht. Coach Anthony Correia called the side his 'Ultimate Team' and said it is growing [2]. Goalkeeper Padt went further: 'something is really growing', he said, after watching team-mates make sliding tackles and block shots [3].

The table adds the context. Utrecht sit 11th with 11 points from nine games, Zwolle 15th with seven. Both clubs won last weekend, but only one of them knows who will be picking the team next month.

Utrecht's results are ahead of their numbers

Utrecht's last five league games brought ten goals scored and eleven conceded, with expected-goals averages of 1.4 for and 1.56 against — a side that trades chances rather than controls matches. They won 1-2 away at Twente, www.telegraaf.nl reported [1], one result in a five-game form line of three wins, one draw and one loss. The defeat in that sequence is the other face of the same team: the form shows both the wins and the vulnerability.

Zwolle's run reads worse. They have conceded 14 goals in their last five league games, an expected-goals-against average of 2.12, and they have lost six of their nine league matches this season. Even the derby win needs a second look — Zwolle created 1.81 and allowed 2.76, so Go Ahead Eagles had the better chances and lost 4-1. The leak at the back is real, and it is not bad luck.

Zwolle's search goes on

Zwolle are looking for a head coach while preparing a match. René Hake was their top candidate but withdrew last week, according to drimble.nl [11]. On ESPN's Voetbalpraat, Bram van Polen said Maurice Steijn's name is being discussed at the club, reported by voetbalprimeur.nl [10]. Nothing is settled, so the group that beat Go Ahead Eagles is likely to be guided by the same voices on Saturday.

Utrecht had the calmer week. Amrabat became the oldest outfield player to wear the Utrecht shirt in the win at Twente [4] — a squad using everything it has. That win followed a home victory over Willem II, after a difficult start to the season [5].

Clear favourites, narrow line

The win chances here come from the ten bookmakers we compare, with their margin removed. Utrecht are 56.3% to win, the draw is 22.5% and Zwolle are 21.2% — Utrecht's chance is more than twice Zwolle's. That is a clear home favourite, but with the draw taking almost a quarter of the probability it is not treated as a formality.

The main handicap line is -0.75, and the fair line has moved from -0.91 to -0.80, so the market still rates Utrecht about 0.8 goals the better side. On -0.75, a one-goal Utrecht win counts as only half a win: the market is calling a narrow home victory, not a rout. The total-goals line is 3.25, with Over at 47.3% — just on the Under side — while both teams to score is priced at 62.8%. Over 3.25 has lost about two points in recent days and the fair goals line has come down from 3.28 to 3.14, consistent with a controlled, one-goal game.

Our read

The managerless club arrives with the better weekend behind it; the club with the coach arrives with the better season behind it. Utrecht's wins over Willem II and Twente are real, but their expected goals say they win by taking chances rather than by dominating — Utrecht's own 0-5 at Feyenoord on 20 September is the warning. Zwolle's 4-1 came with 2.12 expected goals against, so the scoreline flattered them. That is why the market makes Utrecht clear favourites, rates them about 0.8 goals better and still expects a narrow win, with the goals line leaning just under three and a quarter. More likely than not a home win — not a formality.
