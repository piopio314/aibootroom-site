The form team comes to Anfield

Brighton have won their last five matches, scoring 17 goals and conceding 2. They are third in the Premier League with 16 points from seven games and a goal difference of +18. Liverpool are fifth on 12 points from the same seven matches, and they have already lost 0-3 at home to Manchester City this month.

That is the tension of Sunday's game. Anfield usually means a clear home edge, but the bookmakers we compare make Liverpool 45.3% to win, Brighton 30.4% and the draw 24.4%. The home side are favourites; the gap between the teams is rated as small.

Liverpool's week adds to the question. They came from 2-0 down to beat Villarreal 4-3 at Anfield on Tuesday [1], a result that shows fight and also a third goal conceded at home in Europe. The last meeting between these clubs, in March, finished 2-1 to Brighton.

What the xG says about both runs

Liverpool's last five read four wins and one defeat, with 13 goals scored and 10 conceded. The underlying numbers point the same way: they create a good amount at 1.98 xG per game but allow 1.33, and the difficult nights sit right on that line. Against City they managed 0.94 xG and lost 0-3; at Lask they won 6-3 while allowing 2.08 xG. Even the Villarreal comeback came with 1.62 xG against.

Brighton's run is built on better numbers. They average 2.19 xG created and 0.79 allowed across the five wins, and the 5-0 derby win over Crystal Palace came with 2.0 xG for and 0.77 against [6]. The one note of caution is the 3-0 win over Arsenal, when they allowed 1.63 xG. Brighton have also had seven days since their last match; Liverpool have had five, after a midweek European game.

Mac Allister scoring, Gómez from 51 metres

Liverpool's midfield is finding the net. Alexis Mac Allister scored twice against Villarreal [1], and 18-year-old Rio Ngumoha set up a goal to become the youngest Liverpool player to register a Champions League assist [2]. Virgil van Dijk called Ngumoha's display a complete threat and said the teenager has learned from Mohamed Salah [4].

The Liverpool coach was direct after Tuesday's match: he said Brighton are flying right now and that his own side must stop conceding [3]. For Brighton, Diego Gómez scored from 51 metres against Palace, the longest-distance Premier League goal since 2017 [6], and The Athletic reports they lead the league in number of different goalscorers [8]. Yahoo Sports reported on Wednesday that Brighton are set for a major injury boost before the trip, without naming the player [10].

A quarter of a goal, and three goals expected

With the bookmakers' margin removed, the ten bookmakers we compare give Liverpool 45.3% and Brighton 30.4%. That makes Liverpool favourites, but it also makes Brighton's chance a real one rather than a token one. The reason is in the football: five straight wins and a +18 goal difference against a home side that has conceded 10 goals in five matches.

The handicap shows the size of that edge in plain terms. Liverpool are set at -0.25, a quarter of a goal, and the fair line is -0.32 — about a third of a goal. For a home team at Anfield against a side sitting third, that is a thin advantage.

The goals line is 3.0, with over and under split evenly at 50.0% each, and both teams to score is priced at 62.5%. That fits the record of both clubs: Brighton have scored in every win, and Liverpool have conceded in four of their last five.

The favourite is not the better team on form

Brighton are the better side across the last five games — more goals, fewer conceded, stronger xG at both ends — and they arrive with two extra days of rest. Liverpool have Anfield, a deeper squad and a midfield scoring goals, and that is what keeps them ahead in the price.

The market joins the two pictures without choosing one: Liverpool at 45.3% to win, a handicap of about a third of a goal, and a goals line of 3.0 with both teams to score at 62.5%. That is the shape of an open game between teams rated close to level, where the home ground is the difference rather than the quality of the run. More likely than not, Liverpool avoid defeat. Three points are nowhere near certain.
