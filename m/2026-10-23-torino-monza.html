A home side in form, a visitor with a sharper edge

Torino go into Friday night's game at a good moment. They won 3-0 at Parma on Sunday, their second Serie A win in a row, with Giovanni Simeone scoring twice and Che Adams heading in [1]. According to Tuttosport, advance ticket sales have climbed since then, and a near-full, lively stadium is expected [2].

Monza arrive with a story of their own. They beat Cagliari 4-0 at home, ending Cagliari's four-game unbeaten run [7]. Ivan Juric's team sit 14th, four places and three points behind Torino, but they have the more dangerous attack on recent evidence.

There is also a recent result between these two that matters. Monza won 1-0 at this same stadium in the Coppa Italia on 1 September, and Juric said after the Cagliari win that his team will approach Friday as they did that night [6]. Torino are favourites. They are not comfortable favourites.

Torino win, Monza create

Torino's last five read three wins, one draw and one loss. The results are good, but the chance numbers are level: they average 1.36 expected goals for and 1.36 against. The 3-0 at Parma came with a near-even xG count of 1.58 to 1.61, and the 2-1 win over Udinese was their one truly dominant display at 2.62 to 0.76. In other words, they are winning some tight games.

Monza's last five read two wins, one draw and two losses, yet they average 1.98 xG per game — more than a goal better than Torino's 1.36. The 4-0 over Cagliari came with 2.1 xG each way, and they created 2.79 in a 2-3 defeat at Lecce. Both teams had four days of rest, so freshness is not a factor. The table says Torino. The chance quality says Monza.

Monza thin at the back, Torino missing a defender

Monza's list runs to five. Yannick Maye, a defender with 131 minutes this season, is out with a muscle injury. Erik Zeballos, who has one goal in 71 minutes, has a knee problem, and Toro.it reports that Matteo Pessina is also out with a knee injury [9]. Toro.it reports that Juric still has doubts over Ciurria and Touré for Friday [9]. None of these are full-season starters, which helps explain why Monza remain underdogs.

For Torino, the main doubt is defender Niccolo Fortini, who has played 357 minutes across six games and was absent from the squad at Parma according to Toro News [5]. Eljif Ilkhan is out with a muscle injury after 99 minutes. Coach Ignazio Abate said this week that the team is fighting with itself and that Nikola Vlasic must stay calm [3]. According to Gazzetta dello Sport, Bragança has settled quickly under Abate, scoring against Udinese and starring at Parma [4] — a reason the home midfield looks stronger than its injury list suggests.

Favourites by less than half a goal

That makes Torino clear favourites, but not by much: Monza's chance is closer to Torino's than a 14th-placed visitor's would normally be. What we said at Parma

On Sunday we previewed Parma v Torino and had Torino as the quietly favoured away side, though nothing was guaranteed, and they won 3-0. But we also called a tight, low-scoring game and leaned toward the under, and three goals cleared that. The lesson carries into Friday: a flat price does not mean a flat performance, and a favoured side can still land a big result. It also means we should be careful about calling this one low-scoring when Monza's numbers point the other way, with Gustavo Varela having scored six goals in six Serie A appearances this season, including a double against Cagliari [8].

Our read

Torino deserve to be favourites: two straight wins, a loud home crowd and a visitor with five absentees. But the football and the price only partly agree. Monza create far more per game, have the in-form scorer on the pitch and have already won at this stadium this season. That is why the handicap is only a quarter of a goal and Monza's chance sits near 28% despite their table position. Our view is a close game with goals in it: Torino more likely than not to edge it, but Monza very much in the match.
