Two teams arriving on their best weekend

Torino host Monza on Friday night, and both clubs come in from their strongest result of the autumn. Torino won 3-0 at Parma on Sunday, their second win in a row, with Giovanni Simeone scoring twice and Che Adams heading in [1]. Monza beat Cagliari 4-0 at home the same afternoon, their first clean sheet of the season [6].

The recent history makes Turin a careful place for the home side. Monza won 1-0 at this stadium in the Coppa Italia on 1 September, the last meeting of any kind. Ivan Juric said after Sunday's win that Torino should expect Monza to play "as in the Coppa" [8].

The table offers a gap that the last month does not: Torino are 10th on 10 points, Monza 14th on 7. Add Gustavo Varela, who has six goals in six Serie A games this season and sits joint-second in the scoring charts [7], and Friday looks like a close game between two teams in good mood rather than a home procession.

Torino's results are better than their chances

Torino's run reads well — three wins, a draw and a loss in five, eight goals scored and five conceded — but the underlying numbers are flat. They average 1.36 expected goals for and 1.36 against, and the 3-0 at Parma came with almost even chance quality, 1.58 to 1.61. Expected goals (xG) measures how good the chances were, and by that measure Sunday was a scoreline built on finishing, not domination.

Monza's numbers point the other way. They average 1.98 xG over their last five, against Torino's 1.36, and have passed 1.5 in each of them. Their clearest evidence is the 3-2 defeat at Lecce in September: 2.79 xG to 1.08 and no points. Both squads had four days between matches, so neither side carries a rest advantage into Friday.

Fortini's thigh is the one real question

Torino's defence is the uncertain part. N. Fortini, a regular with 357 league minutes in six appearances, has a thigh problem and was again left out of the squad at Parma [2]. Ardian Ismajli came in for that game with Ricardo Rodríguez dropping to the bench [3], so the replacement is known; the question is whether Torino need a second reshuffle. E. Ilkhan, with 99 minutes, is also out.

Monza's list is longer but shallower. Defender Y. Maye, the most used of the absentees with 131 league minutes, is out with a muscle injury, as are E. Zeballos (one goal in 71 minutes), J. Ziolkowski and I. Toure. The eleven that started against Cagliari is largely untouched, which makes this a squad problem rather than a first-team one.

A favourite, but only by a quarter of a goal

The bookmakers we compare, ten of them with the margin removed, make Torino 44.2% to win, the draw 28.1% and Monza 27.7%. Torino are the favourites and they are at home, but 44.2% is well short of a coin-flip, and Monza's share is close enough that a home win is the most likely single result, not the expected one.

The fair handicap line is -0.31, which in plain terms means Torino are rated about a third of a goal better than Monza — a quarter-ball start for the visitors. The goal line sits at 2.47, so over 2.5 is almost a coin-flip at 49.1%, and both teams to score is priced at 53.3%. That fits two teams whose recent games have been open: Monza's last five have finished 4-0, 3-1, 2-1, 3-2 and 1-1.

Off Monza's 4-0 over Cagliari (sports.yahoo.com [6]), their recent scoring and conceding make both teams finding the net a plausible outcome.

Our read: close, open, home side just ahead

Friday brings together a narrow home favourite and an away side creating more clear chances. Torino have the better table position, the home ground and two straight wins; Monza have the stronger xG average, a striker with six goals in six games and a 1-0 win at this stadium seven weeks ago. The market puts all of that into one price and lands on 44.2% for Torino, with about a third of a goal between the teams. Our read is a close game with chances at both ends, where Torino's 44.2% is the single most likely result but under half the match, so a draw or an away result would surprise nobody.
