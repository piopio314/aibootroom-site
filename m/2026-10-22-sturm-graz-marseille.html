Nothing to show for two nights

Marseille have played two Europa League matches and have no points: a 1-4 defeat at Beşiktaş and a 0-1 home loss to Olympiakos, which leaves them 32nd of 36 [5]. Five league-phase games follow this one, so a third defeat in Graz would not end the campaign — but it would leave very little room in it.

Sturm Graz are 26th with one point, from a 0-0 at home to Rennes, and they arrive on a high: a 6-1 win over city rivals Grazer AK on Sunday, with a hat-trick from Oumar Diakité, reported by footmercato [1]. The home side have won two of their last five and lost two, and the two defeats were the European ones.

The backdrop is tense. Austrian police have increased security around the match after violent clashes at the Graz derby, with around a thousand Marseille supporters expected, according to RMC Sport [4].

Two scorelines, two different levels

Sturm Graz's 6-1 came with 3.58 expected goals and 0.64 against, a dominant domestic afternoon. Their European games tell another story: a 0-0 with Rennes and a 0-3 at Bournemouth, where they managed 0.37 expected goals [2]. The gap between those two levels is the question of the night.

Marseille's form runs the other way. They have scored nine goals in their last five matches and won the last two in Ligue 1, 2-0 at Troyes and 5-2 at Angers, where they created 2.71 expected goals [6]. In Europe it is one goal in two games, and their last five matches average 2.06 xG against, so they give up chances. Both teams had four days between matches.

A stretched home squad, a doubt in Marseille's midfield

Sturm Graz list seven absentees who have played this season. The heaviest are defender E. Soglo with 638 minutes and a 7.16 rating, and forward S. Jatta with 641 minutes and two goals. Mamageishvili, with 320 minutes, and Weiper, with two goals in 243 minutes, are also out, so the attacking options are thinner than a 6-1 suggests.

Marseille's question is in midfield. Pierre-Emile Hojbjerg is a doubt with a muscle strain, and Bruno Genesio said he is an option for Thursday but that it is unclear whether he can start, according to Rotowire [7]. Hojbjerg has 540 minutes this season at a 7.17 rating, so losing him or using him for part of the game would matter against a side that has just scored six.

Favourites, but only by about a goal

Marseille are 47.5% to win, nearly twice Sturm Graz's 26.6%, with the draw at 25.9%. That makes Marseille clear favourites, but still short of a majority. The handicap shows how big the gap is: Sturm Graz get a +0.5 start and the fair line sits at +0.42, so the market rates Marseille roughly half a goal the better team — tighter than the percentage gap suggests. That points to a game more likely settled by one goal than by a wide margin.

Goals are the other half of the picture. The line is 2.75, with over at 51.0% and the market's fair total at 2.79, and both teams to score is 58.1%. That fits two defences that concede: Marseille in four of their last five matches and Sturm Graz in four of five. Marseille's win chance sits at 47.5% — their two Ligue 1 wins and nine goals in five matches are already in that number, while their blank European record is why it stays below half.

The read: league form against the European record

The through-line is simple: the market rates Marseille on their league form, not their European one. Two Ligue 1 wins and nine goals in five matches are why they are 47.5% favourites; zero points and one goal in two Europa League games are why that number stays below half. Sturm Graz bring the derby high and the 6-1, but their two European matches produced no goals and only 0.37 expected goals at Bournemouth. A one-goal Marseille win fits the handicap and the goal line, and the home side's case rests on the derby level being closer to their real one than Bournemouth was.
