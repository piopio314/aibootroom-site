Braga take a new coach and a smaller gap to Nacional

A reset with no time to settle

Braga travel to Nacional on Sunday with a new man in the dugout. The club sacked Carlos Vicens with immediate effect [1] and appointed Franclim Carvalho in his place [2], both confirmed a day ago, and this away match is Carvalho's first in charge.

The table explains why the market still leans heavily towards the visitors. Braga are 9th with 11 points, Nacional 16th of 18 with 4. The form explains why the leaning is softer than it was a few days ago: Nacional have lost five in a row, but Braga have lost three in a row, the last of them 1-2 at home to Gil Vicente on 19 October [3].

That third defeat cost Vicens his job within a day. Club president António Salvador described the change as a cut rather than a transition, according to A Bola [4] — the language of a club that sees a season going wrong, not a planned handover.

Five defeats, and a goal difference of minus nine

Nacional's last five read LLLLL, and the numbers behind it are ugly: three goals scored, thirteen conceded. A 0-4 home defeat to Famalicao and a 1-3 home defeat to Alverca are the heaviest, and a 0-1 exit at Sanjoanense in the Taça de Portugal sits at the top of the run. Eight games into the league season they are on minus nine goals.

The xG adds some context. Nacional created 1.23 against Famalicao while losing 0-4, and 2.2 against Alverca while losing 1-3, so the defeats were heavier than the chances suggested. They also had eight days to prepare, one more than Braga, after that cup exit.

Braga's problem is at the other end. They have scored three goals in their last five matches, and managed only 0.55 xG in the 0-3 defeat at Copenhagen. Even in the 1-3 home loss to Sporting their xG was higher than the visitors' (1.5 to 0.95), which says the finishing, not the build-up, has been the issue.

Carvalho's first team sheet

What Carvalho does with the shape is the open question of the weekend. He has had a day in the job and his first selection is this match, so the training-ground work behind it is thin. He said he always had the desire to sit in this position [6], which tells us about his ambition rather than his plan.

The mood around the club is part of the story. Braga issued a public statement calling refereeing a total loss of direction and saying they were harmed against Gil Vicente, Moreirense and Sporting [5]. A Bola reported [7] that Salvador received a vote of confidence from the SAD during a period of contestation; the club has not confirmed it. Vicens, in his farewell, called his time at Braga a privilege [8].

Clear favourites, with less conviction

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Braga are 60.2% to win, the draw 23.4% and Nacional 16.4%. That makes the visitors clear favourites — more likely than not to take three points, and close to four times Nacional's chance of doing so.

The handicap says the same in goals. The main line is Nacional +1, and the market's 50/50 line sits at 0.92: Braga are rated about nine-tenths of a goal better, down from 1.07 when the line was first seen. Nacional +1 has gained 4.1 points while Braga's win chance fell from 63.2% to 60.2%. The timing is consistent with the third straight defeat and the coaching change.

The goals line sits at 2.5, with over priced at 50.3% — about as close to a coin flip as this market gets. Nacional have conceded thirteen in five, but Braga have scored only three in the same span. Both teams to score is at 48.5%.

What we said before Gil Vicente

On 19 October we called Braga clear favourites at about 63% before they hosted Gil Vicente, and noted how little the price had moved. Gil Vicente won 1-2, and our marginal lean towards under 2.5 goals went with it. The lesson we took was that a still market on a heavy favourite is worth watching as a warning sign. This week the movement has gone the other way, with Braga trimmed and the handicap line cut — the same direction as the news.

The gap is real, and it is smaller

Braga remain the better side by the table and by the price, and Nacional's five straight defeats are the reason the market can rate an away team on a three-match losing run as a 60.2% favourite. But the two things that changed this week — the sacking and the trimmed handicap — point the same way. Whether a new coach can change that on his first day is the one question the numbers cannot answer.
