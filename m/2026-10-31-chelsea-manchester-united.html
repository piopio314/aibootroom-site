Fourth against thirteenth, and a manager in the middle of it

Stamford Bridge has the lunchtime game on Saturday, and the two clubs arrive from opposite directions. Chelsea are fourth with 13 points from seven league games. Manchester United are 13th with seven points, one win and four draws. Chelsea are 48.5% to win at Stamford Bridge, the draw 24.6% and Manchester United 26.9%.

United's week made the gap in the news wider than the gap in the table. They lost 4-1 at Como in the Champions League, and BBC Sport reports that Michael Carrick's job is in jeopardy [6]. Carrick said afterwards that he takes responsibility for the team and the performance and that it stops with him [7], and he insists the board support him [8].

ESPN reports that this is United's worst start since 1986 [9]. That is the background a visiting manager carries into a ground where his team won 1-0 in April.

United are not being blown away, they are being out-created

United's last five read LDLDD, and three of those were draws, so they are not being taken apart every week. The chance numbers say something harder. Across those games they created 1.08 xG a game and conceded 1.66, and they have scored four goals in five matches. They have won once in seven league games, with four draws.

Chelsea's run is better but not clean. They won 2-0 at Everton on 17 October with the xG 1.47 to 0.43, their most controlled afternoon of the stretch, after a 5-1 win over Bournemouth. Even so, that Bournemouth scoreline came with only 1.1 xG created and 1.38 against, and they lost 0-3 at Brentford in the same run. Chelsea have had 14 days since Everton; United have had 10 since Como.

Eight missing for Europe, and a squad question at Chelsea

The Manchester Evening News reported that eight players were absent from United's 23-man squad for the trip to Como [11]. After a European away game and with a lunchtime kick-off to follow, who is available again matters to this match more than the small price shift towards Chelsea suggests.

At Chelsea the news has been about the squad rather than the first eleven. BBC Sport reported that Josh Acheampong's playing time this season had been in doubt [2], and sixteen hours later Yahoo Sports reported that the club consider him untouchable and part of Xabi Alonso's plans [3]. Two reports, one day apart, pointing in different directions.

Half a goal better, and a game expected to have goals

Chelsea's 48.5% is nearly twice United's 26.9%, so the home side is a clear favourite without being a formality. On the handicap Chelsea are -0.5, which is the market's way of saying they are about half a goal the stronger team: only a Chelsea win lands it, a draw or a United win does not. The fair line has edged from -0.38 to -0.46, still short of a full goal. The goals line is 3.0, with over at 52.9% and both teams to score at 63.9%, which fits a United side conceding 1.66 xG a game and a Chelsea side with 15 goals in their last five.

The fair handicap line has moved from -0.38 to -0.46, still short of a full goal, with Chelsea's win chance up 1.9 points to 48.5% and United's down 1.5 to 26.9%. The moves were small, and that fits a market that had already read United's run before the 4-1 defeat at Como reported by BBC Sport [6].

Our read

Chelsea are the favourites for reasons a fan can see: fourth against 13th, a United team being out-created almost every week, and home advantage. The caution sits on the other side. Chelsea's own chance numbers in this run are not those of a side that wins comfortably, and United have won the last two meetings, including here in April. A clear but not overwhelming home favourite, in a game expected to have goals, is the fair summary of that argument.
