The one place Beşiktaş are safe

Beşiktaş lost 0-2 at Trabzonspor on Sunday, their third away defeat of the season [1]. On Sunday they come home, to a ground where, according to Turkish media, they have won every league game this season [5]. That contrast is the whole match: a team that travels badly and hosts well, against a Başakşehir side already beaten 0-5 on its travels this season.

The pressure sits on the home side. Turkish media describe this as a turning point and a critical game for Beşiktaş [2], and Fotomac reports concern that the club could again be out of the title race by November [3]. Vincenzo Italiano's team started the season strongly before results dipped, according to Yeni Akit [6], and they are fifth with 15 points from eight games. A win keeps them in the leading group; anything less feeds the autumn story.

Başakşehir are tenth on 11 points and arrive off a 2-1 home win over Gaziantep FK, settled by a late goal [7]. They are not in crisis, but their away days have swung hard, and that is exactly what the price is built on.

2.24 expected goals, and one bad afternoon

Beşiktaş's numbers are healthy. Across their last five matches they average 2.24 expected goals — the quality of chances they create — with 1.71 against, and they have scored 11 goals in those games. Sunday was the exception in creation as well as result: at Trabzonspor they made 1.32 and allowed 2.41, so the defeat was not just bad finishing.

Başakşehir's away form is the wide part of their season. They drew 0-0 at Konyaspor with 0.97 expected goals and allowed only 0.41; at Amed on 13 September they made 0.39 and allowed 4.25 in that 0-5 defeat. Their averages are 1.61 for and 1.74 against. Rest is almost equal — seven days for Beşiktaş, eight for Başakşehir — so neither side arrives short of time.

Kökçü missing, and the middle of the pitch

Orkun Kökçü is not expected to feature against Başakşehir while he recovers from injury, according to Sporx [4]. He is a central midfielder, so his absence thins Beşiktaş's control in the middle — the area where a favourite needs to keep the game calm while it chases a second goal. We flagged the same absence before the Crystal Palace game in Europe.

Başakşehir have no absences listed for this match. Their win over Gaziantep featured Muhammed Şengezer, Karbownik, Emin Bayram, Shomurodov and Visca [8], and Visca is the name to watch in that group.

What the price says, and why it moved

The bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, give Beşiktaş a 68.2% chance of winning, the draw 18.1% and Başakşehir 13.8%. That is the home record weighed against the away form: Beşiktaş are roughly five times more likely to take the win than the visitors.

The handicap is the more interesting number. Beşiktaş -1.5 means they must win by two goals or more, and that chance has risen 3.6 points in recent days. The fair line went from -1.28 to -1.38, so the market rates Beşiktaş about 1.4 goals the better side. The main goals line is 3.25, with the over a slight favourite at 52.6%, and both teams to score more likely than not at 57.6%.

What we said in the derby

On 19 October we called Trabzonspor v Beşiktaş a coin-flip derby, 38.1% home against 37.1% away. Trabzonspor won 2-0 and the goals came in under the 3.25 line, so the split read held and our lean toward goals did not. What that night did show is the pattern this preview rests on: Beşiktaş away are a near coin flip, Beşiktaş at home have been something else. We also flagged Kökçü's absence before the Crystal Palace game; it is still the main question in the middle.

Our read

Beşiktaş should win this. The home record, the 2.24 expected goals and a Başakşehir side that has already lost 0-5 away all point the same way, and the market has firmed rather than softened after Sunday. The harder question is the margin. A two-goal win is rated close to a coin flip, and with Kökçü out of the midfield and the pressure of a critical game around the team, an open 3-2 like the February meeting is as plausible as a comfortable afternoon. The win is the likely part; the -1.5 is the part to hold loosely.
