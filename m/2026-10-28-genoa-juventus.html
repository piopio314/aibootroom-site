A wounded favourite meets a struggling host

Juventus lost 0-3 at home to Lazio on 18 October [3], and the questions have not stopped since. Most of them are about coach Luciano Spalletti. Gianluigi Buffon told Football Italia that the coach is not the problem, that he deserves time, and that he understands Bremer's outburst after the game [2]. Get Italian Football News reports that chaos followed the defeat and that Bremer's comments drew particular attention [4].

The talk has gone further than that. Massimo Mauro said Juventus played like a Serie D team against Lazio [5]. Gazzetta writes that Antonio Conte, a free agent living in Turin, does not rule out a new mid-season job and that Juventus fans are calling for him [6]. That is a report about a possibility, not a decision by the club.

Genoa are the hosts, but they come with problems of their own. They are 17th in the 20-team table, with 4 points from 7 games, and they lost 0-2 at Roma on 17 October. Calcio Magazine calls their next league game, at home to Venezia, a delicate relegation clash [9]. So this is a hurt team against a struggling one: the table is on Juventus' side, the mood around the club is not.

The chances say the Lazio game was closer than 0-3

A heavy score does not always tell the whole story. Against Lazio, Juventus created slightly more than they allowed: 0.75 expected goals (xG, the quality of chances) against 0.57. Over their last five games Juventus have won three, drawn one and lost one, scoring 10 goals and allowing 5. Their average is 1.49 xG for and 0.69 against. Even the 1-1 draw at Cagliari came with 1.95 xG for Juventus and only 0.52 against.

In Serie A Juventus are 8th with 11 points from 7 games: three wins, two draws and two defeats. Genoa's league record is harder to read. Over their last five games, which include a Coppa Italia win over Sudtirol, they have scored 5 and conceded 6, with 1.13 xG for and 1.15 against. That is an even picture on chances. The table is not even: Genoa have one win, one draw and five defeats in the league, with a goal difference of -8. They did beat Fiorentina 2-1 at home on 10 October, but at Roma they created only 0.47 xG against 1.33 for the home side.

The history favours the visitors. Juventus have won four of the last five meetings, and the other was a 0-0 draw. Genoa did not score in any of the five. The latest game was a 2-0 Juventus win in Turin on 6 April.

A shoulder injury for Juventus, small returns for Genoa

Juventus announced that Francisco Conceicao dislocated his shoulder against Lazio. He will not have surgery and will receive conservative treatment instead, according to Goal.com [1].

Genoa list L. Venturino as out with jumper's knee. There is better news for coach De Rossi, at least on the bench. Repubblica Genova reports that Colombo [7] and Meichtry [8] are back training with the group, but only for a few minutes. The report says they could help with the choice of substitutes, not that they will start.

The market still sees a clear Juventus favourite

Across the 9 bookmakers we compare, and with their margin removed, the win chances are 58.6% for Juventus, 24.5% for the draw and 17.0% for Genoa. That makes Juventus favourites by more than three times Genoa's chance. The gap in the table, 8th against 17th, is consistent with that price. It also fits the chances created: the market does not seem to treat the 0-3 against Lazio as a sign that Juventus have collapsed.

The handicap is a head start in goals. Genoa get a head start of three-quarters of a goal on the main line, and the fair line is about 0.84 goals in Juventus' favour. In recent days Juventus' chance edged up from 57.7% to 58.6%, and the fair handicap line moved from 0.81 to 0.84. Those are small changes. On goals, the main line is 2.5 and the chance of over 2.5 is 46.8%, so a game with fewer goals is slightly more likely. That is consistent with Genoa's weak attack in their last away game and with Juventus allowing few chances recently. The chance that both teams score is 47.3%.

Our read

This match turns on one question: does a team in trouble off the pitch still beat a team in trouble on the table? The football says Juventus played better than the score against Lazio, and Genoa have not scored against them in their last five meetings. The market agrees and keeps Juventus clear favourites, but clear favourites are not certain winners. If Genoa defend as they did in their better games and Juventus carry the mood of the Lazio defeat, a low-scoring game is the most natural shape.
