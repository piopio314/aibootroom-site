Kauno's first home night, and a gap in level

Kauno Žalgiris have waited a long time for Thursday. It is their first ever home match in a European league phase, at the Darius and Girėnas Stadium, against a Brann side that, according to Lithuanian media, started their European season with an unexpectedly hard-fought win [6]. The timing is odd: Kauno arrive from a 1-0 win at Sūduva on Monday, scored in the third minute of stoppage time, that left them close to a second league title [3].

One week earlier came the measure of the step up. Brighton beat them 4-0, with Georginio Rutter scoring twice [5], and Kauno created 0.04 expected goals across the whole night while allowing 4.02. Brann beat Lincoln Red Imps 2-1 on matchday one and sit 12th of 36 in the table; Kauno are 36th of 36, with no points and a goal difference of -4.

Kauno's coach Ž. Sopićius said his team are proud and happy to play in a European competition and will give everything to win points against Brann [2]. That is the honest frame for the night: the occasion belongs to the home side, the level belongs to the visitors.

Brann lose, Brann create

Brann have lost their last two Eliteserien matches, and neither defeat was a collapse in performance. They made 2.57 expected goals in a 0-2 home loss to Viking, then 2.93 in a 1-3 defeat at Vålerenga on Sunday, a game in which they also allowed 2.76. Over their last five they average 2.14 expected goals for and 1.50 against — chances at both ends of the pitch.

Kauno's domestic results are good: three wins in five, including the cup win at Minija and league wins over Šiauliai and Sūduva. The only game in that run with xG data is Brighton, and 0.04 against 4.02 is why this looks like a step up in class. Rest is short on both sides — three days for Kauno, four for Brann — so some rotation is likely.

What the price says: 56%, and a bigger head start

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Brann are 56.1% to win in Kaunas, the draw 22.7% and Kauno 21.2%. Brann are therefore more than two and a half times more likely than the home side — consistent with a club that won its opener against one that was beaten 4-0 and created almost nothing.

The handicap says the same in different words. Kauno are given a +0.75 goal head start, the middle ground between half a goal and a full one: a draw or a Kauno win clears it, a one-goal defeat only half clears it. The fair line has moved from 0.67 to 0.78 in Brann's favour in recent days, so the market now places the visitors closer to a full goal the better team than to two-thirds of a goal.

On goals, the main line is 2.75 and over is priced at 51.2%, with the fair total up from 2.68 to 2.79; both teams to score is at 55.7%. That fits a Brann team whose last five produced 2.14 expected goals for and 1.50 against, and a Kauno side that will have to open up at some point.
