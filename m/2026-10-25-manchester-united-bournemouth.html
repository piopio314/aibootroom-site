United chase a first win in seven against Bournemouth

A club under pressure, a visitor finding its feet

Old Trafford hosts a game that is less about the table than about the mood. Manchester United are 13th with seven points from seven league games and have gone seven matches without a win [4]. Tuesday brought a 1-4 defeat at Como, and BBC Sport reports that Michael Carrick's job is in jeopardy after it [1]. BBC Sport also reports the club first need to decide who will be in charge for Sunday [3].

Carrick has not stepped away from the result. "I'll take responsibility for the overall team and performance, it stops with me and I'm fine with that," he said after the Como game, according to The Guardian [2]. The Guardian also reports he insists the board support him, after Erik ten Hag pointed to a "toxic culture" at the club [6]. Bleacher Report counts two wins in 11 matches across all competitions this season [5].

Bournemouth arrive with the opposite feeling. They beat Sunderland 2-0 at home on 18 October for their first league win of the season, with 4.4 expected goals to 0.13 against, according to LiveScore [10]. They sit 15th with six points, so the table still looks modest. What they create says something different.

United's draws hide a chance problem

United's last five read three draws and two defeats, and the draws were not dominant ones. At home to Tottenham they had 0.8 expected goals to 1.62 against; at Leeds it was 0.93 to 1.48. Across those five games they average 1.08 expected goals and allow 1.66, so the 4-1 at Como fits a pattern rather than breaking one. Four rest days separate them from that night.

Bournemouth's numbers run the other way: 2.36 expected goals per game over their last five and 0.81 against. Their 1-5 defeat at Chelsea on 10 October looks worse than the performance, because they had 1.38 expected goals to Chelsea's 1.10. They have had seven days to prepare, and BBC Sport notes a Europa League meeting with AC Milan awaits them after the trip to Old Trafford [12].

Bournemouth lose a midfielder, United lose certainty

Bournemouth will likely be without Scott in midfield, with Sports Mole reporting a two-week absence that covers Sunday's trip [9]. Their attacking options may grow instead: Goal reports teenager Eli Junior Kroupi has returned to first-team training and could make his first appearance of the season [7], though he has no minutes this season behind him. One is a midfield option lost, the other a squad place gained.

United's uncertainty is not about players. BBC Sport reports the club first need to decide who will be in charge for Sunday's game [3], which is unusual preparation for any match, let alone one against a side creating 2.36 expected goals a game. Carrick's own words after Como were about taking responsibility for the performance [2], and the mood around the team is the main story of the week.

A half-goal favourite with a three-goal line

The win chances come from the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed. United are 50.8% to win, the draw is 25.0% and Bournemouth 24.2% — the visitors at roughly half the home side's chance. The handicap explains the size of it: the fair line has United about half a goal better at -0.55, and the main line is -0.5. That describes a narrow home win, not a comfortable one, which fits a team with three draws in its last five.

The goals line is 3.0, with Over priced at 48.1%, so the market leans just under three goals — a high line for a match with only a half-goal favourite. Both teams to score is more likely than not at 61.1%, and the recent meetings show why: 4-4 at Old Trafford in December 2025 and 2-2 at Bournemouth in March. The one small move this week was on goals, with Over 3.0 down 1.3 points and the fair line drifting from 2.97 to 2.92, consistent with a slightly tighter reading. United's win chance barely moved, down 0.5 points, so the Como defeat did not shake the home price.

Receipts: two days ago, a very different price

On 22 October we wrote about Bournemouth's 4.4 expected goals against Sunderland and made them 47.7% favourites at home to AC Milan. On Sunday the same team is a 24.2% underdog at Old Trafford. The shift is about the ground and the opponent, not about Bournemouth's chance creation disappearing.

Home advantage against chance creation

Sunday's story is home advantage against chance creation. United are the favourites because they are at Old Trafford and because Bournemouth have won once in the league, not because anything in their recent play looks convincing. The half-goal handicap and the three-goal line describe the game the market expects: close, with both sides scoring. Bournemouth's expected goals say their chance is a real one, and United's seven-game wait says it is not a small one.
