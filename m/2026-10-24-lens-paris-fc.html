A European hangover at Bollaert

Bollaert has an awkward fixture this weekend. Lens are the home side and the favourites, but they arrive three days after a 0-3 defeat at Club Brugge in the Champions League, their second straight loss in Europe [1]. Paris FC arrive third in Ligue 1, with 14 points from seven games and a 4-3 win over Rennes behind them [7].

That is the tension of the night: the table points one way, the venue points the other. Lens are eighth with 10 points from seven, and they had won their two previous league games before Bruges, including 3-1 at Troyes [2]. The market still makes Lens favourites at 40.8% to win, with Paris FC at 33.0% and the draw at 26.2%.

It matters because both teams are in a good moment but for different reasons. Lens have a European score to answer for in front of their own crowd. Paris FC have a chance to stay near the top of the table by winning at one of the loudest grounds in France.

Lens' league run against Paris FC's chance data

Across their last five matches Lens average 1.56 expected goals and allow 1.65, so the chance data does not flatter them. The two league wins were narrower than the scores suggest: 1.43 xG created at Troyes, 1.34 at home to Lyon. In Bruges they created 2.14 and allowed 2.54, an open game they lost by three.

Paris FC have the better attacking numbers of the two, at 1.85 xG a game over their last five, and they allow 1.21. The 4-3 against Rennes came with 3.62 xG created, though three of Rennes' goals were Estéban Lepaul penalties [7]. Their one flat afternoon recently was the 0-1 at Lorient, where they created 0.97. They have also had six days to prepare; Lens have had three.

Rotation, and a president leaving

The interesting Lens question is freshness. According to sports.fr, Lens started with Kyllian Antonio, Florian Thauvin and Odsonne Édouard on the bench in Bruges [5]. Thauvin had set up two goals in the 3-1 win at Troyes four days earlier [2], so a fresher version of him on Saturday would matter for a side that needs to create at home.

Paris FC's week has been about the boardroom more than the pitch. Yahoo Sports reported that president Pierre Ferracci is leaving his position, bringing his exit forward from the end of the season [6]. That is a change at a club sitting third, in the week of a trip to Bollaert.

A quarter of a goal, and a line of 2.75

The gap is the venue

The football and the market point the same way, with a small gap between the teams. Lens have the ground, the recent head-to-head — 5-0 in Paris in February, 2-1 in Lens last October — and a league run that is healthier than two European defeats suggest. Paris FC have the table, the chance creation and three extra days of rest. At 40.8% Lens are favourites for the win, and with the draw at 26.2% they are more likely than not to avoid defeat — but nowhere near sure of the win, in a match the goals line expects to be open.
