A table that does not match the performances

Bournemouth are 15th in the Premier League with six points from seven games, and one of those games was a 5-1 defeat at Chelsea [2]. On Saturday they host a Leeds side that sits seventh with ten points and has lost once all season. On results alone, the visitors are the better team.

The chance data says something different. Across their last five matches Bournemouth have created 2.36 expected goals a game and conceded 0.81 — the profile of a top-half side, not one in the bottom six. Their only league win, 2-0 at home to Sunderland, came with 4.4 expected goals created and 0.13 allowed [1].

That gap is the story of the afternoon. Bournemouth need to turn good chances into points before the table becomes the whole conversation, and Leeds arrive wanting to make the game narrow.

The Chelsea scoreline and the Leeds pattern

Bournemouth's run also includes a 3-0 Europa League win over Sturm Graz with 2.88 expected goals to 0.37, and a 0-1 home defeat by Liverpool in which they managed 0.79. The Chelsea afternoon is the odd one: the score was 1-5, but the xG read 1.38 to 1.10 in Bournemouth's favour. A result like that pulls a league position down faster than it pulls the underlying numbers.

Leeds have drawn 1-1 with Manchester United [6], lost 1-2 at Arsenal with only 0.62 expected goals created, drawn 0-0 with Crystal Palace and beaten Newcastle 4-1 at 2.08 to 0.88. They average 1.37 expected goals a game, so the Newcastle match is the exception rather than the pattern. Both sides have had 13 days since their last fixture.

The same two numbers as in Europe

Earlier this month we made Bournemouth favourites at home in Europe at 47.7%, and the reasons were the same two numbers that matter here: how much they create and how little they allow. Nothing in the last few weeks has changed that view. The open question for Saturday is whether the league table catches up with the chances, or whether Leeds' organisation keeps the gap at a quarter of a goal.

Both things are true at once

Bournemouth are 15th and favourites, and both facts come from the same place: their results have run behind their chances. Leeds are eight places higher in the table and still rated only a quarter of a goal behind, because one defeat in seven earns respect. The market has joined the two pictures rather than choosing one — home advantage and chance quality on one side, points and organisation on the other. If Bournemouth repeat the Sunderland level, the result follows; if Leeds repeat the shape of their Arsenal and Palace games, the quarter-goal line will look about right.
