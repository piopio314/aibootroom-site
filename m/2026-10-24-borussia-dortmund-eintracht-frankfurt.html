A gap in the table, a gap in the legs

Six games into the Bundesliga, Borussia Dortmund are second on 14 points and have not lost. Eintracht Frankfurt are eleventh on eight. The distance between them is not only points: Frankfurt have conceded 13 goals in their last five league games, and their one away trip to a top side this season ended 0-6 at RB Leipzig on 10 October.

There is a second story inside this match, and it is about recovery. Dortmund played in Baku on Tuesday and have four days before Saturday. Frankfurt have not played since 16 October and have had eight. Dortmund's own website counted 11,322 kilometres travelled in a week across Bodø, Berlin and Baku, and said players and staff found the schedule exhausting [3].

The opportunity is easy to state. Dortmund can stay unbeaten and keep their place near the top, but they have to do it with half the rest of their opponents. Frankfurt, meanwhile, have a chance to show that Leipzig was an accident rather than a pattern — and that will require defending far better than they did that afternoon.

Dortmund win without blowing teams away

Dortmund's league record is built on not losing more than on dominating. Their last Bundesliga match was a 0-0 at Union Berlin [6], and before that they drew 2-2 at home with Werder Bremen after creating 2.42 xG. Across their last five matches in all competitions they average 1.89 xG for and 1.11 against, but those five games produced only five goals. Their last three league results — 1-0, 2-2 and 0-0 — did not include a two-goal win.

The midweek trip to Sabah FK looked different: 3.75 xG, the highest of their last five matches, and a 2-1 win [1]. Frankfurt's problem is at the other end. They give up 2.23 xG per game over their last five, and Leipzig alone generated 4.03 against them. Even so, they beat Köln 1-0 last time with only 0.8 xG conceded, so the defence can hold when the opponent is not a top side. Their attack is not the issue: Frankfurt average 1.94 xG for over the same five games, and their last five matches have produced 20 goals.

Rotation in Dortmund, transition in Frankfurt

Dortmund's midweek team sheet told a rotation story. Konstantinos Karetsas was absent against Sabah FK, Carney Chukwuemeka started in his place and was praised by Niko Kovač for his effort, according to getfootballnewsgermany.com [4]. Jobe Bellingham was named among the substitutes rather than the starting XI, according to athlonsports.com [5]. Serhou Guirassy, who scored twice in Baku [1] and has two Bundesliga goals this season [2], is again the forward they will look for.

Frankfurt's news is off the pitch. The club confirmed that sporting director Markus Krösche left at his own request three days ago [7], and Bild reports that AC Milan are in advanced talks to appoint him, with an exit clause worth between 3.5 and 5 million euros [8]. A successor is still being sought, according to echo-online.de [10]. Coach Adi Hütter, meanwhile, says he has good memories of beating Borussia Dortmund, according to the same outlet [9].

One and a quarter goals better

With the margin removed across the six bookmakers we compare, the market makes Dortmund 65.2% favourites, the draw 18.4% and Frankfurt 16.4%. Dortmund's chance is roughly four times Frankfurt's. That is consistent with an unbeaten second-placed side meeting a team that has shipped 13 goals in five league games.

The fair handicap is Dortmund -1.25, which means the market rates them about one and a quarter goals the better team. In plain terms, a one-goal Dortmund win is not enough; they need two. The goals line sits at 3.59, making over 3.5 a marginal favourite at 51.8%, and both teams to score is at 64.1% — the market expects Frankfurt to contribute something.

Quality against fresh legs

This is the match the numbers describe: the better team, at home, unbeaten, against a side that leaks chances and has just lost its sporting director. The price reflects all of that. What it does not show is the one thing that could close the gap — four days of recovery for Dortmund against eight for Frankfurt, after a week of travel their own staff called exhausting. Dortmund are clear favourites. Whether they are a goal and a quarter better on tired legs is the open question, and it is the question Saturday answers.
