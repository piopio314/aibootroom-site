A winless host and a defence that leaks

Friday night in Deventer brings together two teams stuck in a run: Go Ahead Eagles have drawn four and lost one of their last five, and Telstar have taken two points from their last five. Yet the market puts the hosts at 59.5% to win. That gap between the run and the price is the story of this match.

Go Ahead's most recent game was a 1-4 defeat at PEC Zwolle on Sunday, five days before kick-off. A heavy scoreline like that usually pushes a price the other way. It did not, and the reason sits in the chances: Go Ahead created 2.76 expected goals at Zwolle against 1.81 for the home side. They lost the finishing, not the game.

Telstar come in 16th of 18 with six points from nine games, five behind the hosts, and they lost 3-4 at home to Feyenoord on Saturday [5]. For a side near the bottom, though, they are not a team that sits deep and defends: they go forward and leave space behind.

Four draws, and the chances to explain them

Go Ahead's run reads LDDDD, but the expected goals say it is a team creating enough to win. They average 2.08 xG per game across those five matches and allow 1.57. The clearest example is the 1-1 with Groningen: 2.23 xG to 0.16 against, and only one point. The 3-3 with Sparta Rotterdam went the other way, 1.29 created to 2.36 allowed.

Telstar's numbers point the same way but with less on the good side. They create 1.12 xG per game and concede 2.35, and their three away matches in this run produced 0.52, 0.71 and 0.1 xG — almost nothing on the road. At home they are livelier: 2.86 xG against ADO Den Haag, and 1.43 against Feyenoord while allowing 3.83. The table gap — 9th on 11 points against 16th on six — is the base of the home favouritism, and Go Ahead have had one day less rest (five against six).

Cillessen arrives as cover

Go Ahead are without P. Saathof, who is out with a knee injury. The bigger news of the week is in goal: the club signed goalkeeper Jasper Cillessen until the end of the season after reserve keeper Nick Marsman suffered a knee injury [1]. The signing was announced on Wednesday evening [2], and according to De Telegraaf, Cillessen will compete with Kjetil Haug for the goalkeeper spot [3].

That is cover for the squad rather than a change to the first team. Off the pitch the club also published strong annual figures on Tuesday, with net revenue rising sharply thanks partly to last season's Europa League participation [4]. Telstar list no absentees for this game.

One goal better, and a line above three

The win chances, across the 10 bookmakers compared with the margin removed, are 59.5% Go Ahead, 23.3% draw and 17.2% Telstar. The handicap line is -1, and the fair line sits at -0.97: the market rates the hosts roughly one goal better, with Go Ahead more likely than not to win and the draw the second-most likely result. The draw at 23.3% fits a team that has drawn four of its last five.

The goals line is 3.25, with the over at 51.0% and both teams to score at 63.6%. The fair goals line has come in from 3.41 to 3.30 in recent days, still above three. Telstar's last game produced seven goals in a 3-4 defeat to Feyenoord, and Go Ahead have scored nine and conceded twelve in five, so an open evening is the base case rather than a surprise.

Winless, not collapsing

The football and the price agree on the important part. Go Ahead cannot close games out — four draws in five says that clearly — but they keep creating more than they allow, and they now meet a defence that concedes 2.35 expected goals a game and creates almost nothing away from home. That combination is why a winless side is still rated about a goal the better team, and why a line above three goals is the market's base case. The hosts are favourites, not certainties: the draw is priced at 23.3%, the second-most likely result on Friday.
