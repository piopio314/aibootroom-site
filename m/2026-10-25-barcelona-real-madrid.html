Nine from nine, then Paris

El Clasico arrives with the table telling one story and the last seven days telling another. Barcelona have won all nine of their La Liga matches this season and lead Real Madrid by six points. A win on Sunday puts them nine clear; a Madrid win cuts the gap to three.

Barcelona, though, come into it off a defeat. Tuesday's 4-2 loss at PSG ended an 11-game winning run and was their first loss of the season [1]. It was not a narrow one either: PSG created 3.74 expected goals against them, more than any of their previous four opponents managed.

Madrid arrive a different team. They beat Sevilla 4-1 at home with Kylian Mbappe scoring twice [7], and Jose Mourinho returns to Camp Nou as their coach needing to close the gap [9]. Raphinha brushed aside the Paris result and said the focus had moved to Sunday, according to World Soccer Talk [5].

The chances say one thing, the scorelines another

Barcelona's league run is not just a product of finishes. Over their last five matches they average 2.72 expected goals, against Madrid's 1.82, and both sides allow exactly the same at the other end, 1.19. The gap between these teams sits in what they create.

Madrid's numbers carry a warning. Their 1-2 loss at Atletico Madrid in September came with just 0.3 xG created, and their 4-1 over Sevilla was built on 2.28 xG. Rest is slightly in their favour — seven days since that Sevilla win, five for Barcelona since Paris. But the recent history at Camp Nou leans home: Barcelona won the last meeting there 2-0 in May, and their only Clasico defeat in the last five across all competitions came at Madrid in October 2025.

Barcelona's week is about fitness

Dani Olmo was forced off injured in the first half against PSG and replaced by Fermin Lopez, USA Today reported [2]. Mundo Deportivo now reports the club is increasingly pessimistic about Jules Kounde being fit for Sunday because of a serious ankle sprain [3].

Losing the right-sided defender matters more than most absences, because Madrid's attacking plan at Camp Nou starts with Mbappe and the space beside him. For Madrid, SI.com reports Vinicius Jr is currently set to miss the Clasico and two more league games through suspension, with the club trying to have the ban reduced [8]. That points the other way: Madrid may travel without their first-choice threat on the left.

Favourites, by a goal, in a high-scoring game

The win chances come from the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed. Barcelona are 59.4% favourites, the draw 16.5% and Madrid 24.1% — the home side rated more than twice as likely as Madrid to win. On the handicap Barcelona are rated about a goal the better team: the main line is -1, meaning the market expects them to win by around a goal, with the two-goal margin the dividing line.

The goals line is high. The market's fair total is 4.18, the main line sits at 4.25 and over is 48.4% — close to a coin flip. The market prices both teams to score as more likely than not at 73.2%, which fits both sides' recent games: Barcelona's last five produced 25 goals in total, and Madrid have won 4-3 in Rome and 4-1 over Sevilla.

This week the price moved towards Madrid. Their chance rose from 19.8% to 24.1%, Barcelona's fell 3.6 points, the handicap fair line came in from -1.19 to -1.02 and the goals fair line climbed from 4.03 to 4.18. The shift is consistent with Barcelona's injury news after Paris rather than with anything on the Madrid side, because the Vinicius ban points the other way.

The football and the price pull apart

Sunday sets Barcelona's nine-win league start against a team that has found its scorer. Barcelona have won every league game, create roughly a goal's worth more chances per game than Madrid, and beat them 2-0 at Camp Nou in May. Madrid have Mbappe, two extra days of rest, and a coach returning to Camp Nou.

The market still makes Barcelona more likely than not to win, but its confidence has been trimmed since Paris, and the goals line has climbed into the high fours. The football reasons sit mostly with Barcelona; the news of the week sits mostly against them. That is the tension to watch: a home side that has not dropped a league point, tested by the one opponent that can punish a defence held together with doubts.

The market expects goals, and it expects the champions of this autumn to edge it — but by less than it did a week ago.
