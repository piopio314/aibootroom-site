The table says one thing, the market another

Alverca host Arouca on Saturday with a league table that reads the wrong way round for the home crowd. The visitors are 5th in the Primeira Liga on 14 points after eight games; Alverca are 8th on 11. Yet the home side is the favourite at 44.0% to win, against Arouca's 25.6%.

The reason to look closer is what happened last weekend. Alverca went out of the Taça de Portugal at Tondela, losing 0-2 to goals from Carraça and Tiago Dias [1]. A Bola reports that this league game now carries extra weight for them, as the chance to heal the wound of that cup exit [2].

Arouca arrive from the same competition on a very different note. They beat Belenenses 4-3 after extra time to reach the fourth round [5] — a win, but one that required extra time. Both teams have had six days since their last game, so neither arrives short of rest.

Alverca create more, Arouca convert more

Alverca had won three league games in a row before the cup defeat, and the chance numbers behind that run were strong. They averaged 2.11 expected goals per game across their recent league matches, including 3.3 xG in the 1-0 home win over Rio Ave and 2.92 in the 4-1 win at Famalicão. Even the 3-1 win at Nacional came with only 1.16 xG against 2.2 allowed, so not every result was built on control.

Arouca's recent record runs the other way: the results are better than the performances. They beat Estrela da Amadora 1-0 at home with just 0.74 xG and 1.45 allowed, and their average across recent league games is 1.15 xG created against 1.19 conceded. Their best attacking display was the 2-2 at Sporting CP with 2.08 xG. That gap between creation and points is why the home side is favoured despite the table. The last two meetings were split: Alverca won 2-1 at home in April, Arouca won 1-0 at home in December.

A drought ends, and eyes on Alverca's players

The week's Alverca stories are about attention from bigger clubs rather than about absences. According to Glorioso1904, a forward in the Alverca squad is being tracked by Benfica [3], and ESPN reports that defender Carlos Lopes Rodrigues is being scouted by Sampdoria, Pisa and Empoli [4]. Both are reported, not confirmed, and neither changes what Alverca are on the pitch.

For Arouca, the cup night produced the news they wanted. Mayulu scored to end a goal drought lasting more than two years and was decisive in the tie, according to Record [6] — exactly the contribution an Arouca attack averaging 1.15 xG per league game has lacked. FPL Tuga's review of the Estrela match notes that Barbero scored early in that 1-0 win [7]. Pedro Ramalho will referee the game, with Paulo Barradas on VAR [8].

A quarter of a goal between them

The win chances, with the bookmakers' margin removed across the ten bookmakers we compare, are 44.0% Alverca, 30.4% draw and 25.6% Arouca. That is a home advantage, not a clear one: the draw is closer to Alverca's number than Arouca's is. The handicap tells the same story in a different way. The main line is a quarter of a goal to Alverca, and the fair 50/50 line sits at -0.32 — the home side rated about a third of a goal the better team, so one goal either way settles it.

The goals line sits at 2.49, with Over 2.5 at 49.7% — almost a coin flip — and both teams to score at 54.6%. That fits two sides who have found the net regularly but neither of whom dominates.

Why the home side is favoured anyway

Nothing here is a contradiction once the chance numbers are in view. Arouca have the better table because they have turned ordinary games into points — 0.74 xG for a home win, four goals in a cup tie that needed extra time. Alverca have the better attack, with 2.11 xG per game and a three-win league run behind them, and they are at home. That is why the market puts them at 44.0% and rates them a third of a goal stronger, but no more than that. The gap is small enough that the draw and Arouca's counter-attacking night are both live, and a single goal is likely to decide which way it falls.
