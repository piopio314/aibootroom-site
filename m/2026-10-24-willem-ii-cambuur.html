A new voice in the bottom corner

Willem II go into Saturday's home match against Cambuur bottom of the Eredivisie with two points from nine games and a goal difference of -17. The club sacked head coach John Stegeman two days ago [1] and named Adrie Koster as interim coach only hours ago [2]. A season that had already gone wrong now has a new man in charge of fixing it, with very little time to work.

Cambuur arrive from the opposite direction. They are unbeaten in five league matches and drew 2-2 at home with AZ Alkmaar last weekend, a result Dutch media treated as a strong performance [7]. One club is changing its voice mid-season; the other is in rhythm and has been for a month.

That is why 18th against 14th matters more than the table suggests. Willem II are chasing a first league win of the season in front of their own crowd, and Koster has had days rather than weeks to prepare. Cambuur are protecting a run that has made them difficult to beat, and a trip to the bottom club is exactly the kind of afternoon that can end one.

The chance data is harder still: they created 1.15 expected goals per game and allowed 2.51. Recent meetings also favour the visitors: Cambuur are unbeaten in the last five against Willem II and won 0-1 in Tilburg in December 2025, though most of those games came in the second tier.

Koster's first week

The team news at Willem II is the bench. Stegeman and assistant Pascal Diender were dismissed together [1][5], and Koster will work with Danny Schenkel and Pim Hendrix, stepping back once a permanent head coach is hired [3]. Koster himself called the job "quite a challenge" [4]. A short week is not much time to change how a side defends, and defending is precisely the problem: 2.51 expected goals conceded per game is a structural issue, not a run of bad luck.

Cambuur's week has been calmer. Leeuwarder Courant reports that the squad travels with confidence but describes the fixture as a "measuring stick" and notes that "these are often the trickiest games" [9]. Coach Johan Plat is reported to be expecting a difficult away afternoon [10], and pundits Kenneth Perez and Leon ten Voorde said this week that the promoted sides, Cambuur among them, are growing into the division and making life harder for opponents [11].

Our read

The through-line is a home team that cannot be priced like a home team. Willem II's trouble is not only the two points and the -17; it is the volume of chances they give away every week, and Koster has one short week to tighten a shape that has been opened repeatedly. Cambuur are the side with the run and the belief, and the market rates them virtual equals in Tilburg rather than underdogs. The level-ball line and the 3.25 total describe a close, open game between two teams separated by very little, where the venue is the home side's only clear advantage — and the prices say that advantage is worth close to nothing.
