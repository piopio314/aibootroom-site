A good start against a stuck one

Dinamo Zagreb have started the Europa League unbeaten and host NEC Nijmegen on Thursday with something to protect. Four wins in five matches, fourteen goals in that run, and an opponent that has not won in five. The shape of the night is clear before kickoff, and the table says the same: Dinamo are 14th of 36 with four points from two games, NEC 27th with one.

NEC arrive in Zagreb at a strange moment. They have drawn four in a row, including 1-1 at Ajax, and lost the game before that 0-5 at Juventus. A team that cannot turn good spells into wins is not in crisis, but it is running out of room: they need a result to stay close to their aim of reaching the knockout stage, according to Drimble [6].

The clubs have met once before in Europe, in 2008, when NEC lost 3-2 in Zagreb [9]. Thursday is only their second visit to Dinamo [10]. That history is thin. The current picture is not.

Dinamo's chances match the scores

Sunday was Dinamo's clearest statement of the run: 5-1 away at Slaven Belupo, with 3.78 expected goals to 2.19 against. The scoreline matched the chances, which is not always true. Across their last five they average 2.29 xG, more than two goals' worth per game, and their one defeat, 1-2 at Hajduk Split, came with 1.39 xG of their own. They had four days to recover, the same as NEC.

NEC's numbers say something more interesting than their results. Against Fortuna Sittard on Sunday they created 3.06 xG, allowed 0.45, and drew 2-2. Against Levski Sofia they made 2.06 and drew 1-1 [7]. The chances are there; closing games out is not. Their 1.8 xG average over five matches is decent for an away side, but they have conceded ten goals in that run, and the 0-5 in Turin still sits in the middle of it.

Tadić stays home, Beljo stays hot

The big team news is Dušan Tadić. According to Drimble's reporting from NEC's press conference in Zagreb, he is missing on Thursday [4]. He has played 1,082 minutes this season, more than any other NEC player, across 13 appearances with three assists. He is the man who usually carries their build-up, and losing him from a side that already struggles to finish games is a real cost.

A full goal, and a three-goal night

The ten bookmakers we compare make Dinamo 58.4% to win once their margin is removed. The draw is 21.8% and NEC 19.8%, so the away chance is barely a third of the home one. A full-goal handicap is on offer, and Dinamo are 46.5% to win by two or more, which puts a one-goal home win at roughly the coin-flip point. The fair line is -0.88 goals: the market rates Dinamo a little under a goal the better team.

Goals are expected as well. The main line is 3.0, with over at 50.9%, so a three-goal game is the 50/50 point, and both teams to score sits at 59.6%. That fits the evidence: Dinamo's last five produced 19 goals, and NEC have scored five across their last five.

The football and the price agree

Dinamo are favourites for football reasons that stack up: fourteen goals in five, 2.29 xG per game, an unbeaten European start, and a striker in Beljo who has 12 in 13. NEC's case is narrower but real. They create chances, they made 3.06 xG on Sunday, and they have drawn four rather than lost four. But they travel without the player who has been on the pitch more than anyone else, and without two defenders, to face a team rated 0.88 goals better. The picture and the price point the same way: more likely than not, Dinamo win, and the game should carry goals.
