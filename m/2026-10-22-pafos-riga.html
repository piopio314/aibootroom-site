A club with no points and a night to change that

Pafos have played one league phase game in the Conference League, and they lost it. They went to Atalanta on 15 October and lost 1-3, which leaves them with 0 points and 32nd place in a table of 36 teams. Tonight they face Riga.

Cypriot media describe the game as the chance to win for the first time in the league phase [2]. That is the through-line of this match: a favourite that has not yet scored a point, against an opponent that has started to collect them.

Riga arrive with the opposite start. They drew 1-1 at home with Kairat Almaty, which gives them 1 point and 22nd place. They are still far from safe, but a first point is a better base than none.

Why the 1-3 at Atalanta was a fair result

The score at Atalanta was not a lucky one for the winners. Pafos created 0.91 expected goals, a measure of how good a team's chances are, and they allowed 3.47. So Atalanta had much better chances, and the 1-3 result was fair. Pafos then drew 0-0 at home with Apoel Nicosia on 19 October, which means they come into this game with a goalless league match behind them and 3 days of rest. Over their last five matches Pafos have won 2, drawn 1 and lost 2, scoring 5 and conceding 6.

Riga look better on paper. They have won 4 and drawn 1 of their last 5 games, scoring 7 goals and conceding 2. One note of care: four of those five games were in the Latvian league, so the opposition was weaker. Even so, the chances were real. They created 2.45 expected goals per game and allowed 0.84. In the Conference League against Kairat Almaty they created 2.31 and allowed 1.15, and still drew 1-1. They also had 4 days of rest after their 2-1 league win over Super Nova on 18 October.

The talk is about mood, not injuries

Before this game the stories are about the importance of the match and the atmosphere. According to Kerkida.net, a Cypriot site, around 4,000 Pafos fans are travelling to Limassol, where the match is played at the Alphamega stadium [3]. Pafos are the home side on the fixture list, but the crowd will have to travel to see them.

Latvian media say Riga keep collecting points in the Conference League as they visit the Cypriot club for their second league phase match [4]. That is a report about their mood and momentum, not a team-news item, but it fits the picture of a side that arrives with confidence.

What the market makes of it

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with their margin removed, the market gives Pafos a 59.5% chance to win, the draw 23.2% and Riga 17.3%. That makes Pafos clear favourites and more likely than not to win, but far from certain. The handicap, which is a head start in goals, tells the same story: the main line is 1 goal, and the fair line says the market rates Pafos about 0.9 goals better than Riga.

In recent days the market moved a little further towards the home side. Pafos's chance rose from 57.1% to 59.5%, a rise of 2.4 points, and Riga's fell from 18.9% to 17.3%. The fair handicap line followed, from 0.83 to 0.9 goals. It is a small move, and it came even after the 1-3 defeat, which is consistent with a market that rates Pafos higher than Riga on the whole. On goals, the main line is 2.75 and the fair total is 2.71 goals. The chance of Over 2.75 is 49.1%, almost level with Under, and the chance that both teams score is 53.2%. That sits well with Riga's scoring and Pafos's mixed results.
