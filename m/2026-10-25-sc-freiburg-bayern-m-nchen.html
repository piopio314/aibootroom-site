First against third, one point apart

On Sunday the Bundesliga leaders go to the team in third, and one point separates them. Bayern have 14 points from six games and have not lost. Freiburg have 13 and have lost once. It is the kind of fixture that usually promises a contest, and the market does not see one: Bayern are 69.3% to win, Freiburg 14.7%.

Freiburg's unbeaten start ended on 18 October at Bayer Leverkusen, a 1-2 defeat [6]. They are still third [7] because they had won three of their previous four, including 5-0 against Gladbach and 2-1 against Schalke. Even so, the step up is real: Bayern have won four of the last five meetings, including 3-2 at Freiburg in April.

Bayern come in off a 6-1 win over RB Leipzig, with Luis Díaz scoring a hat-trick [3], and then a 2-2 with Arsenal on Wednesday [4]. That leaves four days between games, against Freiburg's seven. And Jamal Musiala will not play [1].

What the xG says about 6-1 and 1-2

The 6-1 was spectacular but not a freak. Bayern have averaged 3.63 expected goals across their last five matches and created at least 2.5 in every one of them. That includes the draws: 4.96 xG at Augsburg, 3.51 in the 3-3 with Viking. Chance creation, not only finishing, is behind those scorelines.

The other side of the record is softer. Bayern have conceded in four of their last five and allow 1.11 xG a game. Freiburg's last five read 13 goals for and 7 against, with 1.98 xG created and 1.1 allowed. Those are good numbers, and a full week to prepare them.

Musiala out, and a short week

Sky report that Musiala will miss the Freiburg match because he is not yet back in team training [1]. According to sport.de, he will also sit out at least two more games [2]. He is Bayern's main creative midfielder, the player who usually plays the pass before the assist.

The rest is about recovery rather than injury. Wednesday's Champions League night against Arsenal leaves four days before this game, while Freiburg have had seven since Leverkusen. Freiburg are the fresher side, and that matters against a team that wants to press and attack.

A goal and a half better

With the margin removed, the bookmakers we compare — ten of them — make Bayern 69.3% to win, the draw 16.1% and Freiburg 14.7%. That is more than four times likelier for the visitors. The handicap puts Freiburg 1.5 goals ahead at kick-off, and the fair line is 1.49 goals: the market rates Bayern about a goal and a half the better team. That fits a head-to-head record in which three of the last five meetings were drawn or settled by a single goal.

The total is set at 4.0 goals, with over at 47.8%, a high line and a fair one given Bayern's chance volume. Both teams to score is 66.7%. In recent days the small moves have leaned one way: over 4.0 down 2.2 points, both teams to score down 2.1, the fair total trimmed from 4.0 to 3.9, while Bayern's win chance rose 1.2 points. That is consistent with a short week and with a Freiburg side that has been tighter than the leaders.

Our read

The through-line is this: the best team in the league goes to the third-best, and the gap the market sees — about a goal and a half — is a gap the table cannot show, because one point separates them. Bayern's strength is chance creation and a head-to-head record they control. Freiburg's is a full week, a defence allowing 1.1 xG a game, and a home crowd. That is why the win chance is lopsided while the goals expectation has eased: the likeliest version of this game is a Bayern win without the open, high-scoring afternoon the 6-1 suggests.
