Fourth against sixteenth, and two different moods

Chelsea are fourth, Tottenham are sixteenth, and both walk into Stamford Bridge on Saturday with something to prove. Chelsea have won three of their last five in all competitions and come off a clean-sheet win at Everton. Spurs have just ended their wait for a first Premier League victory of the season, and they did it with their two best creators back on the pitch.

That is the tension of this derby: the table points one way, the news points the other. Chelsea are clear favourites at 54.9%, with Tottenham at 22.5% and the draw at 22.7%, from the bookmakers we compare. But a Spurs team that spent the opening weeks without James Maddison and Dejan Kulusevski is not the same team that now has them [2].

The market made its mind up after the Everton result, not after Tottenham's Monday night win. Chelsea's chance rose by about two and a half points in recent days while Tottenham's fell by around one and a half. Whether that holds depends on whether the 3-1 over Coventry was a turning point or just a good night: the BBC reported that Spurs won it 3-1 [1].

Chelsea's goals are bigger than their chances

Chelsea's run reads won, won, lost, drew, won, but the numbers underneath are odd. They scored 15 goals across those five games and averaged only 1.08 expected goals — the 5-1 home win over Bournemouth came with 1.1 xG for and 1.38 against. They have also conceded 1.46 expected goals per game in that stretch, so the wins have come with real defending to do, and the 0-3 defeat at Brentford fits the pattern.

Tottenham's form is less flattering and arguably more solid: 1.19 xG created and 0.97 allowed per game. The 3-1 over Coventry came from 0.9 xG, while the 1-1 at Manchester United with 1.62 xG and the 2-3 home defeat by Aston Villa with 1.56 were better performances without the points. Chelsea have had seven days since their last match and Tottenham five, two extra days of recovery that matter in a fast derby — a point Spurs Web makes too [15].

One quiet bulletin, one long list

Chelsea's team news is short. Xabi Alonso made one change to the starting XI at Everton [8], and Mike Penders is reported to be back in training next week after making his Premier League debut [10]. Nothing on that side of the derby shakes the picture the market has built.

Tottenham's list is the opposite. Sports Mole reports that Pedro Porro, Savio and Lucas Bergvall are on the injury and suspension list for this match [5], although De Zerbi confirmed that two players will be available despite injury scares [4]. The better news is larger: Maddison and Kulusevski both featured against Coventry and Maddison scored [2]. De Zerbi's message afterwards was that his side must keep their feet on the ground, good but able to get better [3].

Three-quarters of a goal, and a three-goal line

After removing the margin, Chelsea are 54.9% to win, the draw 22.7% and Tottenham 22.5%. The handicap makes Chelsea about three-quarters of a goal the better side: a one-goal Chelsea win only half covers that line, so the line points to a win by roughly a goal rather than a rout.

The goals line sits at 3.0 with Over at 51.2% and both teams to score at 60.6%, which is an open game by the market's own numbers. That fits Chelsea's recent home matches — 5-1, 2-2 and a 6-3 in the cup — and Spurs scoring in each of their last three. The week's move went towards Chelsea: 52.5% to 54.9%, the home handicap at -0.75 gaining around two and a half points, and the fair line shifting from -0.63 to -0.73. That is consistent with a 2-0 win at Everton rather than with anything Spurs did on Monday.

Monday's call, and the part we got wrong

On Monday we called Tottenham clear 62% favourites against Coventry and read the goals market as flat; Spurs won 3-1 and the four goals went over, so the favourite read held while the goals call leaned the wrong way.

A likely home win, in a game that stays open

Chelsea's case is real, but it rests on the table, a good result at Everton and two extra days of rest, not on chance dominance: across their last five they conceded more expected goals than they created. Tottenham's chance quality is better than sixteenth suggests, and they now have the two players who turn that quality into goals. The market's answer is three-quarters of a goal and a three-goal line — a probable Chelsea win, in a derby that should not be quiet.
