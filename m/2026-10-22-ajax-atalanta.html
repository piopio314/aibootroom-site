Two wobbling teams, one home night

Ajax and Atalanta meet in Amsterdam on Thursday with neither side settled. Ajax lost 2-1 at Hajduk Split in their Conference League opener, a result Goal called humiliating [3], then answered with a 5-2 win at Groningen on Sunday. Atalanta arrive after a 3-2 defeat at AC Milan, their third loss in five matches.

The league phase table makes the contrast sharp. Atalanta are 4th of 36 after beating Pafos 3-1; Ajax sit 25th of 36 with no points from their opening game. For Ajax, a home night is the clearest chance to get moving. For Atalanta, every match now carries extra weight.

La Repubblica reports that Sarri's position is on the line, with Thiago Motta and Tudor named as possible substitutes, though the club is said to keep confidence in him for now [7]. Sarri described his team's pattern himself: "In every match we do well for a spell, but we alternate good spells with moments of major limitations" [6].

Goals at both ends

Ajax's last five matches produced 14 goals scored and 8 conceded, with an average of 2.45 xG created and 1.55 allowed. The Groningen win was open in both directions: 3.04 xG for, 2.29 against. Their one European night was the opposite — 1.15 xG created and 1.99 allowed in Split, the only time in this run they were clearly second best.

Atalanta's five games split 9 goals for and 9 against, with 1.95 xG created and 1.72 conceded per match. The 3-1 win over Pafos was their strongest attacking night at 3.47 xG, while the Milan defeat read 1.74 for and 2.58 against. Both clubs had four days between matches, so neither arrives with a rest advantage.

Returns for Ajax, youth for Atalanta

Neither side carries a listed absentee, so the team news is about who is coming back rather than who is out. Daley Blind and Steven Berghuis rejoined Ajax's group training a day before kick-off, according to Voetbalzone [2]. Voetbalzone also reports that Míchel Sánchez is expected to make several changes after the Groningen win [1].

On the Atalanta side, Sarri has named a 22-man squad for the trip [9]. Quotidiano Sportivo reports that he is turning to young players, with the U23 pair Obric and Idele leading what it calls a new green line [10]. That is a squad in transition, and it fits a coach searching for a shape that holds for the full match.

Favourites by a quarter of a goal

The win chances, across the 10 available prices with the margin removed, are Ajax 44.7%, draw 25.3% and Atalanta 30.1%. Ajax are the favourites, nearly half again Atalanta's chance, and the Asian handicap of -0.25 says the same: the market rates Ajax about a quarter of a goal better at home.

The week's moves are small but point one way: Ajax's win chance is up 0.8 points, over 3.0 is up 0.9, and the fair handicap has moved from -0.28 to -0.31 — a slight nudge towards the home side and goals.

The close tie the numbers describe

Everything here points the same way. Ajax are favourites because they are at home and scoring freely, but their only European match so far was a defeat in which they created little. Atalanta are the better-placed side in the table and a dangerous away team, yet they have lost three of five and their coach is working under public pressure. The football and the prices agree: this is a close tie with goals in it, and Ajax's advantage is small enough that a single defensive lapse would level it.
