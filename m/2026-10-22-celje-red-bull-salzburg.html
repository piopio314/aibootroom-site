A first meeting, and a gap in the table

Celje and Red Bull Salzburg have never met before [4]. The Slovenians' third match of the Europa League league phase [5] arrives with zero points, no goals scored in the competition, and a place of 35th out of 36 in the table. Salzburg sit 21st with three points from two games, and the distance between those two lines is the story of the night.

Salzburg come in off a 3-0 Bundesliga win at home to SCR Altach on Sunday [8], four days before this match. The problem with that picture is the other European night: Milan beat them 4-0 in Salzburg, a result Italian media have not let go of [12]. So the favourites are both the team in form and the team with the loudest bad night behind them.

The coach's own words add a wrinkle. Danny Röhl said he will use fresh players against Celje and that he finds it hard to call the match a mandatory win [6]. Sky Sport Austria still describes the trip as a real key game on Salzburg's path [9], and Röhl is aiming for the win that pushes them towards the knockout phase [10]. Rotation and ambition in the same breath.

One goal in five, and why the xG agrees

Celje's last five read draw, loss, win, draw, loss, with a single goal scored — the 1-0 home win over Aluminij. The two European games explain the drought better than the scorelines do. Against Omonia they created 0.97 expected goals and allowed 2.12 in a 0-3 home defeat; away at Leverkusen they managed 0.9 against 1.93. They are not being shut out by luck. They are not making chances.

Salzburg's numbers are the mirror image in the league and a warning in Europe. The Altach win came with 2.94 expected goals against 0.58, and the 3-0 over Sturm Graz with 1.61 against 0.49. Even the goalless draw at Grazer AK was a match where the chances ran slightly against them, 1.18 to 1.59, and Milan generated 3.45 against them. Both teams had four days of rest, so freshness is level — only the quality of the chances is not.

Salzburg's defence and a doubtful finisher

Salzburg are without defender N. Veratschnig, who has played 673 minutes this season across 12 appearances with a 7.44 rating and two goals. Losing a regular from a defence that conceded four to Milan matters, though he was already on the injury list before this week. Austrian outlet laola1.at reports that a Salzburg striker was recently out with illness and his participation here is uncertain [7]. Separately, K. Konate is listed as doubtful with four goals in only 390 minutes — their sharpest finisher per minute; doubtful is not out, but it is not nothing. E. Aguilar, with 81 minutes this season, is also missing.

Röhl's promise of fresh players [6] means the eleven may not be the strongest available, which is a real counterweight to the favouritism. Celje are without J. Pisek, out with a knee injury. The mood in the camp, according to RTV SLO, is not despondency despite results falling short of the champions' ambitions [1], and coach Zeljković offered his own summary: form is climbed step by step but falls by lift [2].

The drought against the rotation

Everything here points the same way except one thing. Celje's problem is not the fixture, it is the finishing: two European games, no goals, under one expected goal in each. Salzburg's problem is not the opposition, it is themselves — a four-goal night at home and a coach openly planning to change the eleven. The market has weighed both and come down hard on the visitors, rating them a goal the better side and giving the home team less than a one-in-five chance. The question a fan should carry into kick-off is simple: whether Salzburg's league form travels with a rotated team, or whether a team that cannot score makes even a changed Salzburg comfortable. More likely than not, it is the second.
