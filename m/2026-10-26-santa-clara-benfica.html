A table that says close, a price that says otherwise

Eight league games, no defeats, fourth place, one point behind Benfica. Santa Clara have started the season better than anyone outside the big three, and on Monday night they host Benfica. The market's answer is blunt: Benfica are 67.4% to win, Santa Clara 11.2%.

The standings tell a different story. A Bola notes that Santa Clara cannot drop a place even if they lose, and would move above Benfica with a win [2], and describes them as under no pressure for the visit [3]. Benfica are second on 19 points with a goal difference of +17; Santa Clara are a point back with +8. The one-point gap is not the story — the margin of the wins is.

That is the tension of this match. The table says two of the league's best starters; the price says one team is far stronger. The rest of this preview is about why, and about how wide the gap really is.

Santa Clara's wall, Benfica's chances

Santa Clara's league run is five wins and three draws. The one defeat in their last five came in the cup, 2-3 at Mafra on 18 October, and it is the only recent night when they were properly opened up. In the league they allow 0.49 expected goals per game, and A Bola calls it the best defence in the Liga [1].

The other side of that record is chance creation: 1.15 xG per game. The 4-0 over Rio Ave came with 1.8; the 0-0 at home to Braga with 1.04; the 1-0 at Casa Pia with 0.61. They win small or not at all. Benfica average 2.07 xG per game, including 3.22 in the 4-1 over Celtic, and their only recent defeat, 1-3 at Porto, was also their only low-creation game at 0.84. Benfica have had 11 days since Celtic, Santa Clara eight since Mafra.

Durán, rotation and a plan for Monday

The news around Benfica is about their forwards and their schedule. Jhon Durán has a right-knee problem, did not travel to Cyprus for the Omonia game, and is expected back for Monday's trip, according to El Colombiano [4]. OneFootball reports that he is training and the problem is not considered serious [5].

Benfica travelled to Cyprus with five absences and called on B-team players, A Bola reports [7], and O Jogo says Marco Silva is set to rotate his eleven for that game with Monday in mind [6]. That is consistent with Benfica treating this league match as the priority. Fredrik Aursnes now wears the armband and has become a fan favourite, Sporten reports [9]. Cláudio Pereira has been appointed referee, with Rui Soares on VAR [10].

Favourites, but not by a rout

The win chances come from the nine bookmakers we compare, with their margin removed: Benfica 67.4%, the draw 21.0%, Santa Clara 11.2% — more than six times the home side's chance. The handicap measures the size of the expected win: Santa Clara start 1.25 goals ahead, and the fair line of 1.31 says the market rates Benfica about 1.3 goals the better team. To fully cover -1.25 Benfica need to win by two; a one-goal win only half-pays.

The history fits that line. Benfica won 2-1 on their last visit in February and are unbeaten in the last five meetings, but the last two league games at Santa Clara were each settled by a single goal. The goals line sits at 2.55, with Over 2.5 at 51.1% — three goals or more is close to a coin flip — and both teams to score is 44.1%.

The handicap line has moved out to 1.31 from 1.23 earlier in the week, so Benfica are rated a little further ahead than they were, while the goals fair line has come down from 2.62 to 2.55 and both teams to score Yes is 2.3 points down. The market makes a controlled Benfica win the more likely outcome in a game with few clear chances at either end.

The same number, a different competition

On 22 October we looked at Benfica's Europa League trip to Omonia and noted the market made them 70.4% favourites there, against 11.9% for the Cypriots. Monday's numbers are almost identical in shape: 67.4% against 11.2%. Different competition, different opponent, same view of this Benfica team.

Our read

Join the two halves and the picture is clear. Santa Clara's unbeaten start is real, and it rests on a defence that gives up almost nothing in expected goals. Benfica's case is simpler and stronger: they create about twice as much, they arrive after a long rest, and their coach has been planning around this trip while rotating elsewhere. The price and the football agree on the winner. They also agree on the shape — the handicap and the goals line both point to a narrow Benfica win, the kind of night Santa Clara have made a habit of, decided by one goal.
