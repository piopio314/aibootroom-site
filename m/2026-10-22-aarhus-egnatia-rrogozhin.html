A flat night in Gent, a very different opponent

Aarhus opened their Conference League campaign with a 0-1 defeat at Gent and almost nothing to show for it: 0.23 expected goals to Gent's 2.24. That leaves them 29th of 36 with no points, and it makes tonight's home match the fixture they are expected to take.

Egnatia arrive with the opposite feeling. Their 1-1 draw with Midtjylland at Elbasan Arena gave them their first point in a European competition [2], and Albeu.com reports they now travel to Aarhus for only their second match in the league phase [4].

The motivation is easy to see. Aarhus need a home win to get their campaign moving, while Egnatia, after the best European night in their history, are described by Newsport as looking to surprise another opponent [5].

Good results, thin chances

Aarhus' domestic run is solid enough: a 1-1 at FC Copenhagen on Sunday, a 6-1 cup win and a 3-0 home win over Sonderjyske. But the numbers under those results are flat. They average 0.91 expected goals over their last five matches, and even in the 3-0 win Sonderjyske created slightly more (1.56 to 1.47). At Copenhagen they made 0.94 to the home side's 2.01.

Egnatia have won three of their last five, most recently 3-1 at home to Vllaznia on Sunday, as reported by Shkodra Web [3]. Their historic point against Midtjylland came with 0.59 expected goals to 1.37, so they were out-created on the night. Both teams played on Sunday and both have four days' rest, which means freshness is not part of Aarhus' advantage here.

Dede: the schedule is not an excuse

Egnatia coach Nevil Dede said before the match that the fixture congestion does not worry his team and that they are ready for the challenge [1]. The calendar backs him up: his side had the same four-day turnaround as Aarhus.

What Egnatia bring is the habit of a goal: they scored in every win in their last five and, according to albeu.com [2], took a point off Midtjylland with only 0.59 expected goals.

What the prices say about the gap

The fair probabilities make Aarhus 64.7% to win, the draw 21.2% and Egnatia 14.1% — the Albanian side is rated more than four times less likely to take the three points. The handicap tells the same story in a different way: the main line is -1.25 and the fair line has moved from -0.99 to -1.14. In plain terms, Aarhus are rated about a goal better than Egnatia: the fair line has moved from -0.99 to -1.14.

The win chance has risen by 3.3 points to 64.7%, and the handicap moved four points in the same direction. That is consistent with confidence in the venue and the difference in level rather than in anything Aarhus showed in Gent. The goals line sits at 2.75, with Over at 50.8% and the fair total moving from 2.64 to 2.78 — just under three goals, and only a fraction above a coin flip for Over. Both teams to score is rated 51.5%.
