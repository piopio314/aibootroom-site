Two wins from two, against a team in trouble

Beşiktaş have won both of their Europa League matches, scored seven goals and sit fourth in the 36-team table. On Thursday they host a Crystal Palace side that lost 5-0 at Brighton on Sunday and conceded three times in the first ten minutes [8]. That is the shape of this match: the team in form at home against the team in trouble away.

Palace are 15th with three points from two games, one win and one defeat — 4-0 over Lech Poznan, then 3-2 at Lyon [9]. BBC Sport reports that Pierre Sage is fighting to keep his job but will be given more time [7]. Even so, Sage spoke before the game about scoring first and changing Beşiktaş's plan, and said his side know about the home team's attacking quality [10].

For Beşiktaş the aim is simple: a third win from three would give them nine points from nine. The complication is a domestic defeat three days ago and a midfield missing its most-used player.

One bad Monday, one very good European run

Beşiktaş lost 0-2 at Trabzonspor on Monday, and the chances say it was a bad night: 1.32 xG created against 2.41 allowed. Their European form is a different story. They beat Marseille 4-1 at home and won 3-2 at Hoffenheim, where they created 3.39 xG. Across their last five matches they average 2.24 xG created and 1.71 allowed.

Palace's numbers point the other way. They have conceded chances worth 2.17 xG per game across their last five, and the Brighton collapse fits that pattern. Even the 3-1 home win over Nottingham Forest came with 3.34 xG allowed. Palace have had one more day to rest, four against Beşiktaş's three, but that is a small comfort next to those defensive numbers.

Kökçü out, and a Palace back line patched together

Beşiktaş are without Orkun Kökçü. The midfielder has played 701 minutes this season, with two goals, four assists and a rating of 7.31. Turkish media report he misses this game through injury [2], and Vincenzo Italiano confirmed at his press conference that he will change the line-up and named who comes in [1]. Milot Rashica is also out, but he has played only 21 minutes this season, so that absence is small.

Palace's problems are at the back. Riad is out with a knee injury, Chilwell and Tomiyasu are doubtful, and The Athletic reports the club lost both international centre-halves, Marc Guehi and Maxence Lacroix, in 2026 and replaced them with loanees and youngsters [12]. That is consistent with a defence that has been giving up good chances all season.

Favourite, but only by about a third of a goal

The win chances, taken from the ten bookmakers we compare and with the margin removed, are Beşiktaş 46.4%, draw 24.0%, Palace 29.6%. The home side is the favourite, though not a majority, and their chance is about one and a half times Palace's. The handicap line is -0.25 and the fair line sits at -0.37: both say the market rates Beşiktaş a little under half a goal better. That is a close game in Istanbul, not a rout.

The goals line is 3.0, with Over at 53.0%, up 2.1 points in recent days, and the fair line pushed from 3.04 to 3.12. Palace allow 2.17 xG per game across their last five, which supports the lean. Both teams to score is rated more likely than not at 63.6%, with Palace's 2.17 xG conceded per game and Beşiktaş's 2.24 created both pointing the same way.

What we said before Trabzonspor

On 19 October we previewed Beşiktaş's trip to Trabzonspor and called it a derby the market could not split: 38.1% home, 37.1% away. Trabzonspor won 2-0, so the narrow home favourite came in. We also leaned towards goals on a 3.25 line, and with only two scored that part went the other way. Our note then was to treat Beşiktaş as close to a coin-flip away from home; at home in Europe they are clearly the favourite, so that note does not carry over here.

Same direction, smaller size

The football and the market point the same way without agreeing on the margin. Beşiktaş have the better European record, the home crowd and a Palace defence that gives up good chances; Palace have a manager under pressure, a patched back line and one extra day of rest. The prices say the same in quieter words: 46.4% for the home win, a home edge worth about a third of a goal, and a goals line above three. A Beşiktaş win is the more likely result. A comfortable one is not.
