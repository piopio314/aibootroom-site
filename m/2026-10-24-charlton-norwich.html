Two cold teams, one lunchtime kick-off

Charlton host Norwich at The Valley on Saturday lunchtime, and both sides arrive with a run they badly want to end. Norwich have lost their last six Championship games [7]. Charlton have not won in eight [1]. A draw would leave both still stuck, which is part of what makes this game awkward to read.

The table says the home side should be the happier. Charlton are 17th with 14 points from 11 games; Norwich are 21st with 9, in the bottom four and five points behind the team they are favourites to beat. That gap is the tension of the afternoon: the league position points one way, the market points the other.

Both managers are under pressure. BBC Sport reports that the sixth straight defeat piled pressure on Norwich boss Philippe Clement [8], and The Sentinel describes the trip to Charlton as one of a big few games coming up for him [9]. Nathan Jones did not protect his own players after the Watford loss, calling the performance inexcusable [2].

Charlton draw, Norwich sink

Charlton's last five read LDDLD — three draws, two defeats, no win. The chance numbers are not disastrous: they average 1.46 expected goals a game and 1.68 against. They created 2.13 xG at Burnley a week ago and came away with a 2-2 draw after Ivan Mesik equalised late [4]. Against Watford they gave up 2.87 xG and lost 0-2. The pattern is a side that stays in games but cannot finish them.

Norwich's problem is at the other end. Across their last five league matches they average 0.73 expected goals — under one clear chance-load per game — and 1.70 against. They conceded 13 goals in those five, and in the 0-1 home defeat to Portsmouth they managed just 0.37 xG. That is why the results do not look like bad luck: the chance numbers agree with them. Both teams have had seven days since their last match, so neither side arrives with a rest advantage.

Norwich lose their spine

The team news makes Norwich's afternoon harder, not easier. Top scorer Toure is out with a shoulder injury after 536 minutes and six goals this season — most of what a low-scoring side has produced. Goalkeeper Kovacevic, who has played 909 minutes, is out with concussion, and midfielder McLean (809 minutes, a 7.23 rating) is also missing. That is the middle and the back of the team, not the fringes.

Clement said this week he is not alarmed by the attacking nosedive [10], even as the chance numbers keep falling. Charlton's list is shorter: Chalobah is doubtful with a muscle injury after 317 minutes and a 7.08 rating, and Godden is also a doubt. The home side is close to full strength, which is one reason the market cannot pull the two teams far apart.

Favourites by a third of a goal

Across the ten bookmakers we compare, Norwich are 44.5% to win at The Valley, Charlton 29.1% and the draw 26.4%. Those chances have the margin removed. The draw price matters here: it is more than a quarter of the outcome, which fits a game between two teams who have forgotten how to win. In handicap terms the market rates Norwich only +0.31 goals better — about a third of a goal — so a single goal separates them, not a class gap.

The goals line is priced almost level: over 2.5 goals is a 51.0% chance and both teams to score 55.1%. Norwich conceding 13 in five games pushes the total up; their 0.73 xG pushes it down. The two cancel out. Norwich's win chance has moved just under one point this week and Charlton's is up 0.7 — small shifts, consistent with a market weighing the fixture history against the losing run rather than reacting hard to Saturday's result.

Favourites on the fixture, not on the form

Norwich have won the last three meetings between these clubs, all by a single goal, including 0-1 at The Valley in March. That record is what keeps them ahead in the price while they lose every week, and it is why the gap is only a third of a goal. Take the head-to-head away and there is very little between a team winless in eight and a team beaten six times running.

So the read is narrow. Norwich are more likely than not to win, but only just, and they do it without their top scorer and with the weakest attack of the two on recent chance numbers. Charlton, at home and nearly full strength, have the draw in a quarter of the outcomes and a manager asking for a response. The team that wins probably wins by one goal — and the team that loses will have a longer week than it already had.
