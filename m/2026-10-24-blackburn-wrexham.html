Two mid-table sides, one point apart

Blackburn are 10th in the Championship with 15 points from 11 games; Wrexham are 14th with 14. One point and four places sit between them, and Wrexham are the narrow favourites at Ewood Park on Saturday afternoon — 36.0% to Blackburn's 34.5%, with the draw at 29.5%.

The two most recent results point in different directions. Wrexham beat Preston 4-1 at home last weekend [4]. Blackburn went to West Bromwich Albion, led 2-0, and lost 3-2 [1]. One side finished the week scoring four; the other finished it giving away a lead it had earned.

That is why the away side is a fraction ahead. Wrexham carry a patched-up squad and a manager who has had a noisy month, but they also carry the better weekend behind them. Blackburn have home advantage and two straight home wins, and the market still does not make them favourites.

Blackburn score, then let opponents back in

Blackburn's last five league games brought three wins, a draw and a defeat, and 10 goals scored. The xG tells a less comfortable story: they created 1.05 expected goals per game in that run and allowed 2.0. The 2-1 win over Cardiff was the one game where the performance matched the result, with 2.42 xG created.

Wrexham's run is more mixed. The 4-1 over Preston came with 1.97 xG against 0.79, their best attacking display of the month. But the same five games include a 0-6 defeat at West Ham and a 1-2 home loss to West Brom in which they created 0.38 xG. Their numbers allowed sit at 1.47 per game, better than Blackburn's. Both clubs have had a full seven days since their last match.

Wrexham down to seven, with one close to returning

Yahoo Sports reports Wrexham have seven players sidelined [8]. Phil Parkinson called it unprecedented and probably the worst spell in his time at the club, with Max Cleworth, Nathan Broadhead and Dan Sheaf among those out, per BBC [5].

There is some relief: Ben Sheaf is set to return to full training and could be available on Saturday, according to LeaderLive [7]. The Athletic reported that Parkinson's future had been under close scrutiny before the Preston win brought him breathing space [6]. Blackburn have no comparable list of absentees to report, and come into the game off a full week's rest.

The market's level game, and the reason for it

The through-line is simple. The market sees two mid-table sides of equal weight, and the football supports it: Blackburn create less than they concede, Wrexham travel with a squad stretched to its limit. One side's problem is structural, the other's is temporary, and the two roughly cancel out. That is why the away win is narrowly favoured and why the handicap gives nobody a goal. The likeliest story is an open game with goals, decided by which defence holds up longer — and Blackburn's has been the one letting games slip.
