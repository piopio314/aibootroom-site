One point apart, two different moods

Genk and Standard Liege sit one point apart in the Pro League table — Genk seventh on 12 points, Standard ninth on 11 — but they come to the Cegeka Arena in very different states. Genk won their last home league match 3-0. Standard have lost their last three league games.

That run is the centre of Sunday's story: 0-1 at home to Charleroi, 1-2 at Gent, 2-4 at Westerlo. The 2-0 cup win over Dender stopped the run of defeats, but Belgian media were not calmed by it. rtbf.be described the performance as unconvincing [3], and lavenir.net reported that the display raised psychological questions around the team [4].

So the hosts have the results and the home ground, and the visitors have a league record that looks worse than their football. That space between results and performances is what makes this match more interesting than a simple seventh-against-ninth.

Genk's solid numbers, Standard's better chances

Genk average 1.27 expected goals across their last five and allow 1.09. The 3-0 against Kortrijk on 10 October was their best home display of that run: 1.78 xG to 0.65 against. Even the 0-3 defeat at Club Brugge came with 1.96 xG, so the scoreline there was harsher than the play. The 0-0 at Anderlecht, with just 0.4 xG, was the one genuinely quiet afternoon.

Standard's numbers point the other way. They average 1.81 xG over their last five — more than Genk's 1.27 — and 1.29 against, and they found the net in four of those five games. Both teams had cup ties last week, so the rest is close: eight days for Genk, nine for Standard. The table says Standard are one point behind; the chance data says they are not the weaker team.

Nielsen a doubt, and a Genk homecoming

Standard's main concern is midfielder Casper Nielsen, who is uncertain for Sunday after a Dender defender stepped on his foot and it swelled up. voetbalkrant.com reports he could miss the trip [1], and footnews.be says Standard could be forced to do without him [2]. A regular midfielder matters most in a game where the visitors will spend long spells without the ball, chasing and covering.

Genk have no absences listed for this match. The news around them is happier: the club has organised a return to the Cegeka Arena for Mike Origi and his son Divock around Sunday's game [6], and a recently retired former Red Devil will give the kick-off [7]. Standard also had one small positive from the cup tie — Dimitri Lavalée played his first minutes of the season with the first team after a long spell out [5].

Three-quarters of a goal, and goals at both ends

Genk are 54.2% to win, the draw 25.7% and Standard 20.1% — the home side is more than two and a half times more likely than the visitor. The handicap explains the size of that edge in plain terms: Genk are rated about three-quarters of a goal better, with the fair line at -0.71. A single-goal Genk win is roughly the expected margin, so the market sees a controlled home win rather than a rout.

The goals market sits at 3.0, with Over at 49.7% — close to a coin flip on three goals or more — and both teams to score at 59.5%, more likely than not. Standard scoring six goals across their last five league games fits that picture.

Favourites, not certainties

Genk deserve to be favourites: they are at home, they won 3-0 in their last league game here, and Standard have lost three league matches in a row. But the football underneath the price is not one-sided. Standard create more per game than Genk, and their record at this stadium is good — a 3-0 win in February, then a 1-1 and a 0-0 in the two meetings since. A Genk win is more likely than not, by about three-quarters of a goal, in a game the market expects to bring goals at both ends.
