Two dugouts, one favourite

Selhurst Park gets a match between two teams in poor form and two managers who have both spent the week in the news. Crystal Palace lost 0-5 at Brighton on Sunday and were three goals down inside ten minutes [2]. Newcastle have lost their last two league games: 1-4 at Coventry, then 0-1 at home to Aston Villa.

For Palace the pressure is immediate. BBC Sport reports that Pierre Sage is fighting to keep his job but will be given more time [1], and The Athletic reports that the club does not plan an immediate change and expects him to take charge on Sunday [3]. The Standard reports that Sage knows results must change, with Besiktas and then Newcastle in the same week [6].

Newcastle's week has been quieter, but not calm. The Athletic reports pressure on Matthias Jaissle over his tactics and the club's form [7], and there is a prize on Sunday: Chronicle Live notes that a win at Selhurst Park would lift Newcastle into a European place [10]. A home game against a mid-table rival is exactly what a struggling manager wants. The market, even so, does not see it that way.

The numbers behind two bad runs

Palace's run reads worse than it partly was. The 0-5 at Brighton came with 0.77 expected goals created and 2.0 allowed: the chances given up were bad, and the finishing against them was brutal. Their one recent league win, 3-1 at home to Nottingham Forest, came with 2.41 xG created and 3.34 allowed, so the result was better than the performance. Across their last five games they average 1.45 xG for and 2.17 against, and they sit 14th with seven points and a goal difference of -8.

Newcastle's numbers are mixed rather than flattering: they created 1.84 expected goals at Coventry and lost 1-4, and managed 1.07 at home to Villa. They have scored five goals in their last five games and allowed 1.69 xG per game. They sit 11th with eight points, one point and three places above Palace.

A rebuilt defence and a meeting of senior players

The bigger Palace issue is structural. The Athletic reports that Palace lost centre-backs Marc Guehi and Maxence Lacroix during 2026 and replaced them with loanees and youngsters, with an obvious drop in quality [4]. That is the football reason they are outsiders at home: nine goals conceded in their last five games, and a back line still finding its level. Left-back Tyrick Mitchell has urged the team to get "back to basics" [5].

On the Newcastle side, Jaissle has given hints on the fitness of Dan Burn and William Osula [8] and has met his senior players to ask for feedback on his "vertical football" and whether they are comfortable with its demands [9]. Chronicle Live reports he is relishing the pressure and weighing a tactical change [11]. A LiveScore opinion piece argues the problem runs deeper, noting that only Aston Villa and Coventry have won the ball back in the final third less than Newcastle this season [12].

A quarter of a goal, and a line of three

The chances below come from the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed. Newcastle are 40.8% to win, Palace 32.7% and the draw 26.5%. That is a clear lean to the visiting team at a ground where Palace have been strong against them: they beat Newcastle 2-1 at Selhurst Park in April and are unbeaten in their last three home meetings. The price says Palace's current defending counts for more than that record.

On the handicap, Palace receive +0.25 and the fair line has moved from 0.04 to 0.17 goals, so Newcastle are rated a small fraction of a goal the better side. Newcastle's win chance has risen 3.3 points in recent days while Palace's fell 3.1 — a move consistent with the reaction to the Brighton result rather than to Newcastle's own defeat. The goals line sits at 3.0, with over at 48.3% and both teams to score at 62.0%: the market expects an open game, not a careful one.

What we said about Palace this week

Earlier this week we wrote about Palace's Europa League trip to Besiktas and described them the same way: a team just beaten 0-5 at Brighton, a manager under pressure, and a price that made them clear outsiders at 29.6% against Besiktas' 46.4%. Sunday's line follows that same logic rather than reversing it. The market has not softened on Palace after the Brighton result; it has moved a little further away from them.

Our read

Put the two sides together and the picture is uncomfortable for the home team, but not complicated. Palace's problem is the one a manager cannot fix quickly: a rebuilt defence that has conceded nine goals in five games. Newcastle's problem is finishing and defending in moments, not chance creation — the 1.84 expected goals at Coventry are why they arrive as 40.8% favourites after two straight defeats. The line of 3.0 goals fits both teams: two leaky defences, and a Palace side that has to attack in front of its own crowd. Our read is a Newcastle advantage of about a quarter of a goal, an open game, and a home crowd with very little patience left.
