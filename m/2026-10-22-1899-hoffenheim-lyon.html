A table that says one thing, a ground that says another

Two matches into their Europa League campaign, Hoffenheim have nothing to show: 0 points and 30th place out of 36 teams. On Thursday they host the side top of Ligue 1 and perfect in Europe. It is the kind of night where the competition table and the home ground pull in opposite directions.

Hoffenheim coach Christian Ilzer has warned his players about the threat the French team carries [5]. The situation is simple. Another defeat leaves the German club far from the knockout places, while Lyon can stay unbeaten in the Europa League league phase and keep their route to the next round clean.

Both teams arrive off a win. Hoffenheim ended a four-game losing run by beating Borussia Mönchengladbach 2-1 away on Sunday [3]. Lyon beat Nice 2-1 at home and moved top of Ligue 1 on goal difference, and they did it with ten men [7][8]. Neither side had a quiet weekend.

Hoffenheim's results are worse than their chances

The home side's run reads WLLLL, but the last entry changed the mood. At Gladbach they created 1.8 expected goals and allowed 0.47, the largest xG margin in their last five matches. Before that came a 2-3 home defeat to Beşiktaş in which they allowed 3.39 xG, a 2-3 home loss to Hamburger SV, and a 1-3 defeat at Paderborn where they actually out-created the opponent, 1.5 to 0.68, and still lost. Their season averages, 1.36 xG for and 1.46 against, describe a tight team; the 12 goals they have conceded in five games describe a looser one.

Lyon's run is WWLWW with 1.82 xG created per game. The Nice win was level on chances, 1.6 to 1.61, and they still found a way through a man down [9]. Their only defeat in that stretch came at Lens, 1-2, when they created less than they allowed. Both squads had four days between their weekend match and this one, so freshness is not a factor for either.

Wimmer out, Openda out — two attacks thinned

Hoffenheim are confirmed without Albian Hajdari (447 minutes this season), Patrick Wimmer (344 minutes, two goals and an assist from midfield) and Nathan De Cat [1]. Wimmer is the one that hurts: a goalscoring midfield option is exactly what a team chasing its first European point needs. The better news, reported by fussballnationalmannschaft.net, is that Kabak and Bernardo are back available and Lemperle is set to start up front [2]. Extra bodies at the back matter against a Lyon attack that has scored in all of their last five games.

Lyon's absence list is headed by Loïs Openda, with 823 minutes this season — the heaviest workload of anyone missing from either squad — plus two goals and an assist. A leg injury removes him from the trip. Ouedraogo (220 minutes) and Tagliafico (136) are also out, both defenders. Ernest Nuamah is reported by Le Progrès to be hoping for an important role against Hoffenheim [10].

Our read: a close night with goals in it

Hoffenheim's favouritism is not a quirk. It rests on home advantage, the Gladbach performance where they barely allowed a chance, and a Lyon squad travelling without its most-used attacker. But the zero points are real too, and so is that 3.39 xG allowed at home to Beşiktaş — the same defence now faces a team that has scored in every recent match. Lyon's habit of winning tight games, even a man down, keeps their chance live rather than decorative. The football and the numbers meet in the same place: Hoffenheim are the better side by roughly half a goal, the game should produce chances at both ends, and a first European point for the home team is more likely than not — but not by much.
