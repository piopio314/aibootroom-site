Fourth against thirteenth, with a warning from history

Sunday at Het Kasteel puts the Eredivisie's fourth-placed team against its thirteenth, and the table gap is as wide as that sounds. Ajax have 18 points from nine games, exactly double Sparta Rotterdam's nine, with a goal difference of +15 against -6. Ajax also arrive from a 5-2 win at Groningen on Sunday [6].

Sparta arrive from something rarer: a win. They beat Willem II 3-2 at home last Saturday, with Jens Toornstra scoring one goal and making another [1]. That ended a run of five games without a victory, a stretch OneFootball's preview said Sparta had been trying to halt [2]. The defence, though, has not been fixed — 15 goals conceded in five league games, including a 0-4 home defeat to Heerenveen.

Het Kasteel has produced goals rather than clear away days: the last meeting there finished 3-3 in October 2025 and the one before it 2-2 in March 2024, though Ajax did win 0-2 there in December 2024 and won 4-0 in Amsterdam in March.

Sparta's problem is at the back, not in build-up

Ajax's last five games in league and Europe read W-L-D-D-W, with 14 goals scored and eight conceded, and the chance numbers agree: 2.45 xG created per game, 1.55 allowed. The 5-2 at Groningen was not a burst of lucky finishing — Ajax created 3.04 xG there and still gave up 2.29 at the other end. They have now scored five in each of their last two league wins.

Sparta's numbers explain their position better than the results do. They average 2.24 xG created across their last five, which is healthy, but 1.91 conceded, and the heavy defeats to Heerenveen and PSV were built on chances they could not stop. Sparta have had eight days since beating Willem II; Ajax seven since Groningen, so freshness is not an issue for either side.

Ajax's good news arrives a week late

Ajax's week brought encouraging news of a limited kind. Daley Blind and Steven Berghuis are back on the training pitch after weeks out, and coach Míchel hopes to have them available next week, according to NOS [5]. That makes this trip to Rotterdam a game too early for both — and Ajax scored five at Groningen without them. Míchel also defended Jofre Torrents after the defeat at Hajduk Split, saying the full-back had not played badly [7].

Sparta's news is quieter. Toornstra's performance against Willem II is the obvious positive [1]. Away from the pitch, livescore.com reports that Milan Kostic has had a difficult start to the season and that there is speculation he could change clubs in January [3]. Valentijn Driessen, writing on twenteinsite.nl, rates Sparta striker Milan Zonneveld as a candidate to become the new Wout Weghorst [4].

A one-goal favourite in an open game

The win chances, with the bookmakers' margin removed and taken from the 10 bookmakers we compare, are Ajax 61.5%, the draw 20.0% and Sparta 18.6%. Ajax are therefore more than three times as likely to win as the home side. The handicap explains how: Sparta start with a one-goal head start, the 50/50 point sitting at Ajax by roughly 1.05 goals. In plain words, a single-goal away win is the central expectation, not a rout.

The goals expectation points the same way but adds a twist. Over 3.5 goals is at 49.3% and both teams to score at 65.0%, which describes an open afternoon rather than a controlled one. Sparta's last five matches alone have produced 24 goals, and Ajax have twice scored five in a league game this season, so the goals expectation fits the form on both sides. With Blind and Berghuis still a week away, according to NOS [5], there is little to suggest a quieter game.

What we said earlier this week

Earlier this week we wrote about Ajax's European night against Atalanta, when the market barely separated the sides at 44.7% to 30.1% after the 1-2 defeat at Hajduk Split. That read described a club wobbling in Europe. The league picture is different: the Ajax that turns up at Het Kasteel is the one that has scored five in each of its last two Eredivisie wins, and the Split defeat sits between two of those afternoons.

Clear favourites, but not a walkover

Ajax are clear favourites and the football explains why: twice Sparta's points, a goal difference of +15 against -6, and a 5-2 win at Groningen on Sunday [6]. But the market's own totals keep this from being a simple away day — a one-goal handicap means a single-goal Ajax win is the central expectation, and with both teams to score at 65.0% Sparta are expected to find the net. Sparta create enough to make that plausible; what they have not done in five games is defend it. That is the game: Ajax's chances against Sparta's, with both sides likely to take theirs.
