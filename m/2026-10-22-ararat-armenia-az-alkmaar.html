Two games, no points, and AZ next

Ararat-Armenia have played two Europa League matches and have nothing to show for them. A 1-3 defeat at Jagiellonia was followed by a 1-4 home loss to Sparta Praha, and the table now shows them 34th of 36 with no points and a goal difference of -5. On Thursday, AZ Alkmaar come to Yerevan.

AZ sit 20th with three points from two games: a 0-1 loss at Sunderland, then a 2-1 home win over Hapoel Beer Sheva. ClaroSports reports they are chasing a second win of the campaign [3], and Spanish-language previews frame the night as matchday three with no margin for error [1]. That is true for both clubs, but it is far more urgent for the hosts.

The gap in level is the story of the night. Ararat are not falling apart at home — they won their domestic game on Sunday — but Europe has been a different test, and the market has noticed.

Ararat are winning at home, just not at this level

Ararat beat BKMA 1-0 away on Sunday, a second win in five matches. That is a side in normal health, not a crisis. The problem is the step up: at Jagiellonia they conceded 2.1 expected goals in a 1-3 defeat, and at home to Sparta Praha they lost 1-4.

AZ's last five read DWLWL — a 0-2 defeat at Feyenoord, then a 2-2 draw at Cambuur on Sunday. Even so, the chance numbers hold up: 1.84 xG for and 1.57 against across those five games, and a 2.75 to 1.09 split in the Hapoel win. In the Cambuur draw they also edged the chances, 1.56 to 1.53. Both teams had four days between matches, so rest is not a difference.

AZ's midfield is the one soft spot

AZ are without midfielder K. Smit, who has played 402 minutes this season with two assists and a 7.03 rating — a regular, not a reserve. J. Clasie is doubtful with a knock after 395 minutes of his own. If Clasie joins Smit on the sidelines, AZ lose both of their most-used midfield options, and control of the game has to come from a reshuffled middle. Striker J. Hornkamp is also out, with two goals from only 83 minutes.

For Ararat, Z. Banjaqui is doubtful with an injury after 203 minutes, one goal and one assist, and P. Ayongo is off the roster. Mateo Chávez is in AZ's squad for the trip, according to ClaroSports [2]. Patrik Kolaric will referee the match, according to RPP [5]. Off the pitch, Armenian outlet Sportaran reported that the FFA refereeing committee chairman worked for a company linked to Ararat-Armenia's owners [4].

Our read

Ararat's problem is not form but level. They keep winning the games they should win at home, then meet sides who create twice as much as them in Europe, and the scoreline follows. AZ are that kind of side: not on a hot streak, but creating more than they allow in almost every match, with a midfield that may be thinner than usual on Thursday.

So the picture is a fair one. AZ are the stronger team by about a goal, Ararat's head start keeps the result close, and the small drift towards AZ winning by more than one matches what AZ have shown in this competition. The one thing that could pull it back towards the hosts is that missing midfield. AZ are clear favourites — but this is a night where the margin, not the winner, is the question.
