Four days after LASK

Sporting lost at home to LASK on Tuesday, their first defeat of the season, with the visitors winning 2-3 in the Champions League [1][3]. Rui Borges said afterwards that his side lacked inspiration and that he wanted a fast answer [2]. He pointed at Sunday's visit of Académico de Viseu, a game he said Sporting badly want to win for their main objective, the league [2].

The domestic table tells a different story from that European night. Sporting are third with 18 points from eight games and are unbeaten: five wins, three draws. Nobody in the Primeira Liga has beaten them this season, and that record, not Tuesday's result, is what the market is reading.

Viseu sit seventh with 14 points and had won four league games in a row before their week went wrong. They lost 1-0 away to fourth-tier Fátima in the Taça de Portugal [7], a match Record described as a struggle to build play against counter-attacks [8]. So the visitors bring good league form and a fresh bruise.

Wins that run ahead of the xG

Sporting's last five games are a mixed picture. They created 3.2 expected goals against LASK and still lost. They also won 3-1 at Braga with only 0.95 xG, and drew 2-2 at home with Arouca while allowing 2.08. Over that run they average 1.88 xG for and 1.36 against, so the chances are there at both ends.

Viseu's four league wins came with 1.36 xG for and 1.54 against per game. They scored seven goals in five matches, but they allowed better chances than they made: 1-0 at Gil Vicente on 0.33 xG, 2-1 at home to Estoril on 0.73. Those are wins built on small margins. They have had seven days since their last match; Sporting had four and played a Champions League night on Tuesday.

Borges' open question

Both squads come through the week without a confirmed absentee. The one open question is on the Sporting side: four days ago Rui Borges said he hoped to have an injured player available in time for this match, according to leonino.pt [4]. No name was given, so the shape of his team stays open.

Miguel Nogueira has been appointed referee for the game, leonino.pt reported [5], and the same outlet noted he had previously been criticised for not spotting a bad foul by Veiga on Hjulmand [6]. For Viseu the news that matters more is the cup exit, where Record reported they struggled to build play and were beaten by Fátima's counter-attacks [8].

76.2% and a quarter-line move

Sporting are 76.2% to win, the draw 15.3% and Viseu 8.6%, with the margin removed across the 10 bookmakers we compare. That makes Sporting nearly nine times more likely than Viseu to take the three points. The handicap sits at -1.75, with the fair line at -1.81 — the market rates Sporting roughly 1.8 goals the better side. The goal line is 3.25, with Over 3.25 at 49.7% and both teams to score at 49.3% — close to a coin flip for a high-scoring night, but not a guarantee.

Market probabilities shifted toward Viseu in recent days. Sporting's win probability is 4.0 points lower, the fair handicap line moved from -2.06 to -1.81 — a quarter of a goal toward the away side — and both teams to score rose 3.4 points. The goals fair line barely moved, from 3.32 to 3.24.
