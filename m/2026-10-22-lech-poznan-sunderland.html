Bottom of 36, and a 6-1 win

Lech Poznan are 36th of 36 in the Europa League. Two games, two defeats, both by 0-4, and a goal difference of -8 [1]. Sunderland arrive at the Enea Stadion with six points from two European matches and a place in the top half of the table. On the plain numbers, this should be the calmest away night of their autumn.

Football rarely works that way. On Sunday Lech beat Korona Kielce 6-1 at home, and they have scored 15 goals in their last five matches. Sunderland have lost three Premier League games in a row, the latest 0-2 at Bournemouth, a match they played much of with ten men [7].

So Thursday brings two different kinds of trouble together. Lech's problem is the level of the opponent: Sport.pl reports that against Crystal Palace and Bayer Leverkusen they lost both games 0-4 [1]. Sunderland's problem is confidence and a manager under scrutiny — Football Insider reports that Regis Le Bris is under pressure after the league run [6]. The market still makes the visitors clear favourites.

Lech score at home; Sunderland create in Europe

Lech's attacking record is not a fluke of finishing. They scored six, five and four in three of their last five games, and an expected goals average of 2.23 across that run says the chances are being built, not just converted. Patrik Walemark, the Sweden winger, is reported to be their main threat [5]. The other side of it: an expected goals against average of 2.07 in the same five games, and a home night against Leverkusen that finished 0-4 with a chance count of 0.57 to 5.22.

Sunderland's numbers split the same way. In Europe they created 1.82 expected goals at Torreense and 2.9 at home to AZ Alkmaar. In the league they created 0.13 at Bournemouth — their lowest of the last five — and 0.72 at home to Brighton. Both teams had four days since their Sunday match, so freshness is level.

A missing forward, a patched-up defence

Lech's attack is thinner than their form suggests. Sayyadmanesh, the forward with five goals this season, is out with a groin injury, and defender Alex Douglas is missing with an ankle problem [2]. Monka, a defender with 1,125 minutes this season, is doubtful. Losing a five-goal forward is a specific cost for a team that has to score to stay in the game.

Sunderland's list is longer. Chronicle Live reports that up to eight players could miss the trip, with Dan Ballard and Wilson Isidor rated doubtful [8]. Ballard has played 488 minutes this season, so the defence is the area most affected. Football Insider also reports that the players remain clear in their backing of Le Bris [10], and he has spoken to the media before the match [9] — a manager handling noise while his side is two wins from two in Europe.
