Top against bottom in Brussels

Eight matchdays into the Jupiler Pro League, the table has a clear shape at both ends. Union St. Gilloise are fourth with 20 points from eight games: six wins, two draws, no defeats, and a goal difference of plus 18. Cercle Brugge are 18th of 18 with three points, no win and a goal difference of minus seven.

So Sunday afternoon in Brussels looks simple. It is not quite. Cercle won 1-3 at Union in October 2024, and they drew 1-1 with Union in Bruges last December. Union did win the meeting in between, 2-0 at home in November 2025, but the recent history is closer than the 17-point gap suggests.

The situation is different for each side. Union are unbeaten and want to stay near the top of a league where the leading group is tight. Cercle need one win to change the mood of a season that has started badly, and they have already shown they can take points in this fixture.

Union control games, Cercle lose them

Union's last five across all competitions read WDDWW: ten goals scored, two conceded. Their expected goals average is 2.18 for and 0.84 against, which describes a team that creates a lot and gives up very little. Even so, two of those five were draws — the 1-1 with Real Sociedad, where they out-created the Spanish side 1.92 to 0.76, and a 1-1 at home to OH Leuven, where the chance count was level at 1.29 to 1.35. Union can dominate and still draw.

Cercle's run is WLLDL, and their only win came in the cup at Sporting Hasselt. They have conceded ten goals in five matches, including 0-3 at home to Gent and 2-3 away at Charleroi. But their chance numbers are not those of a bottom club: 1.21 xG created, 1.25 allowed, and at Charleroi they created 2.08. They are losing close games rather than being overrun. Cercle have had nine days since their last match, two more than Union.

Depth tested, no confirmed absences

Union's squad was stretched a week ago. Yahoo Sports reported [2] that they were missing ten players for the Europa League tie with Real Sociedad, and that game still finished 1-1 with Union creating almost two expected goals. David Hubert, their coach according to Goal.com [3], has had a week to get players back before this one.

David Hubert's real problem sits at the other end of the table: Cercle have conceded ten goals in five games, pointing to a defence that keeps being opened in the same way, and a trip to the league's most settled home side is the hardest place to fix it. Union, by contrast, have conceded twice in their last five.

A goal and a half, and goals at both ends

Across the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Union are 72.9% to win, the draw is 16.1% and Cercle are 11.0% — better than seven games in ten for the home side. The handicap line sits at Union -1.5, with the fair line at -1.57 goals: the market rates Union roughly a goal and a half the stronger team, so a single-goal home win would not be enough to settle it.

The goals line is 3.25, with Over at 49.3%, and both teams to score is 53.5%. That is a high total next to a 1.5-goal favourite: the market expects Cercle to score, not simply to defend. The week's moves have been small and all in the same direction — Union's win chance up 0.8 points, the -1.5 handicap up 0.9, Over 3.25 up 0.4. A slight firming toward the favourite, consistent with an unbeaten side at home against the bottom club.

We called them 71.1% favourites that night, a similar number to the 72.9% here. Our read

Everything points one way. Union are unbeaten, they concede almost nothing, and they create more than two expected goals a game; Cercle are bottom, winless, and have shipped ten goals in five. The probabilities say the same thing: Union have a 72.9% chance of winning, and they are rated about a goal and a half stronger. The one thing that keeps this from being a formality is the way Cercle keep scoring — four of their last five games produced goals for them, which is why the match is expected to produce 3.25 goals rather than fewer. Union are the clear favourites; the open question is by how many, and whether Cercle's habit of finding the net in defeat makes the afternoon less comfortable than the table suggests.
