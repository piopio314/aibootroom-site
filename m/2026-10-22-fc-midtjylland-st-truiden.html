A derby win, three days before Europe

Midtjylland play their second Conference League match of the league phase on Thursday, three days after winning the Jutland derby 2-1 at Viborg [1]. That win took them level on points with FC Copenhagen at the top of the Danish Superliga and stretched their unbeaten run to five matches. The cost is legs: it was their third game in eleven days, and the squad news from the club this week has not been good.

The visitors are St. Truiden, a Belgian club in their first European group campaign [6], and on the early league-phase table they sit above their hosts. The Belgians are 6th with three points after a 2-0 win over Iberia 1999; Midtjylland are 16th with one, from the 1-1 draw at Egnatia in Albania [3].

The market reads the night differently. Midtjylland are 56.7% to win, St. Truiden 19.7% — nearly three times the visitors' chance. That is the tension here: the better results belong to the home side, the better chance creation to the away side.

Results say one thing, xG says another

Midtjylland's five-match line is three wins and two draws, ten goals scored and five conceded. The expected goals tell a quieter story: 1.25 created and 1.16 allowed per game, and in Monday's derby they were out-created 0.84 to 1.56. They won it anyway, as they won 2-1 at home to Copenhagen in a match where the chances were almost level.

St. Truiden's run reads WWLLL — three straight league defeats, two of them 0-2, before wins over Iberia 1999 and second-tier Lommel United in the cup. Even so, their chance numbers are the better of the two teams: 1.48 xG created and 1.11 allowed. The 3-4 defeat at Mechelen came with a 1.45-0.74 xG advantage. Rest is a small edge to them, four days against three.

Bad news on the home fitness front

Ekstra Bladet reported on Wednesday evening that the injury problems continue, and that coach Mike Tullberg gave largely bad news at his press conference, though Bak is back [2]. No absentee has been confirmed by name.

The players available still carry threat. Philip Billing was called the best player in the Superliga by Rasmus Nissen Kristensen after the derby [4], and teenage forward Julius Emefile has signed a new deal to 2031 after a hat-trick in a cup game [5]. St. Truiden arrive with an extra day and with Taiga Hata named in the squad travelling to Denmark [7].

About 0.8 goals better, not more

The chances come from the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed: Midtjylland 56.7%, the draw 23.5%, St. Truiden 19.7%. The Asian handicap is set at Midtjylland -0.75, which puts them roughly 0.8 goals ahead of the Belgians before kick-off. The goals line is 3.0, with Over priced at 48.4%, so the market leans just under three goals; both teams to score is at 58.9%.

This week's moves are small and all point the same way. Midtjylland's win chance fell 1.3 points, the home handicap 1.8 points and Over 3.0 fell 2.1 points, while the fair goals line slipped from 3.02 to 2.94 and the fair handicap from -0.86 to -0.80. After a derby that asked more of them than the scoreline suggests, the market prices Midtjylland slightly lower than a week ago.

A controlled favourite, not a rout

Join the two halves and the picture is a home side favoured by results, home ground and a five-match unbeaten run, against a visitor with the better chance numbers and one more day of rest. The market's answer is a 56.7% home chance and a line of about 0.8 goals — nearer the xG gap than the league-phase table, which has the Belgians 6th and the Danes 16th after one round. A narrow home win, or a draw that would not shock anyone, fits everything above.
