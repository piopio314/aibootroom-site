Bottom Sunderland host Leeds, and the market calls it level

Bottom versus seventh, on paper

Sunderland go into Sunday's home game bottom of the Premier League, with four straight league defeats behind them. The latest was a 0-2 at Bournemouth, a match in which they created 0.13 expected goals — almost nothing — and were beaten by a side who had not won a league game all season [2]. Leeds arrive seventh on 10 points, after a 1-1 draw with Manchester United at Elland Road, Dominic Calvert-Lewin scoring the equaliser [8].

On the table alone this looks like a mismatch. The market does not see it that way: Leeds are favourites at 37.8%, Sunderland keep 32.0% and the draw takes 30.1%. That is a narrow edge for a top-seven side visiting the bottom club, and it says how much the home shirt is worth on a Sunday at the Stadium of Light.

The week around Sunderland has been noisy. Football Insider report that Regis Le Bris is under pressure, with the board expecting more from him, though there is no immediate expectation that he will be sacked [1]. Kevin Danso is banned for this game after his red card 17 minutes into the Bournemouth defeat [3]. A club at the bottom, a manager in the news and a hole in the defence — Leeds travel into exactly that.

Sunderland's scorelines hide worse numbers

The 0-2 at Bournemouth was kinder than the performance. Sunderland took 0.13 xG and allowed 4.4, which is the profile of a team being cut open rather than one losing narrowly. In their previous league game, at home to Brighton, they lost 0-2 with 0.72 xG. Even so, this is not a side with nothing going forward: they scored three at Manchester City in a 3-5 defeat and built 3.57 xG that day, and they have won two Europa League matches inside the same run of fixtures.

Leeds have lost only once in seven league games and have drawn four. The point against Manchester United came with 1.48 xG to 0.93 against, and the 4-1 over Newcastle was their strongest afternoon, 2.08 to 0.88. Their away day at Arsenal was the exception, a 1-2 with 0.62 xG. Both teams had seven days since their last match, so freshness is not a difference here.

A centre-back short, and a winger maybe back

Danso's absence is more than a name missing from a teamsheet. He is a first-choice centre-back, and Sunderland's back line is already the part of the team under most strain, having allowed 4.4 xG at Bournemouth. There is one piece of better news: Brian Brobbey trained ahead of the Poznan and Leeds matches, according to OneFootball [5].

For Leeds, the Sunderland Echo report that Dan James could return at the Stadium of Light, as part of possible team news on several injured players [7]. The same outlet reported Daniel Farke sending Sunderland a warning, with his side confident after the Manchester United performance [9]. Leeds are also reported to be aiming at European qualification after a strong start [10] — a club with something to chase, not one managing a crisis.

Ten bookmakers, and a level game

Leeds are the narrow favourites at 37.8%, Sunderland are 32.0% and the draw is 30.1% — a spread of 7.7 percentage points across the three outcomes. The handicap tells the same story in goals: the main line is +0, and the fair line rates Leeds only about a tenth of a goal better than Sunderland. Home advantage is doing almost all of the work of closing the gap between seventh and twentieth.

The goals line sits at 2.25, with Over at 51.5% and both teams to score at 52.0%. That fits the recent matches, because Sunderland's 3-5 at Manchester City and Leeds' 4-1 over Newcastle were open games at both ends. This week's moves are small: Sunderland's chance slipped from 33.9% to 32.0%, Leeds rose from 35.9% to 37.8%, and the fair handicap line drifted from 0.04 to 0.11. The timing is consistent with the Bournemouth defeat, the red card, and the report on Dan James.

The same team, a different opponent

Earlier this week we wrote about Sunderland before their Europa League trip to Poznan, calling them the market's clear favourites at 56.4% to win despite three straight league defeats. The team has not changed in four days; the opponent has. Against the side bottom of that European table they were priced to win comfortably. Against seventh in the Premier League, the same players are priced as the home outsider.

Everything points one way except the venue

Sunderland are bottom, giving up chances in volume, missing their centre-back and playing under a manager who, Football Insider report, is under pressure [1]. Leeds are settled, seventh, and have lost once all season. Yet the market keeps this close: the away side are favourites but only narrowly, and the handicap says the two teams are almost level. The goals line adds the last piece — Over 2.25 is at 51.5%, so an open game is marginally the more likely shape, and that would ask questions of the side that has just been opened up on the south coast.
