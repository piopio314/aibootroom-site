A struggling home side meets the form team

Antwerp host Charleroi on Saturday afternoon at the Bosuilstadion, and the two clubs are heading in opposite directions. The home side have lost their last five league games and sit 13th with seven points from eight matches. Charleroi are third with 21 points and have won seven of their eight games.

That is why the market makes the visitors the favourites, at 52.9% against Antwerp's 23.9% — more than double. Favouritism of that size for a travelling side is the market's comment on the gap between these two teams right now.

There is a second story inside the first. Antwerp's coach has faced questions over his position, according to Voetbalkrant [1], and one of his players admitted this week that the team is not playing its best football [3]. A home crowd, a struggling team and a confident opponent: the tension on Saturday is easy to see.

Five defeats, and the numbers behind them

Antwerp's league run reads LLLLL, and the expected-goals numbers say the results are fair. They have allowed 3.03 xG per game across their last five matches and created only 1.06. The 0-2 home defeat to Union St. Gilloise was 0.41 xG created against 3.34 allowed; the 1-5 at Westerlo was 0.91 against 2.43.

Charleroi's last five read WWWWL, including 1-0 league wins at Standard and Zulte Waregem and a 1-0 cup win at Albert Quévy-Mons; every win in that run came by a single goal. Their expected goals are more modest — 1.87 for, 1.46 against — but they win the small margins. Both teams had seven days of rest.

A coach under pressure, and an empty away end

The main off-field story concerns the Antwerp bench. Voetbalkrant reported five days ago that Compper was facing questions over his position [1], and three days ago the same outlet described the 1-0 cup win at amateur side Heist as far from good, with coach and players disagreeing after the match [2]. Antwerp's Somers said the most important thing that day was qualifying, and that the team is not at its best [3].

Charleroi will play without travelling supporters. The club confirmed that the trip to Antwerp will be made without away fans as a precaution after the incidents at Mons [6], and Le Soir reported that Charleroi condemned the pyrotechnics thrown toward home fans [7]. The Storm Ultras said on Tuesday that they will not travel [8]. It removes the usual backing for the team the market rates most highly.

Our read

This is a form mismatch with a clear market view. Antwerp's problem is not bad luck: allowing 3.03 expected goals a game is a leak that shows up every week. Charleroi, meanwhile, have learned to win tight games, beating Standard and Zulte Waregem on the road already this season, with narrow margins in their recent run. The market agrees and goes further, rating the visitors more than twice as likely to win and two-thirds of a goal better. The one thing that could tilt the picture is the strange setting — a favourite with no travelling support, after Charleroi announced the trip would be without away fans [6], against a home team and a coach under real pressure. Charleroi are the favourites; the size of the gap is the argument.
