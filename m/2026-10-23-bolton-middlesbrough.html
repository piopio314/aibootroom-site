Third against fifteenth

Third against fifteenth is the simple frame for Friday night. Middlesbrough have not lost in nine Championship matches and sit on 22 points from 11 games; Bolton are 15th with 14 [1]. One side is protecting a run, the other is trying to build something at home.

Bolton's last five have been strange. They won 3-1 at Wolves and 4-3 at Norwich, but lost 1-2 at home to Stoke after creating 3.13 expected goals. Their 1-0 home win over Cardiff came with just 0.48 xG of their own, and the 0-0 at Southampton was a rearguard job [6]. The results are better than the performances underneath them.

Middlesbrough's run has a similar wrinkle. They have drawn three of their last five, including 2-2 with Cardiff on Saturday, a game they controlled and did not win [1]. Creating chances has not been the problem. Turning control into three points has.

What the chance numbers say

Over the same five-match window, Middlesbrough average 1.98 expected goals and concede 1.03. They made 2.57 xG at Stoke and 2.78 at home to Cardiff, more than three times what they allowed that day. That is the profile of a side that controls matches away from home, even when the scoreline stays level.

Bolton's numbers are flatter: 1.51 xG created and 1.47 allowed across the same span, and their 3-1 win at Wolves came with 1.62 xG against 2.23 for the hosts. Both teams had six days since their last match, so freshness is not a factor either way. The table separates them more clearly than the form strings do: 22 points against 14, and a goal difference of +6 against -3.

Bolton short in defence, Boro close to full strength

Bolton's clearest absence is at the centre of defence. C. Forino-Joseph is out with a groin injury after 616 minutes this season and a rating of 7.29, the highest in the Bolton list. Losing a regular there, against the third-placed attack in the division, changes how the home side has to defend its box.

Bolton may also be short wide. According to wakingthered.com, winger Jayden Nelson suffered an apparent leg injury playing for the under-21 side on Tuesday [5] — a report from a reserve-team match, not confirmed by the club. Middlesbrough's list carries less weight: A. Jones (322 minutes), D. Strelec (90) and R. Pukstas (22) are out, with no first-choice regular among them. A predicted-lineup report from thefalse9.com says Will Lankshear returns to the XI at Bolton [2].

A clear favourite, by about a goal

Across the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Middlesbrough are 58.5% to win, Bolton 20.0% and the draw 21.5%. Bolton's chance is barely a third of the visitors'. The handicap says the same thing in goals: Bolton get a one-goal start, and the market's fair line is 0.9 goals in Middlesbrough's favour. In plain terms, Boro are rated about a goal the better team, so a one-goal away win sits near the midpoint. The total is set at 3.0 goals, with over at 52.7% — a lean, not a certainty — and both teams to score at 60.9%.

This week's moves point one way. Middlesbrough's win chance rose by 2.5 percentage points in recent days, to 58.5%, and the fair handicap line moved with it, from 0.8 to 0.9 goals, while the fair goals line eased slightly from 3.14 to 3.11 around a 3.0 main line. Bolton's absence in defence is already reflected in the prices: they are 20.0% to win, barely a third of Middlesbrough's chance.

Bolton can create, Boro should control

Bolton have a real route into this game. The 3.13 xG they produced against Stoke shows a home side that can carve out chances even in defeat, and a team with little to protect can play with freedom. But the season so far — third against fifteenth, a large xG edge over the last five matches, nine games unbeaten — is what the prices describe, and the market has followed the football rather than the other way round. Middlesbrough are clear favourites, rated about a goal the better side, with the goals line leaning slightly high. The caution for the visitors is their own recent habit: the draws have come in games they controlled, and control alone has not settled them.
