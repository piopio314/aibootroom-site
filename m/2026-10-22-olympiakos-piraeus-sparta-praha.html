A perfect run meets an unbeaten one

Olympiakos and Sparta Praha sit either side of the middle of the 36-team Europa League table after two matchdays, 10th and 11th, and neither has lost a game in the competition. Three points in Piraeus on Thursday would strengthen the home side's place in the top third and keep Sparta in the chasing pack.

Olympiakos come in on five wins from five, seven goals scored and one conceded. The best of them came a week ago in Marseille, where Hezze scored the only goal of the game [1] — a result that left Marseille bottom of the table.

Sparta's run is quieter but real: three wins and two draws in five, ten goals scored and two conceded. There is history in the air as well — infotbal.cz reports that no Czech team has won away at Olympiakos [7], though coach Priske said he is thinking mainly about Sparta and not about the Czech record [6]. Novasports, meanwhile, reports that Olympiakos have a poor historical record against Czech opponents [4].

Narrow wins, and an underdog that creates

The scorelines say more than the badge. Four of Olympiakos' five wins came by a single goal, and their expected goals over that run read 1.52 for and 0.71 against — a team that wins without needing to dominate the ball. The 1-0 in Marseille came with only 0.63 xG created against 0.90 allowed; the 2-0 at Panetolikos on Sunday was the one clear performance, 2.52 xG to 1.31.

Sparta's numbers point a different way from their price. They average 1.75 expected goals a game over the same five matches, more than Olympiakos' 1.52, and allowed 0.94. Yet they drew 0-0 at Banik Ostrava on Sunday with 1.64 xG, so the finishing has not matched the chance creation. Both teams had four days between matches, which makes rest even.

Who is missing, and where it hurts

Olympiakos are without centre-forward Ayoub El Kaabi, out with a broken leg after 419 minutes this season. That absence was public before this week, so it is already in the price — and they have kept winning without him. J. Roca (333 minutes) and Y. Yazici (knee) are also unavailable; the coach named his squad for the game [2] and spoke with Yaremchuk at the pre-match press conference [3].

Sparta's problem is bigger. Adam Karabec is out with an ankle injury: 970 minutes this season, 12 appearances, three goals and three assists, and the most-used player missing from their midfield. Defender M. Rynes (770 minutes, two goals, two assists) is also out with an ankle problem, and two more defenders, Sevinsky and Mbe Soh, are doubtful. According to gazzetta.gr, Sparta will not train at the Karaiskakis before the match and will be backed by their own fans [9].

A clear favourite, a one-goal line

Across the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Olympiakos are 62.2% to win, the draw 21.3% and Sparta 16.4% — the home side are clear favourites, more than three times Sparta's chance. The handicap shows the size of the gap in goals: the fair line is -1.01, so Olympiakos are rated about one goal better. On the main -1 line, an Olympiakos win by two or more goals is roughly a coin flip, and a one-goal win would not be enough.

The goals line is 2.75, with over priced at 48.7% — the market leans just under three goals, consistent with four one-goal Olympiakos wins and Sparta's 0-0 at the weekend. Both teams scored in 51.1% of the market's read, so a tight game with goals at both ends is the likelier picture rather than a shutout.

The read

This is a home win the football explains. Olympiakos win narrowly and often — four of five by a single goal — and they have done it without El Kaabi, so his absence does not break the case. Sparta are unbeaten and create more per game than their hosts, which is why 16.4% is not nothing, but they come to Piraeus without Karabec and Rynes, two of their most-used players. That is why the market has Olympiakos clear favourites, the teams rated about a goal apart, and the total just under three goals.
