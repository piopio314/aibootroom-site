The best away team in the league, at home again

Mainz are fifth in the Bundesliga and have won their last five league games on the road. The latest was a 1-0 win at Schalke, which the FAZ described as one of Urs Fischer's side's best performances of the season [1]. On Saturday they host Werder Bremen at the Mewa Arena as clear favourites. The trouble is that both of their last two home games ended in defeat.

Werder are the opposite kind of team right now. They are unbeaten in five league games and have come back from a two-goal deficit in three of them, most recently a 2-2 draw with Paderborn [10]. Niclas Füllkrug has scored in five consecutive Bundesliga games and is joint-top of the scoring charts [9]. So the favoured side is the one that creates, and the resilience belongs to the visitors.

The task is simple enough. According to WELT, Mainz want to stay in touch with the top of the table with a result here [3]. Local media are also asking a different question, reported by allgemeine-zeitung.de: whether this team can finally deliver in front of its own crowd [2]. A win answers both.

Mainz create more, Werder keep coming back

Mainz have scored 14 goals in their last five league games and averaged 2.56 xG. They out-created their opponent in three of those five matches. Even the two home defeats were not collapses in open play: they lost 3-4 to Bayer Leverkusen with 3.13 xG and 1-3 to Eintracht Frankfurt with 2.12. The chances were there; the results in front of their own fans were not.

Werder's numbers are softer. Their 1.84 xG average is well below Mainz's, and their 3-1 win over RB Leipzig came with only 0.95 xG against 1.5 for the opponent. Their last game was the other way round, 3.21 xG in the 2-2 with Paderborn. That mix of good and lucky results is why they sit eighth on nine points, three places and three points behind Saturday's hosts. Both teams had seven days of rest.

Werder lose their left-back, Mainz wait on Silas

Werder will be without left-back Moussa Ndiaye. He misses this match, the following game against Hoffenheim and the DFB-Pokal tie with Borussia Dortmund because of persistent groin problems, africasoccer.com reports [7]. That takes out a first-choice defender from the side Werder must hold together against Mainz's 2.56 xG attack. The Kreiszeitung also reports that ten players were missing from training before the trip, though Justin Njinmah has returned [8].

Mainz have better news. Robin Zentner and Silas are back in full team training, but a comeback in this match is reported to come too soon [4]. Silas has completed his first full session since a serious leg injury [5]. Up front, Phillip Tietz has five Bundesliga goals this season and is set for a direct duel with Füllkrug, according to bundesliga.com [6].

Nearly three times Werder's chance

Our read: the attack is the favourite, the home form is the question

This is a match where the market and the football agree on the same thing: Mainz create more than Werder, and they create it consistently. The price is built on that, on five straight away wins and on 2.56 xG a game. What the price does not remove is the doubt in front of their own crowd, two home defeats with seven goals conceded in them. Werder's three comebacks and Füllkrug's run explain why the visitors are still expected to score, and why the goals line sits where it does. Mainz are favoured; the home form is the part of the afternoon nobody can price away.
