Eight wins from eight, one crack

FC Porto have won all eight of their Primeira Liga matches this season: twenty-four points, a goal difference of plus sixteen, first place. On Saturday night they go to Barcelos to face Gil Vicente, who are sixth on fourteen points and who won at Braga last weekend.

The doubt around Porto is not domestic. On Tuesday they lost 0-1 at home to PSV Eindhoven, their second Champions League defeat in a row, and according to onefootball that result equalled the club's worst start to a UEFA group or league phase [8]. Porto made 1.49 xG that night and allowed 1.97, so the scoreline was no accident. Former player Rodolfo Reis said he was disappointed with the display and that Porto "played like a small team", as Record reported [9].

Gil Vicente are the reason this is not a formality. Luís Pinto's team won 2-1 at Braga on Sunday [3], and Braga answered by changing coach, with Franclim Carvalho replacing Carlos Vicens [4]. Record noted that Gil Vicente have now won three away games in a row [1]. Their last league game at home was quieter: 1-1 with Maritimo.

Control, not luck

Porto's league run is built on control rather than shootouts. At Maritimo they created 2.13 xG and allowed 0.19 in a 2-1 win. They beat Benfica 3-1 with a modest 1.3 xG and won 4-1 at Casa Pia with 2.6. Across their last five in all competitions they average 1.71 xG for and 1.27 against, because the two European defeats pulled both numbers the wrong way.

Gil Vicente's last five read three wins, one draw and one loss, with 1.33 xG for and 1.56 against. They are creating less than they allow and winning anyway, and the Braga win was close on chances at 1.54 to 1.39. Porto had four days between PSV and this match, Gil Vicente five since Braga.

Porto travel without two

O Jogo reports that Vasco Sousa and William Gomes are unavailable for Porto in Barcelos [5], two absences from Francesco Farioli's squad four days after the PSV defeat.

Luís Pinto has Gil Vicente sixth after eight games [3], and beIN Sports reported this week that they will look to use their home advantage [2]. Luís Godinho has been appointed as referee [11], and Porto's away tickets for Barcelos sold out within hours [10].

More than five times Gil Vicente's chance

The bookmakers we compare make Porto 65.5% favourites, with the draw at 21.9% and Gil Vicente at 12.4% — Porto's chance is more than five times the home side's. Eight wins from eight and a plus-sixteen goal difference against Gil Vicente's plus two explains most of that gap. The recent meetings point the same way: Porto won 2-0 on their last league visit to Barcelos and 3-0 at home in January.

The handicap rates Porto about 1.17 goals better, and Gil Vicente are given a start of one and a quarter goals, so a single-goal Porto win does not quite settle it in Porto's favour. The goals line leans slightly under: the fair total is 2.47, over 2.5 sits at 49.2% and both teams to score at 43.9%.

Our read: Portugal is not Europe

The through-line is simple. Porto are perfect in Portugal and shaky in Europe, and the market has decided the second part does not travel home with them. Gil Vicente's case rests on three away wins and a home chance near twelve per cent, while the price says Porto win by roughly a goal in a game with no strong goal expectation. That is the match: a controlled Porto night, or the team that lost to PSV and Betis running into a side that has already beaten Braga.
