A short week against a long one

Arsenal and Everton meet at the Emirates on Saturday afternoon, and the two clubs arrive from very different weeks. Arsenal played in Munich on Wednesday and drew 2-2, scoring late through Declan Rice after a Harry Kane penalty [4]. Everton's last match was on the 17th, a 0-2 home defeat to Chelsea [9].

The story of Arsenal's week is Bukayo Saka. He came off in the 10th minute in Munich with an apparent left leg injury, as reported by Yahoo Sports [1]. Saka is the player much of Arsenal's right-side attack runs through, and a game three days later leaves little time to work on a problem like that.

That is the tension. Arsenal are second with 18 points from seven games, but this is their third match in eight days and the last one involved travel. Everton have had seven days to prepare, and a manager in David Moyes who was reported to be angry about the decisions that went against his team against Chelsea [7].

Everton's away plan, and its limit

Arsenal's last five read DWWWL, and the numbers behind that run are steady: 1.65 xG created per game and 1.14 allowed. Munich was the only one of those five matches where the opponent created more, 2.55 against Arsenal's 1.42. At home the picture is calmer — Leeds were beaten 2-1 with 2.33 xG created and 0.62 allowed.

Everton's away plan in this run has been built on draws: 2-2 at Hull and 0-0 at Tottenham, both with a reasonable share of the chances. The problem sits at the other end. Against Chelsea they managed 0.43 xG, the lowest figure in either side's last five matches, and it came at home. They are eighth with 10 points from seven games, four of them draws.

Saka, Hincapie and Everton's right side

Everything depends on how serious Saka's injury is. Yahoo Sports reported that he was forced off with an apparent left leg problem after ten minutes [1], and Arsenal have not confirmed how long he will be out. There is better news at the back: Mikel Arteta said Piero Hincapie could be available if he trains in the coming days [2], and as.com reported that he worked individually before Munich and could return here [3].

Everton's question is at right-back, where David Moyes could have a decision to make before the trip [8]. That is the flank Saka normally attacks, so a change there would be tested early. Everton were also reported to be unhappy with the appointment of Tony Harrington as referee, after decisions went against them last time out [12].

The favourite has to solve two problems

The football and the numbers point the same way. Arsenal are second, they have won six of seven league games, they are at home, and they are unbeaten in five meetings with Everton — two wins and three draws. They beat Everton 2-0 here in March, and a sports.yahoo.com report noted Everton held out until late in that game [10]. That is why the home chance is 68.3% even with a tired squad.

But two things keep this from being a straightforward afternoon. Saka's fitness is unresolved, and Everton have had a full week to set up deep and narrow, the shape that earned them draws at Hull and Tottenham. Everton's own problem is that they created 0.43 xG against Chelsea, and the team they now face has allowed 1.14 xG per game. Arsenal should win; the question is whether they win by enough to cover a line of one and a quarter goals.
