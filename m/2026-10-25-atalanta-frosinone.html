Atalanta's Sunday test: Sarri under pressure, Frosinone above them

Atalanta are 11th and their coach is in the Italian papers. Frosinone arrive sixth, with Serie A's top scorer — and the market still makes the home side clear favourites.

A routine fixture on paper, a strange one in the table

Atalanta go into Sunday's match in Bergamo in 11th place, with nine points from seven games and four league defeats already. Frosinone, newly promoted, are sixth with 13 points and arrive above them. On the fixture list this looks like a straightforward home win; in the table it does not.

The pressure sits on the home bench. La Repubblica reports that Maurizio Sarri is under pressure at Atalanta, with Thiago Motta and Tudor named as possible replacements [1]. newsy-today.com reports, citing La Repubblica, that Sarri keeps the club's confidence for now, but that his position depends heavily on the two-match stretch against Ajax and then Frosinone [2]. Sunday is the second half of that stretch.

Frosinone bring the league's top scorer. Antonio Raimondo has seven goals in seven matchdays, including a hat-trick in the 4-2 win over Sassuolo five days ago [7][8]. A promoted side with the leading scorer and a place in the top six is not the opponent Atalanta's season needed right now.

Atalanta decide games, Frosinone score in them

Atalanta's last five league results read W-L-W-L-L: no draws, and three defeats in that run. There is no draw in their league season at all, so every match is being settled one way or the other — and lately against them. Last weekend's 3-2 defeat at AC Milan followed the same shape: Atalanta made 1.74 expected goals and allowed 2.58 [3].

Chance creation is not the problem. Sarri's side average 1.95 expected goals across their last five matches, and the 3-1 win over Pafos in the Conference League came with 3.47. Frosinone's numbers point the other way: 10 goals in their last five league games, but 2.24 expected goals allowed per game across the same run. They score and they concede, and the 0-3 at Napoli is the result that shows the risk in that style. Frosinone have also had nine days since their last match, Atalanta seven.

A short absentee list, and a young home line

Atalanta's injury list is short: Sulemana and Hien are out, but neither has played a minute this season, so the missing names do not change the shape of the team. What matters more is how Sarri uses what he has. He said this week that his side alternates good spells with moments of major limitations, doing well for 30 minutes in every match [4]. Italian media report that he is making room for young players, with U23 players Obric and Idele in the side [6].

Frosinone have no absences listed, though the local press reports that the condition of Calvani is to be assessed before the trip [9]. Massimiliano Alvini relies on his promotion-winning core, with new signings Schmid and Masini already central to his system [10]. Squawka describes the side as Serie A's surprise package, using high pressing and rapid counter-attacks [11] — which is exactly the space Atalanta's front line will be asked to punish.

Favourites, but less than a full goal better

On fair percentages, Atalanta are 52.8% to win, the draw 22.9% and Frosinone 24.3%. That is a clear favourite rather than a certainty: the visitors still have a 24.3% chance. The main handicap line is Atalanta -0.75, so a two-goal home win lands it fully and a one-goal win only partly. The fair line has drifted from -0.8 to -0.65 goals, so the market now rates Atalanta closer to half a goal than a full goal better than Frosinone.

The main goals line is 3.5, with over at 47.4%, so the lean is very slight towards under four goals, yet both teams to score is priced at 65.5% — more likely than not, both sides find the net. That fits Frosinone scoring in four of their last five and conceding chances at a high rate. In recent days Atalanta's win chance has slipped 3.2 points to 52.8% while Frosinone's moved up 2.8 points to 24.3%, and the fair handicap was cut from -0.8 to -0.65, consistent with three league defeats in their last four Serie A matches and the reports around Sarri. The fair goals line nudged from 3.34 to 3.39, so the goal expectation has barely moved.

What we wrote before Ajax

Earlier this week, before Atalanta's Conference League trip, Ajax held the home edge against Atalanta, and La Repubblica reported that Sarri was under pressure [1], though the club kept confidence in him for now [2]. The same question follows the team into Bergamo. The difference now is the table: Frosinone are four points above them, but the market gives Atalanta a 52.8% home chance and makes them favourite, with the last visit here a counterweight rather than the basis for that status.

Our read

Atalanta create enough to win — 1.95 expected goals a game — against a Frosinone defence allowing 2.24. But 52.8% is not a heavy favourite, the fair handicap is under a full goal, and both teams to score at 65.5% says the visitors are more likely than not to score. Atalanta are more likely than not to take three points on Sunday; a comfortable afternoon is a different question, and with La Repubblica reporting Sarri is under pressure [1], the result matters more than the score.
