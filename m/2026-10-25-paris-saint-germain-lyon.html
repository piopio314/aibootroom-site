First against second, with two problems attached

Sunday night at the Parc des Princes should be the simplest story of the Ligue 1 weekend: the top two meet, both on 14 points after seven games. Lyon are first only on goal difference, +8 to PSG's +5. Win, and you lead the table alone.

Instead the game arrives carrying two problems. The first is football. Three PSG players came out of the 4-2 win over Barcelona injured: Kvaratskhelia ruptured the ACL in his right knee and will have surgery, ending his season [1]. Captain Marquinhos and Ousmane Dembélé were hurt in the same match [2], and Luis Enrique called those injuries the worst part of the game [3].

The second problem is bigger than football. The Paris police prefecture has asked for this eighth-round fixture to be postponed, citing planned protests this weekend and the NFL game at the Stade de France on Sunday, as reported by Goal.com and through news.google.com [10][11]. If that request is granted, there is no match to talk about — no result, no points, no movement at the top.

Five wins, and a warning inside them

PSG have won all five of their last matches and scored 16 goals. The 4-2 over Barcelona came with 3.74 xG created, the 3-1 at Strasbourg with 2.69. Not every win is built the same way: at Manchester City they scored four from 1.85 xG and allowed 2.67, so the result ran ahead of the chances. Across the five games they average 3.37 xG for and 1.92 against — strong numbers at both ends of the pitch.

Lyon have four wins in five, the exception a 1-2 at Lens in which they created 1.04 xG. Their best evidence is the 2-1 over Nice, won with ten men from the 70th minute [7]. Their five-game averages, 1.82 xG for and 1.46 against, describe a good side rather than a dominant one. Seven days of rest against PSG's five is a small advantage they will take.

Three out in one night, a red card for Lyon

These are not rotation absences. Kvaratskhelia is a first-choice wide attacker and is lost for the campaign [1]. Dembélé is the main forward and Marquinhos is the captain at centre-back, so the spine and the attack were hit in one evening [2]. That is a structural change, and it landed five days before kick-off.

Lyon's issue is smaller but real: L'Équipe reported Nakamura was sent off in the 70th minute against Nice, and The Guardian reported Lyon won 2-1 anyway, with L'Équipe adding Wahi had a Nice goal ruled out in stoppage time [7][9]. RMC Sport reported Lyon also arrive on the back of a Europa League win over Crystal Palace [8]; they have been playing twice a week too.

Clear favourites, about one and a third goals better

Across the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, PSG are 67.3% to win, the draw 17.5% and Lyon 15.1%. That makes PSG about four and a half times as likely as Lyon to take the three points. The handicap says the same thing in goals: the main line is -1.25 and the fair line is -1.33, so the market rates PSG roughly one and a third goals stronger. That points to a comfortable win rather than a rout.

Goals are less certain. The line is 3.5 and over is priced at 48.6%, so the market sees a coin flip either side of three and a half, with both teams scoring at 60.9%. That fits PSG's 3.37 xG a game and Lyon's habit of finding the net. The fair handicap has eased slightly from -1.4 to -1.33 in recent days, a small move towards Lyon rather than away from them. Even so, PSG stay clear favourites.

What we said about Lyon three days ago

Earlier this week we wrote about Lyon as a side top of Ligue 1 and perfect in Europe, and noted that the market still made Hoffenheim the narrow favourites against them. Sunday runs the other way. Lyon are top of the table again, but at the Parc des Princes they are clear underdogs at 15.1% — and that gap says more about PSG's squad and home ground than about Lyon's start.

The football and the price, joined

The 67.3% rests on the team that won five straight and scored 16 goals. The team sheet will not be that team: no Kvaratskhelia, no Marquinhos, no Dembélé. A PSG win by one clear goal, rather than a heavy one, is the shape the numbers point to. And all of it holds only if the match is played, with the postponement request from the Paris police prefecture sitting over Sunday night [10][11]. If it goes ahead, PSG are clear favourites — but Lyon, top of the table and winning with ten men, are not there to make up the numbers.
