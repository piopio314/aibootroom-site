One point chase, one clean start

Crvena Zvezda go into Thursday's second matchday with no points in the Conference League. Their opener at Lugano finished 3-2 to the Swiss side, decided by a late penalty [1], and it leaves them 24th of 36. Copenhagen, by contrast, opened at home and won 3-0 against Braga. One team is already chasing; the other is already level with the leaders' group.

That makes the night at the Rajko Mitić heavier than a normal second game. Zvezda cannot afford to fall further behind in a 36-team table where the margins between 24th and safety are thin, while Copenhagen can put themselves on six points and near the top before the winter.

The two clubs also have a short shared history that says nothing gets settled quickly. In 2019 Champions League qualifying they drew 1-1 in Belgrade and 2-2 in Copenhagen, and the tie went to a penalty shootout. Marko Marin, now Zvezda's technical director, brought that night up again this week [6]. Both previous meetings finished level, and the market for this one is not far from level either.

Zvezda score for fun, Copenhagen keep it tight

Zvezda's last five games have produced 15 goals for and five against, and their chance creation is the reason they are favoured here. On Sunday they beat Mladost Lucani 5-1 with 6.02 xG and gave up only 0.52 — a home performance of complete control. Their average of 3.14 xG across the run is the loudest number in this fixture, and it is why the Lugano defeat did not push them out of favouritism.

That Lugano night is the exception in the run: 2.29 xG conceded, the only recent game where their defence was opened up. Copenhagen arrive with the opposite profile — 0.96 xG against per game, and a 3-0 win over Braga built on 1.56 xG against 0.55. But they are not converting enough. Sunday's 1-1 at home with Aarhus came with 2.01 xG, two points dropped, and Midtjylland's win at Viborg drew level on points at the top of the Superliga [7]. Both teams had four days between matches, so freshness is even.

A tweak, not a reshuffle

Novosti reports that Ivanić and Katić have returned to the Zvezda squad ahead of this match [5], and Sportska centrala reports that Muhamed Čam will almost certainly start after making the strongest impression among the substitutes in Lugano [3]. The same outlet says the line-up will be only slightly changed from the defeat in Switzerland [4]. Read together, that points to one or two adjustments rather than a rebuild — a sign the staff saw the Lugano loss as a bad night more than a broken plan.

Copenhagen's week has been quieter on the pitch and busier off it. Inside World Football reports that Parken Sport and Entertainment is exploring a full or partial sale of the club as part of a strategic review of its football business [8]. That is background noise for the players, and nothing in the team news suggests a weakened XI. Zvezda's returning pair and Čam's likely start are the only selection signals worth carrying into the night.

Narrow home edge, goals expected

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Zvezda's win chance is 42.0%, the draw 26.1% and Copenhagen 31.9%. That is a favourite, but a soft one: the handicap sits at -0.25, meaning the market rates Zvezda about a quarter of a goal better than Copenhagen — closer to a level match than to a clear one. Total goals are set at 2.75, with over at 50.9%, so the market expects a game around the three-goal mark, a 2-1 or 2-2 shape rather than a grind. Both teams to score is priced at 60.2%, more likely than not.

The week's moves are small and pointed the same way: Copenhagen's win chance rose by 1.0 point, the draw's fell by 1.5, and Zvezda's gained half a point. That is consistent with a market treating the Belgrade team news as squad depth rather than a decisive upgrade, and with a Copenhagen side that created plenty against Aarhus and still only drew. The goals line has held at 2.75 with over and under moving less than a point each, which fits two teams whose recent games have produced goals at both ends.

Our read

The football and the market tell the same story from different angles. Zvezda's home attack is the best thing in this fixture, and it is why a side with zero points and a European defeat behind it is still the favourite in Belgrade. Copenhagen's defensive numbers are the reason that favouritism is only a quarter of a goal and not more, and their finishing problems against Aarhus are the reason the draw has shortened around them. The goals line and the both-teams-to-score price complete the picture: an open game, a small home edge, and no clean sheet taken for granted. A Zvezda win is the single most likely result here — but at 42.0% it is an advantage, not a certainty, and the 2019 meetings suggest Copenhagen will make it uncomfortable.
