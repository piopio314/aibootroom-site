Bottom visits seventh, with both squads wobbling

Mendizorroza hosts La Liga's 20th-placed team on Friday night. Malaga have four points from nine games and no win — four draws and five defeats — and they arrive five points behind the team directly above them.

Alaves are seventh with 11 points, which sounds like a comfortable place to be. It is not a comfortable moment: they have lost four of their last five, taking one point from fifteen, and their last home league game ended in a 1-0 defeat by Valencia.

That is why this fixture is interesting rather than simple. The gap in the table is wide, the gap in current form is not, and the market has had to weigh both.

Alaves create enough, but concede more

Alaves' run reads LLDLL, and the expected goals say the results are not simply bad luck. They averaged 1.09 xG for and 1.46 against across those five games; the 1-2 defeat at Celta Vigo on 18 October [1] had the same shape as the 1-2 home loss to Atletico Madrid, where they created 1.30 but allowed 2.05.

The 0-0 at Athletic Club is an outlier in a different way. Alaves made almost nothing that day, 0.05 xG, and the point came from defending. Malaga's numbers are weaker at both ends: 0.70 xG created and 1.94 conceded per game, and only once in five matches did they pass one expected goal. The 5-2 home defeat by Real Sociedad on 18 October [5] was the heaviest scoreline in that run, with Takefusa Kubo scoring and assisting on his first start since September [6]. Both teams had five days between matches.

Malaga lose another midfield option

Malaga's midfield is the area under pressure. Cadena SER reported nine hours ago that Joaquin Munoz has a right soleus muscle injury and is expected to miss Friday's game [4]. J. Cruz is doubtful with a muscle injury after 251 minutes across seven appearances this season, and D. Murillo is also a doubt with a knee problem — a lot of options missing from a squad already bottom of the table.

Alaves' list is shorter in impact. M. Rodriguez is out with an ankle injury after 54 minutes in three appearances this season, and F. Garces is suspended. Nothing there reshapes the home eleven the way Munoz's expected absence could reshape Malaga's.

Three-quarters of a goal, and a low total

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Alaves are 54.9% to win, the draw 25.7% and Malaga 19.4%. That makes the home side more likely than not, and almost three times as likely as the visitors. The handicap line sits at -0.75, which rates Alaves roughly three-quarters of a goal the better team: a one-goal home win does not fully clear that line, a two-goal win does.

The goals line is 2.25, with Over at 53.1%, and the fair total sits at 2.37 — goals, but not a shootout. Malaga's 1.94 xG conceded per game pulls the number up; their 0.70 created pulls it back down. The week's moves are small and point the same way: the fair handicap moved from -0.63 to -0.70, Alaves' win chance rose 1.6 points, and Both Teams To Score "Yes" fell 1.4 points to 48.6%. That is consistent with a bottom side losing 5-2 at home and then losing a midfielder to injury.

The table wins the argument, narrowly

Both parts of this are true: Alaves are the better team by table and by chance quality, and Alaves are also in a poor run. The market has chosen to weight the first more than the second, and 54.9% for a side with one point from fifteen shows how much Malaga's winless start and 0.70 xG per game count.

A narrow home win with few goals is the shape those prices describe, and it fits what the last month has shown: one team that cannot score enough, another that scores a little but concedes more. It is more likely than not to be Alaves' night — not a certainty, and probably not a comfortable one.
