Five days, two routs

Leipzig lost 1-6 at Bayern Munich on Saturday, according to sportschau.de [3], their third defeat of the season, according to WELT [2]. On Wednesday they lost 1-4 at Real Madrid in the Champions League, and they sit seventh in the Bundesliga with nine points from six games. Two heavy defeats in five days, and Elversberg arrive sixth, a point above them, but the home side are 68.6% favourites — about five times Elversberg's chance.

The team waiting for them is a promoted one, and it is above them. Elversberg are sixth with 10 points, after a 4-3 win over Augsburg that BILD reports as their third Bundesliga victory in six games [6]. Leipzig's week is a football problem; Elversberg's start turns it into an awkward one as well.

There is also the coach. Kicker reports that Martin Demichelis needs wins against Elversberg, Schalke and Rot-Weiss Essen to quieten growing doubts about him [1]. This is the first of those three matches, at home, against the least established of the three opponents.

Split cleanly by venue

Leipzig's last five divide by where they were played. At home they beat Eintracht Frankfurt 6-0, creating 4.03 xG and allowing 1.46, and beat PSV Eindhoven 1-0 with 2.1 xG. Away they lost 1-6 at Bayern, with 0.84 xG for and 3.4 against, and 1-4 in Madrid, 1.46 for and 2.95 against. An average of 2.3 xG conceded per game is the number sitting behind both scorelines.

Elversberg's five games are noisier. The 4-3 win over Augsburg was almost two matches in one, with 3.78 xG for and 3.92 against. Away from home they have mixed a 0-1 at Union Berlin where they created 0.24 with a 4-3 win at Gladbach. They have had seven days since the Augsburg game; Leipzig have had three since Madrid, and that freshness is the visitors' clearest edge.

Onyeka's unclear week

The main team-news story is on the Elversberg side and it is not settled. Francis Onyeka was reported out for up to four weeks with a foot ligament injury [8], and two days later reported back in team training [7]. Whether he plays is genuinely open, and the Saarbrücker Zeitung podcast Inside SVE describes the club's injury concerns as growing [9].

Leipzig's issue is legs rather than names. They played in Madrid on Wednesday [5] and have three days between that match and this one. Vincent Wagner, the Elversberg coach, has said publicly that his side cannot yet do certain things, BILD reports [11] — a sign the visitors will choose their moments rather than trade punches for ninety minutes.

Our read

Saturday asks which evidence is stronger. Leipzig's last two results describe a team in trouble; their home form describes a team that scores freely in front of its own crowd, and the market has gone with the second. Elversberg arrive fresher, higher in the table and with nothing to protect, and the goal line points to an open game, which suits a side that has scored four in two of its last five. A Leipzig win is the more likely result. The uncomfortable version of it — an end-to-end afternoon against a promoted team with nothing to lose — is the one this fixture looks built for.
