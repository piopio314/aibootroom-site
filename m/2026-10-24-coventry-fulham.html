Two of the bottom four, one narrow favourite

Coventry are 17th and Fulham are 18th, and they meet at the CBS Arena on Saturday with one point between them. This is a meeting of two of the bottom four, not a home side in comfort. The table gives Coventry the better position: six points from seven games against Fulham's five. Even so, the market makes Fulham the more likely winner.

The reason is in how both teams reached those numbers. Fulham got their first Premier League win of the season on 17 October, 1-0 at home to Hull City, and allowed just 0.16 expected goals [7]. That was their cleanest defensive afternoon of the season, and it arrived at a useful moment: The Athletic reported that Alvaro Arbeloa had failed to win any of his first six Premier League matches at the club [8].

Coventry's six points have come with an unsteady underlying game: they beat Newcastle 4-1 on 12 October but were out-created in it, 1.47 expected goals to 1.84, and their last match was a 3-1 defeat at Tottenham on Monday [1]. Coventry Live reports questions over two conspicuously absent players as Frank Lampard weighs changes [5].

Fitness questions and a manager under pressure

Coventry Live reports that questions hang over two conspicuously absent players, who are not named, as Frank Lampard weighs whether to stick with or tweak the team that lost at Tottenham [5]. Lampard himself said afterwards: "Keep our feet on the ground because performance was good but we can get better. Every game is challenging. Fulham are coming at the weekend" [3]. The Guardian described Coventry as stubborn at Tottenham and raised the question of whether Carl Rushworth should have saved Ben Davies' effort [2].

On the Fulham side, Coventry Live reports that Arbeloa has breathing space before a match it calls a must-win, with the club still feeling the pressure of a poor start [6]. Antonee Robinson said adaptation to change was taking longer than everyone would have liked, but that the side are moving in the right direction and are behind the manager [9]. The Guardian's analysis argued that Arbeloa's attempt to make Fulham free-flowing is already struggling, pointing to the 2-1 collapse at Ipswich after a switch to a flat 4-4-2 [10].

A quarter of a goal, and a split on goals

The bookmakers we compare, ten of them, make Fulham 40.8% to win, Coventry 32.0% and the draw 27.2%. Fulham's is the biggest single chance but not a majority — a Coventry win and the draw together still make up most of the outcomes. On the handicap, Coventry are given a start of +0.25, which says the market rates Fulham only about a quarter of a goal better. The fair line is 0.17 of a goal in Coventry's favour, so home advantage has closed most of the gap that Fulham's chance numbers open.

The goals line sits at 2.75, with Over priced at 49.1% and a fair total of 2.71 — a genuine split just under three goals. That fits a Coventry side that has conceded 12 in its last five and a Fulham side that has scored in four of its last five, with both teams to score at 58.9%. The week's moves have all been under a single point: Over 2.75 down 0.6, Coventry's win chance down 0.4. That is consistent with a week where the news was a first win and unnamed fitness questions rather than a result that repriced the fixture.
