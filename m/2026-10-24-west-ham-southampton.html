A promotion game at the London Stadium

West Ham are second in the Championship and Southampton are eighth, and on Saturday they meet at the London Stadium. Both clubs hope to be playing in the Premier League next season, as Football London reported this week [6]. That makes this a game about promotion, not only about three points.

West Ham have 22 points from 11 games and a goal difference of plus 18. Southampton have 17 points and sit outside the play-off places. A home win would push the Hammers further clear of one of the teams chasing them, while an away win would pull the Saints right back into the race.

The two teams arrive in different moods. West Ham won 6-0 at Lincoln City on 14 October and then 1-0 at Swansea. Southampton beat Norwich 1-0 and Portsmouth 1-0, but their last match was a 0-0 draw at home to Bolton. Both sides had seven days between games, so neither arrives tired.

Bursts away, draws at home

West Ham's last five in league and cup read W-W-D-D-L, with 12 goals scored and six conceded. The expected goals say the results are earned: they average 1.92 for and 1.35 against. The 6-0 at Lincoln came second in that run, with 3.23 xG to 0.39, which is as one-sided as the score suggests.

The soft spot is at home. The 1-1 with QPR on 9 October was close on chances, 1.44 xG to 1.27, and Fulham won 3-2 in the League Cup at the London Stadium in September. Southampton's numbers are similar in shape but weaker in output: 1.82 xG for and 1.28 against over their last five, yet only seven goals. At Wrexham they made 2.08 xG, allowed 0.54 and lost 1-2. That is the season in one match.

Two attacking changes, one doubt

West Ham have no confirmed absentees for this game. The False 9 reports that Lamadrid is the only player out [3]. Claret and Hugh report that Nuno Espirito Santo is expected to make two changes, with Valentin Castellanos starting up front and Edvin Austbo coming in on the left [2]. Both are attacking moves, which fits a side whose goals arrive in bursts rather than in a steady flow.

For Southampton, Football League World reports that Cyle Larin may miss the trip [7]. That is a doubt, not a decision: no confirmed absence has been announced. Goalkeeper Daniel Peretz said this week that the Saints must start turning draws into wins [8], and the Bolton game shows exactly why — 1.91 xG, no goal, two points dropped at home.

Four-fifths of a goal better

The win chances, with the bookmakers' margin removed across the ten firms we compare, are West Ham 56.8%, draw 21.9% and Southampton 21.3%. The Hammers are more than two and a half times as likely to win as the visitors. That is consistent with a top-two side at home against a team sitting outside the play-off places.

Finishers against creators

Southampton's season has a clear shape: they create enough to win games and have not finished the job. They did it against Bolton and they did it at Wrexham, and the numbers say the same thing each time. West Ham are the opposite kind of team — patient, then sudden, with the 6-0 at Lincoln as the loud example and the 1-1 with QPR as the warning at home.

The market has joined those two profiles together. West Ham are 56.8% favourites, more than two and a half times Southampton's 21.3%, and the goals line is high because both teams create chances. Southampton can make this uncomfortable for long spells, but the side that scores in clusters is the one more likely to take the points.
