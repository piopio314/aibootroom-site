Six straight defeats: Samsunspor host Amed with a record in sight

A club record waiting on the pitch

Samsunspor go into Saturday's home match against Amed with six straight Süper Lig defeats behind them and a piece of club history in front of them. Turkish media report that a seventh loss in a row would be a new club record [2]. They are 17th of 18 with four points from eight games [1].

The club's answer was a change of coach. Nenad Bjelica replaced Thorsten Fink with the side second from bottom, according to Austrian outlet LAOLA1 [3]. His first match brought a 4-1 defeat at Kasımpaşa, and Turkish media report that the problems have not been solved [4]. Gazetevatan describes a bleak mood, with the board and fans alarmed by the threat of relegation [5].

Amed come with the opposite story. They are second, unbeaten in five league games, and their coach Besnik Hasi is taking nothing for granted. He called Samsunspor "a wounded animal" and said wounded animals are the most dangerous [9]. He also said this match and the next one matter more to him right now than the meeting with Galatasaray that follows [8].

Two goals scored, fourteen conceded

Samsunspor's run is bad at both ends. They have scored two goals and conceded 14 in their last five league games, and the chance numbers say something similar: 1.21 expected goals created per game against 1.69 allowed. The home defeat by Trabzonspor is the clearest example — 1.66 xG created and a 0-3 loss. Even in the 0-1 at Kocaelispor they made 1.74 xG and took nothing. They had six days to prepare for this one.

Amed's five games read WDWWD, with 13 scored and six conceded, and their chance numbers are stronger still: 2.14 xG created per game and only 0.84 allowed. Konyaspor were held to 0.25 xG last time out in the 1-0 win, with Mbaye Diagne scoring from a Dia Saba assist [10]. The one soft spot is on the road: both away games in this run finished 2-2. Turkish media note that Amed's 18 league goals are second only to Fenerbahçe [11], and they had seven days' rest.

One option back for the home side

There is some positive team news for Samsunspor. Jaures Assoumou is back in contention after a spell out with a hamstring injury, according to Turkish media [6]. For a team with two goals in five league games, any returning attacking option matters to Bjelica.

Bjelica has had a full week on the training ground, with sessions at the Nuri Asan facilities including a small-sided game [7]. Amed's camp sounds calmer: Hasi called his team a new side in the Süper Lig doing good work, and insisted he takes it match by match [12]. Diagne's goal against Konyaspor shows the threat they bring.

Favourites, but only by a quarter of a goal

The bookmakers we compare, 10 of them, make Amed the favourites with the margin removed: 40.7% for an away win, 32.2% for Samsunspor and 27.1% for a draw. That is a small gap for a side second against a side 17th, and home advantage is part of the reason. The handicap says it another way: Samsunspor are given a quarter-goal start, so a draw is not a full win for Amed, and the visitors are rated only about a quarter of a goal better.

The goals line sits at 2.5 and is split almost in half, with Over at 50.4%, and both teams to score at 55.3%. That fits the two attacks, because Amed have scored in every one of their last five and Samsunspor have conceded in each of theirs. The week's moves were small and all pointed the same way: Amed's win chance rose by two points, to 40.7%, while Samsunspor's fell by 1.4 points.

The gap in the table and the gap in the price

The football and the market tell the same story from different angles. Samsunspor's problem is not only the results but the shape of them: they create a little and concede a lot, and now they meet a side allowing 0.84 expected goals a game. Amed's unbeaten run and their defensive numbers make them the rightful favourites. But their two draws are why the market stops short of making them big favourites — a quarter of a goal, not a goal. The 2.5 line, split down the middle, points to a tight game rather than a rout. For Samsunspor, the immediate task is to stop a run of six straight defeats.
