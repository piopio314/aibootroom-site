A home game with an edge to it

Wolves are seventh in the Championship with 18 points from 11 games; Watford are twentieth with 11. On Saturday they meet at Molineux. That gap is why Wolves are 68.5% favourites to win, and it is also why this is not a calm afternoon for the home manager. Football Insider reports that sources say Cesar Peixoto is fighting for his future and could be sacked if Wolves fail to beat Watford [4].

A home match against a side that has lost four of its last five should be the fixture a coach wants. But Wolves have won two of their last five, and Tuesday's 1-1 draw with Portsmouth at Molineux [5] kept them seventh instead of pushing them up. The crowd knows the table. The bench knows the report.

Before kick-off there is a tribute to Kenny Jackett, who managed both clubs and passed away this summer [12]. Molineux will be still for a minute, then loud. Then the game asks its question: can Wolves' chances turn into goals against a Watford side conceding two expected goals a game?

Chances created, chances given away

Wolves' last five brought two wins, one draw and two defeats, with 5 goals scored and 6 conceded. The expected-goals numbers say more than the results. They created 2.23 against Bolton and lost 1-3; they created 2.02 at Burnley and won 2-1 [6]. Against Portsmouth on Tuesday they managed only 0.95 expected goals, well down from the 2.23 they created at home to Bolton. The chances are not the weekly problem — the finishing and the space they leave behind are.

Watford's run has the opposite shape: one win, four defeats, 4 scored and 12 conceded. They gave up 3.67 expected goals at Cardiff in a 1-5 loss and 1.94 at home to Burnley in a 0-4. An average of 2.0 expected goals conceded per game is the clearest reason Wolves are rated so far above them. The 2-0 win over Charlton [9] was a real improvement, with 2.87 xG for and 1.31 against, and Watford have had seven days to rest — three more than Wolves.

No centre-forward, no settled week

Wolves' forward line is the issue. Adam Armstrong has missed the last two matches with a back injury and Raul Jimenez is out until at least the new year after surgery, according to the Express & Star [3]. Kieran Trippier was left out of the Portsmouth squad too, and both he and Armstrong will be checked before Saturday [2]; Peixoto said he is hopeful they will be fit [1]. Until they are, teenage striker Sean Neave has started up front twice in a row [7].

Watford list no absences of note, and their mood is mixed rather than broken. A supporters' group published an open letter declaring a complete loss of confidence in the club's owner [10], while defender Mattie Pollock said the players need to roll up their sleeves and fight [11]. After the Charlton win, head coach Alessio Dionisi said he was very happy and that the group stays together [8].

Minus 1.25, and a goals line of three

Across the eight bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Wolves are 68.5% to win, the draw is 19.1% and Watford 12.4%. That makes Wolves more than five times as likely to take the three points. The handicap says it in goals: the fair line is Wolves -1.34 and the main line is -1.25. In plain terms, the market sees Wolves about one and a third goals the better team, so a one-goal win would fall short of the expectation.

The goals line is 3.0, with Over at 49.9% — close to a coin flip on three or more. Both teams to score sits at 52.0%. That fits both profiles: Watford's last five matches produced 16 goals, and Wolves have conceded six in five while fielding a makeshift forward line. Sky Sports reports that Watford's 2-0 win over Charlton [9] ended a four-match losing run, but they are 12.4% to win and the handicap is Wolves -1.25. One good afternoon does not outweigh four defeats and 12 goals conceded.

The pressure is on the favourite

The football and the market point the same way, and that is what makes the afternoon awkward. Wolves' 68.5% and the -1.25 line are built on Watford conceding 2.0 expected goals a game and losing four of their last five, not on Wolves' attack, which is without Jimenez until the new year after surgery and waiting on Armstrong's back injury, as the Express & Star reported [3], with the BBC reporting he may be fit [1] and the Express & Star noting a teenager asked to lead the line [7]. The market points to a comfortable Wolves win, not just a win. The pressure sits entirely on the home bench: a narrow victory would help the table and still fall short of the -1.25 line, and a draw would help nobody.
