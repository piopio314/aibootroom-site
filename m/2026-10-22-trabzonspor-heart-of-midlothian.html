A four-win run meets a seven-goal wound

Trabzonspor have won their last four matches, scoring 13 goals and conceding two, and the last of those wins was a 2-0 derby against Beşiktaş on Sunday [1]. Hearts arrive in Trabzon four days after losing 7-1 at Celtic, with one win in their past five in all competitions [9].

That contrast is the whole match. Trabzonspor are 3rd of 36 in the Conference League phase after a 4-2 win at KuPS, and a second win would put them in the qualifying places early [6]. Hearts are 19th on one point after a 1-1 home draw with Nordsjaelland, and another defeat would leave them chasing the second half of the phase.

The subplot is Mohamed Salah, who has eight goals in the opening eight Süper Lig rounds and scored again in the derby [4]. Wouter Vrancken warned his players not to fixate on him because Trabzonspor carry other threats too [10] — a warning that is easier to give than to follow for the full match.

Hearts create chances; Hearts also concede them

Trabzonspor's run is built on chances, not finishing luck. They created 2.21 xG per game across their last five, with the 4-0 win over Galatasaray and the 4-2 at KuPS inside that stretch, and only the 0-1 at Konyaspor in September spoiled the sequence.

Hearts' numbers are stranger. They average 2.08 xG created, only a little less than Trabzonspor, and their 5-0 win over St Mirren came with 3.9 xG. But they also allowed 4.16 xG at Celtic Park, so the 7-1 scoreline matched the chances rather than flattering Celtic. Trabzonspor had three days of rest to Hearts' four, so freshness is not where the visitors lose ground.

Onuachu out, Hearts thin in midfield

Thomas Reis has ruled striker Paul Onuachu out of the Hearts match with the injury he picked up against KuPS, ng.headtopics.com reports [2]. That removes a centre-forward option for a side the market makes -1.75 favourites on the handicap, so the scoring may fall to Salah, the wide players and the midfield runners.

Hearts are short of midfield options for the trip to Turkey, according to the Edinburgh Evening News [8]. If that shortage holds, it is the hardest place to be stretched against a side with 13 goals in four wins. Teenager James Wilson said he grew up watching Salah and has no fear of facing him [11].

73.5%, and a handicap that keeps climbing

The fair win chances are 73.5% Trabzonspor, 15.1% draw and 11.4% Hearts. That makes a home win roughly six times more likely than an away win. The handicap says the same thing in goals: the main line is Trabzonspor -1.75 and the fair line has moved from -1.48 to -1.67, so the market now rates them about 1.7 goals better. Even so, a two-goal home win is not treated as a formality.

The goals line is where the result and the game part company. The fair total sits at 3.32, over 3.25 is 51.6% and both teams to score is 53.6% — an open night is expected even though the winner is not really in doubt. The week's biggest move was Trabzonspor's win chance, up from 69.0% to 73.5%, with the draw and Hearts both drifting. The handicap moved at the same moment and in the same direction, consistent with Sunday's derby win and the four-game run. Onuachu's absence did not stop that move, which suggests the market reads it as a squad detail.

What we said before the derby

Three days ago we called Trabzonspor against Beşiktaş a derby the market could not split, with the home side at 38.1% and Beşiktaş at 37.1%. Trabzonspor won 2-0, so the narrow home lean landed. We were less right about the goals: we leaned towards an open game on a 3.25 line and the match produced two, finishing under. The reminder for this one is that a two-goal home margin can arrive without a high-scoring night.

A settled result, an unsettled scoreline

Everything points one way for the favourite: four straight wins against one win in five, 2.21 xG per game against a defence that shipped seven at Celtic, and a market that has moved towards the hosts all week to 73.5%. Hearts' midfield shortage reported by Edinburgh News [8] and Onuachu's absence [2] both matter, but only one of them changes the shape of the night — the visitors are the side weakened. The 3.32 goals line is the part still open: Hearts create 2.08 xG per game, so the 7-1 looks like the outlier rather than the rule, and Trabzonspor's own matches have been open. The result is the more likely one; the scoreline is not.
