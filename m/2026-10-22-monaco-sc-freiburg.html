Two teams level on points, both coming off a first defeat

Monaco and Freiburg meet on Thursday with identical Conference League records: one win each, three points each, and places 9th and 7th in the 36-team table because of goal difference. Both also lost their unbeaten league start on Sunday. The winner at the Stade Louis II gets an early advantage in a league phase where every point changes the route.

Monaco's defeat was the one that raised questions. They lost 1-0 at Lorient, ending their run as Ligue 1's only unbeaten side [3], and created just 0.6 expected goals against Lorient's 1.74. That number has followed the team into this match, and the mood around it was visible in the coach's words. Asked about the favourites tag, Filipe Luís said: "Favourites? I don't think so… you only win titles in May and June." [4]

Freiburg's weekend was less severe. They lost 2-1 at Bayer Leverkusen, their first Bundesliga defeat of the season [7], while creating 1.21 xG away at one of the country's strongest sides. There has been noise off the pitch too: Monaco authorities imposed restrictions on Freiburg fans travelling to and staying in the city, and the club filed for an injunction, according to Sportschau [9]. On the pitch, Julian Schuster sees similarities between the two teams, according to SWR [10].

Freiburg create almost a goal more per game

The recent numbers point one way. Freiburg average 1.98 xG across their last five matches and concede 1.10; inside those games are a 5-0 home win over Borussia Mönchengladbach (2.6 xG against 0.19) and 3.09 xG away at Eintracht Frankfurt. Monaco's last five read differently: 1.18 xG created and 1.33 allowed, with only one match above 1.75 xG. Over a run of games Freiburg have been the better attacking side by nearly a whole goal per game.

Neither team has a rest advantage — both played Sunday, four days ago, and both used a midweek trip for their opening European win. The table explains part of the closeness of this one: level on three points, separated only by goal difference, with the away side ranked marginally higher. Monaco's route to the game is home ground; Freiburg's is the quality of the chances they have been making.

Lienhart out, Monaco's list already known

Freiburg will be without defender Philipp Lienhart, who misses the match through load management, a club spokesperson confirmed to SWR [6]. Load management usually points to rotation rather than injury, but it still removes a regular from the back line on a night when the defence faces a home side that needs a response after creating 0.6 xG at the weekend. Freiburg have conceded 1.10 xG per game over their last five.

Monaco's absences are older news. Eric Dier and Stanis Idumbo are out [1], with the club reporting three players missing and no new injuries ahead of the game [2]. A settled group matters for a coach who has spent the week answering questions about a first defeat. Filipe Luís was direct about the aim: "We will just think about this one. We have to try and win tomorrow. That is the main goal." [5]
