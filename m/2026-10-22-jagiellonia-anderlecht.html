Zero points, and still the favourites

Anderlecht travel to Poland with nothing to show for their Europa League season so far. Two games, two defeats: 1-2 at home to Lyon, then 0-1 away to Dinamo Zagreb, which leaves them 29th of 36 in the league phase [6]. On Sunday they lost 1-2 at Beerschot and went out of the Belgian Cup [10].

That is normally the profile of a team in trouble. Yet on Thursday they are the favourites. Jagiellonia have lost four of their last five matches in all competitions, and the one win in that run came at home to Ararat-Armenia in this competition [4]. In the league they have gone down to Górnik Zabrze, Legia Warszawa and, on Saturday, Zagłębie Lubin, beaten 0-1 by a VAR penalty [1].

So the points situation is uneven in an odd way. Jagiellonia have three points from two European games and sit 17th. Anderlecht have none, and a third straight defeat would leave them with very little to play for in the remaining league-phase nights. The home side, meanwhile, need to stop a domestic slide that Polish media have been quick to point out [5].

Four defeats in five, and the numbers behind them

The 3-1 win over Ararat-Armenia is the outlier in Jagiellonia's run: 2.1 expected goals for and 1.02 against, the only time in five matches they scored three. Around it sit defeats. Against Legia they created 2.3 xG and still lost 1-3. Against Górnik they created 0.59 and lost by the same score.

Across the five games they average 1.39 xG created and 1.88 conceded, and those 16 goals in five matches are part of why the market expects chances at both ends. Anderlecht's numbers are calmer: 1.49 for, 1.26 against, with the Zagreb defeat the clear low point at 1.1 against 2.11. Both teams had four days between their last match and this one.

Wojtuszek out, Biancone back

Jagiellonia's most damaging absence is at the back. Norbert Wojtuszek, a defender with 759 minutes this season across 13 appearances, is out — a hole in exactly the area where their numbers are weakest, at 1.88 xG conceded per game. Prelec, 695 minutes with three goals and three assists, is also unavailable.

Their coach Adrian Siemieniec is serving a six-match Polish league suspension after a scandal, but according to DH Les Sports he will be allowed to sit on the bench on Thursday [3]. Anderlecht's news is better: Giulian Biancone, 487 minutes this season, trained with the squad that travelled to Poland and is set to return to action in Białystok [7]. Sardella and Koutsoupias remain out. Asked about a crisis after the cup exit, Vitor Bruno said: "I don't see the situation like that" [11], and he spoke this week about how much he thinks at night about making the team better [9].

The read

The football and the price agree on the shape of this game. Anderlecht are the better side by the underlying numbers — 1.49 xG created against 1.39, and 1.26 conceded against 1.88 — but zero points in Europe and a cup exit stop that from becoming anything bigger than a quarter-goal tilt. Jagiellonia, at home, with their coach on the bench and one good European night behind them, are close enough that the draw at 26.5% is a real outcome. The market's read: a tight game with chances at both ends, and Anderlecht the favourites without being a majority shot.
