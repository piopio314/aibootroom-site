A mismatch with one loose thread

Leverkusen come to Crete with five wins in a row and 14 goals in those five games. OFI come with three straight defeats and 11 goals conceded across their last five matches. The market reads it as a wide mismatch rather than a difficult away trip.

The home side have one argument, and it is a strong one. In September they beat Hoffenheim 2-0 at home in this competition, according to onefootball.com [6]. That night is why nobody around this fixture treats Thursday as a formality.

The league phase table adds context. Leverkusen are second in the 36-team table with six points from two games, scoring six more than they have conceded. OFI are 18th with three points from two. The clubs have no previous meeting to lean on.

What the xG says behind the run

Leverkusen's streak is not only about results. They created 5.22 expected goals in the 4-0 win at Lech Poznan and allowed 0.57 — the clearest evidence behind a handicap set near two goals. Across the five wins they average 2.63 xG created and 1.36 allowed.

There is a warning inside the run. The 4-3 win at Mainz came with 3.13 xG allowed, so this is a team that gives up chances when the game opens. OFI's own numbers are split the same way: 0.12 xG at Rennes and 0.21 at AEK Athens, barely a chance against organised sides, but 1.98 in Sunday's 3-4 defeat at Kalamata. Both teams had four days of rest.

Squad calls, not a broken attack

Leverkusen are without forward C. Kofane, who has a muscle injury and three goals in six appearances this season, in 176 minutes on the pitch. According to bulinews.com, Jonas Hofmann took Martin Terrier's place in the Europa League squad and Victor Boniface — 12 minutes this season — was left out for the Greece trip [3], a report guardian.ng also carries [4].

Patrik Schick's scoring helped extend the run to five at Freiburg, reported by sports.yahoo.com [2], and the goals have been spread across the front line. OFI's absentees are squad players rather than starters: Chatzigiovanis (168 minutes), Sielis (90), Leya Iseka (68), plus Marinakis with a groin injury.

With the bookmakers' margin removed, Leverkusen are 77.8% to win, the draw 13.6% and OFI 8.6%. The visitors are therefore more than nine times more likely than the home side. The handicap is OFI +2, with the market's fair line at 1.94 goals, meaning Leverkusen are rated about two goals the better team.

The goals line is 3.5, with over and under both at 50.0%, and both teams to score at 52.7%. That fits a side who have scored 14 in five going to a home team whose league meeting with Asteras Tripolis finished 2-2.

The run explains the price, the warning explains the line

Leverkusen's five wins, the 5.22 xG night in Poznan and OFI's near-empty chance creation in Rennes and Athens all point one way, and the market follows: a handicap of about two goals and a 3.5 goal line split straight down the middle. The counter-argument is that September night against Hoffenheim, proof that OFI can defend a home European game and take the chance when it comes.

Our read is that Leverkusen are clear favourites and the most likely winner by two goals or more. But an 8.6% home chance is not zero, and a team that has already beaten a Bundesliga side at home in this competition is the reason the line stops at two instead of going higher.
