A coach's verdict, a team's answer

Juventus lost 0-3 at home to Lazio on Sunday, and the sharpest moment of the afternoon came afterwards. Luciano Spalletti said his side were inferior and that he did not like the lack of tempo, ideas, running and desire to work hard [6]. Most of the home support had already gone, leaving the Allianz Stadium in the closing minutes [8].

That is the mood Juventus bring to Lecce. Italian media report that the club will stand by Spalletti for now, so the pressure sits on the players rather than on the bench [7]. Gianluigi Buffon said this week that the coach is not the problem and that Spalletti deserves time to turn it around [9].

Lecce come from the other side of the story. They won 3-0 at Udinese on Sunday, with goals from Kiolonda Gaspar and Konan N'Dri [3]. The table gives the match its edge: Juventus are 8th on 11 points, Lecce 12th on 9, two points apart after seven rounds.

Juventus' base, Lecce's swing

Juventus have won three of their last five in all competitions, and the Lazio night stands out for how little it looked like the rest. They created 0.75 expected goals and allowed 0.57, so by the chances they actually gave up the three-goal margin was a distortion rather than the story. Across the last five they have conceded only 0.69 expected goals per game — that defensive record helps explain why they are still rated highly.

Lecce are the more volatile team. Their 3.47 expected goals at Udinese was the highest of this run, but the same five games include a 0-3 at Milan with 0.21 xG created and a 0-1 at Cagliari with 0.5. They average 1.35 xG for and 1.82 against, and their last five matches have produced 15 goals in total. Both sides have had seven days since their last game, and Fabio Maresca has been appointed referee for the 18:00 kick-off [11].

Lecce's midfield is the open wound

Lecce's problem is in the middle of the pitch, exactly where Juventus will look to reassert control after Spalletti said the 3-0 home defeat to Lazio lacked tempo and ideas [6]. Coulibaly was forced off in the first half against Udinese, and Gazzetta have reported his scan results and recovery time [1]. Tuttosport report that Di Francesco is in an emergency for this match after a second midfielder was forced to stop, and that he must reshape the midfield [2]. Two absences in that area change how Lecce can press and how easily they can play out.

The route that survives a patched midfield is the set piece. According to Gazzetta, no team in Serie A has scored as many goals from corners as Lecce this season [4], and that is a threat Juventus will have prepared for. On the other side, reports say Juventus expect a quick turnaround across the Rennes, Lecce and Genoa matches [10] — a plan that only works if the response starts here.

The gap between the table and the price

The table says these teams are two points apart; the price says they are a goal apart. The market is weighing squad quality, a defence allowing 0.69 expected goals a game and a head-to-head record of one Lecce win, two draws and two Juventus wins in the last five, and it is treating Sunday's 0-3 as an event rather than a trend. Lecce's counter-argument is real: they arrive off their best attacking night of the run, and their corner routine can hurt anyone. But they must defend the middle of the pitch with a reshaped midfield, against a team whose coach has just questioned its desire in public. That is why a reaction is expected at Lecce — and why the home side's chance stays small even on a good week.
