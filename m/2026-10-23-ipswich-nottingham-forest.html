A bottom-three side the market likes

Nottingham Forest go to Portman Road on Friday in 19th place: five points from seven games, three straight Premier League defeats, one win all season. Ipswich are 10th with nine points and arrive from a 3-2 defeat at Manchester City in which they scored twice. By the table alone, the home side has the better argument.

The market sees it the other way. Forest are rated the more likely winners at 42.7%, against 29.5% for Ipswich, with the draw at 27.8%. That is not a huge gap, but it is a clear one, and it has nothing to do with the league table. It follows the chances: Forest have created 1.72 expected goals per game this season and conceded 1.22, while Ipswich have created 1.48 and conceded 2.05.

That is the tension of Friday night. One team keeps losing, the other keeps giving up good chances, and the table says one thing while the shot numbers say another. Forest also won 4-2 at Portman Road the last time these two met in the league, in March 2025 — Ipswich 2, Forest 4.

Chances better than results

Forest's three defeats tell different stories. The 1-3 at Crystal Palace is the one that matters most: 3.34 expected goals created, and they lost anyway. They made enough to win comfortably and the finishing let them down. The 0-1 at home to Arsenal last Sunday was tighter and lower on chances, 0.94 xG against 1.16 [4]. Before the run, they won 2-1 at Aston Villa with 2.28 xG, which is the version of Forest the price is built on.

Ipswich are the mirror image. They won 2-1 at home to Fulham while creating only 1.15 xG and allowing 1.81, but their 3-2 win at Crystal Palace was built on 1.67 xG against 0.62. Their points have come with mixed chance creation and a defence that gives up 2.05 xG per game. Gary O'Neil's side had six days to prepare; Forest had five.

Gibbs-White back, Lukic doubtful

The most important team news concerns Forest's main creator. Oliver Glasner confirmed that Morgan Gibbs-White is fit to face Ipswich [1]. Gibbs-White is Forest's highest-rated player this season at 7.47, with 540 minutes and three goal involvements, and his availability is the difference between all those created chances turning into goals or not. The Athletic reports that defender Nikola Milenkovic should also be fit for Friday [2], which points to a settled back line for a game where Forest will have to absorb home pressure.

Forest will be without forward Igor Jesus, out with a calf injury after 316 minutes and one goal in six league appearances. That thins the attacking options behind Gibbs-White but does not change the shape of the team. Ipswich have a doubt of their own: midfielder Svetozar Lukic has a groin problem after 502 minutes in seven games, one assist and a 6.7 rating. Losing him would take a senior body out of the department that has to shield a defence conceding 2.05 xG per game.

A lean, not a swing

The win chances above come from the bookmakers we compare, with the margin taken out across ten of them. The handicap tells the same story in a different way: Ipswich are given a start of a quarter of a goal, which means Forest are rated about a quarter of a goal the better side even away from home. That is a lean towards the visitors, not a gulf. The total-goals line sits at 2.75, with over priced at 48.8% — the market expects something just under three goals, and both teams to score is rated 58.9%.

The clearest recent move on Ipswich was small: their win chance slipped by 1.3 percentage points to 29.5%, and the fair goals line eased from 2.76 to 2.70, a move toward fewer goals. The win chance is a lean towards Forest, not a swing — the numbers still keep Ipswich within touching distance, but the away side is clearly favoured.

The read

Forest are 19th and losing every week, and they are still the side the market rates to win at Portman Road — because they create more than Ipswich and concede far less, and because their best player is fit for it. Ipswich score enough to stay in games and concede enough to lose them, which is why a home side above Forest in the table is only a small underdog rather than a level one. The football says the visitors are the better team on chances; the table says they have been the worse team on results. Friday is the argument between those two facts, and the price sits with the chances.
