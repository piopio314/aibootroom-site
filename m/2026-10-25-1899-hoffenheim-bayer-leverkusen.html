The best run meets the wrong fixture

Bayer Leverkusen come to Hoffenheim on Sunday with five wins in a row, 14 goals scored and four conceded. Their chance creation is the loudest number here: 2.63 expected goals per game across that run. Hoffenheim are 14th with six points from six games and have lost four of their last five.

The obvious reading is a comfortable away win. The market does not read it that way. Leverkusen are favourites at 41.6%, Hoffenheim are 34.6% and the draw is 23.9% — a gap of seven percentage points, not the kind of margin you would expect from a top-four side visiting a bottom-half one.

The reason sits in the fixture's own history. Hoffenheim beat Leverkusen 1-0 here in January and won 2-1 in Leverkusen in August 2025, so the last two meetings both went against the visitors. Add Hoffenheim's need for a home result after two 2-3 defeats in front of their own crowd, and Sunday carries more tension than the table suggests.

Leverkusen's run is real, and not always clean

Leverkusen's five wins are not a string of lucky afternoons. They beat RB Leipzig 2-0 at home with 2.27 expected goals to 0.97, and they won 4-0 at Lech Poznan with 5.22. The warning is the 4-3 win at Mainz, where they allowed 3.13 expected goals — the chances against them have not always been small.

Hoffenheim's results are worse than their chances. Across the last five they averaged 1.36 expected goals for and 1.46 against, yet they conceded 12 goals. Sunday's 2-1 win at Borussia Mönchengladbach, 1.8 expected goals to 0.47, was their only win in that stretch [2]. Both home games ended 2-3, to Hamburg in the league and Beşiktaş in Europe — they score at home, then concede more.

The table gap is real but recent. Leverkusen are 4th with 13 points, level with third-placed Freiburg, after losing their opener to promoted Elversberg [5]. Hoffenheim are 14th with six points [3], which is why they are outsiders at home, but only just.

A report from Europe, not a team sheet

Hoffenheim's week brought a report rather than an announcement. According to Olympique-et-Lyonnais, Nathan De Cat, Patrick Wimmer and Albian Hajdari were unavailable for the Europa League game against Lyon [1]. If that keeps them out on Sunday, it takes three squad options from a side that has conceded 12 goals in five games.

At Leverkusen the name to note is Victor Boniface, left out of the squad for the Europa League trip to Greece, with the report noting his long-term knee injury from January 2026 [7]. That concerns a European squad rather than Sunday's eleven. Nothing else in this week's news changes the shape of either team.

A narrow favourite and a high total

The win chances are Leverkusen 41.6%, Hoffenheim 34.6% and draw 23.9%. In words, the market makes Leverkusen the better side by about a sixth of a goal. The handicap line is Hoffenheim +0.25, and the fair line is +0.16 — a quarter-goal start is enough to make the game level. Leverkusen have won their last five matches.

The goals line is 3.5, a full goal above the usual 2.5, with Over at 48.6% and Under just ahead at 51.4%. Both teams to score is priced at 70%, which fits two 2-3 home defeats for Hoffenheim and Leverkusen's 4-3 and 4-0.

Form says Leverkusen, the fixture says be careful

Almost everything separates these two teams except the things that decide one match. Leverkusen have the form, the chance volume and the higher table place; Hoffenheim have the home ground and the last two results in this fixture. The market settles it with a narrow favourite and a high total — Leverkusen more likely than not to win, but only about a sixth of a goal better, in a game priced for goals. The form points one way; the fixture points the other.
