A club in crisis meets a club in two halves

Borussia Mönchengladbach arrive in Stuttgart with zero points from six Bundesliga games, the worst start in the club's history [7]. A seventh straight defeat on Friday would be the worst start to a Bundesliga season on record [10]. The mood around the club is easy to read: after the Hoffenheim defeat, Bild wrote that Gladbach are driving fans away [11]. According to goal.com, there is unrest inside the team too, with reports of a "broken dressing room" [9].

Stuttgart are the mirror image in Europe and a strange one at home. They are unbeaten in the Champions League with seven points from three games, after a 1-1 draw with 10-man Atlético Madrid on Tuesday [2]. In the Bundesliga they are 13th with six points from six and lost at Hamburger SV on Saturday, their fourth league defeat of the season [1]. The club's own task, as bundesliga.com puts it, is to carry the European form into the league, starting on Friday [6].

That is what makes this a real game rather than a formality. Stuttgart have the better side and the better chances, but they have won only two league matches all season, so nothing here is settled. Gladbach, with nothing left to protect, also arrive with five days of rest against Stuttgart's three.

Stuttgart's chances are there; the points are not

The league table hides a lot about the home side. Stuttgart created 2.28 expected goals at Hamburger SV and scored none; they created 2.47 at Paderborn and won 2-0. Across their last five matches in all competitions they average 1.89 xG and allow 1.31, so the two league defeats in that run came in games where the chances were there. The problem is finishing, and now a short turnaround: three days between the Atlético draw and this match.

Gladbach's numbers are even worse than their results. Over their last five Bundesliga matches they are conceding 2.17 expected goals per game and have shipped 17 goals in that run. Against Hoffenheim they made 0.47 xG and lost 2-1; the 5-0 defeat at Freiburg came with 0.19 xG. There was one bright attacking night, a 3-4 home defeat to Mainz with 3.24 xG, but the pattern is clear — they do not keep the ball out and rarely make enough. History agrees: Stuttgart have won four of the last five meetings, including 3-0 in Mönchengladbach in January.

A doubt at full-back, a longer list in the away room

Stuttgart's biggest question is at right-back. Vagnoman is doubtful after 778 minutes this season, with two goals and two assists — exactly the player a dominant home side uses to keep width. Tomas (148 minutes) is out with a muscle injury, and Diehl and Zagadou are also missing. According to yardbarker.com, the poor league start has had reported consequences for the squad, with heavy fines for anyone ignoring the club's measures [3]. Prömel said, per sportschau.de, that the Atlético performance has to be the benchmark [5].

Gladbach's list is longer and it hurts where they are weakest. Honorat, with three assists in only 265 minutes, is doubtful; Itakura, a starting defender with 387 minutes, is doubtful too. Kleindienst, Leszczynski, Leopold and Fleck are out. For a team already conceding more than two expected goals a game, the possible loss of a first-choice defender and their most productive wide player is the last thing they need.

One and a half goals, and a line that expects more

Stuttgart are 69.8% favourites, with the draw at 16.4% and Gladbach at 13.7% — Stuttgart are about five times as likely as Gladbach. The handicap line is 1.5 goals, so Stuttgart are rated about one and a half goals better and would need to win by two or more. The total goals line is 3.75, with the over at 50.9%, and the market has both teams to score at 65% — the goals expectation in a game featuring a side that has conceded 17 goals in its last five league matches.

The price has moved a little towards Stuttgart this week: their chance is up 1.9 percentage points to 69.8%, and the fair handicap line moved from -1.41 to -1.49. That is consistent with a market reacting to the away side's crisis more than to anything new at Stuttgart — whose own league form is why the number stays below 70% rather than climbing higher.

The pressure sits in the away dressing room

Zero points, a historic worst start, 17 goals conceded in five league games: Gladbach's season is already an emergency, and Friday sends them to a team unbeaten in Europe that has won four of the last five meetings between the clubs. Stuttgart's own table is the counterweight — 13th, six points, four defeats — and it is why this is strong home favouritism rather than a certainty. The football says Stuttgart will have the ball and the chances; the market says the same, and adds goals. Both agree on one thing: the pressure this week belongs entirely to the visitors.
