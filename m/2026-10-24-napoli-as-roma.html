A winning night, then a warning

Napoli won 4-3 in the Champions League on Tuesday, their first victory of this season's competition [2]. Within minutes of the whistle, Max Allegri was calling his own team "too fragile" [4]. Three days later, unbeaten Roma arrive at the Maradona.

That is the tension of this fixture. Roma are third in Serie A with 17 points from seven games, five wins and two draws. Napoli are ninth with ten points and three defeats, and the coach's position was discussed in public before Tuesday's win: the club's director said Allegri "is absolutely not in doubt" after the president asked to be "reassured" [1].

The market looks at all of it and makes Roma narrow favourites: 37.8% against Napoli's 34.4%, with the draw on 27.7%. That is close to a coin-flip between the two sides, and it fits a fixture Napoli have not lost in any of the last five meetings, two wins and three draws.

Roma's points, Napoli's chances

Roma's league record is built on control rather than volume. Their only dropped points came in a 0-0 at Como and a 2-2 at home to Inter, and across their last five matches in all competitions they average 1.75 xG created against 1.24 allowed. Tuesday's 4-0 over Slovan Bratislava was their cleanest night: 3.19 xG created, 0.04 allowed, and four different scorers [7].

Napoli's numbers point the other way. They created 2.36 xG at Venezia on Saturday and lost 0-1, then beat Bodo/Glimt 4-3 while being out-created at home, 1.57 xG to 2.69. Their last five average 2.03 xG created, more than Roma, but 1.41 against, and both teams have had four days since their European games.

Dybala reported out, Buongiorno missing

Napoli's listed absentee is Buongiorno, out with a knee problem, which leaves the middle of the defence to deal with Roma's movement. The better news is Scott McTominay, who scored twice on Tuesday, his first goals since a procedure for a minor cardiac arrhythmia kept him out for a month [3].

Allegri also said Kevin De Bruyne "is going to really enjoy himself playing in front of the defence" [4], although Sky pundit Ugolini wrote that, missed penalty aside, De Bruyne does not seem to have "the attitude of someone who made the right choice in coming to Napoli" [6].

For Roma, Italian media report that Paulo Dybala is out and that Pellegrini's injury complicates Gasperini's choices in attacking midfield, with Bryan Cristante's return after a knee problem the positive note in the same report [8]. Gasperini had already rested Malen on Tuesday with this league game in mind [9], and Bah is also listed as unavailable.

Level line, narrow favourite

The win chances, with the bookmakers' margin removed and taken from the ten bookmakers we compare, are Roma 37.8%, Napoli 34.4% and the draw 27.7%. The handicap line is level: the market sees the two teams as almost equal, and a draw would leave them there. The goals line sits at 2.54, with over 2.5 at 50.9%, more likely than not to be a game of three goals or more, and both teams to score at 56.1%.

The week's movement is small: Roma's win chance slipped about a point and Napoli's rose by a similar amount, so Roma remain narrow favourites but by less than before. The handicap line held at level.

Why the gap stays small

The football and the price say the same thing in different words. Roma are the better side by the table, unbeaten and tighter at the back; Napoli are at home, have not lost to them in five meetings, and have calmed the noise around their coach for now. That is why the visitors are favourites and not more, why the handicap is level, and why a game with three goals or more is the more likely outcome. A Roma win would confirm the table; a Napoli win would fit the head-to-head record.
