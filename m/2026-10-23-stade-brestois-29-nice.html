One point apart, and a night for Eric Roy

Brest and Nice meet on Friday with almost nothing between them. Brest are 10th in Ligue 1 with 9 points from seven games, Nice are 13th with 8, and only one point separates the two clubs [12]. The winner moves above the other, so a mid-table fixture carries a direct swing.

The evening means more than the table. Friday comes four months after the death of Eric Roy, who coached both clubs, and the game will be an occasion to pay tribute to him, according to Radio France [9].

The football is tight as well. Brest have won two of seven, Nice two and lost three. Neither side has pulled away, and both come into the match after a week in which small details decided the result.

Brest's home punch, Nice's away trouble

Brest's best day came at home to Angers on 10 October: a 5-1 win with 3.82 expected goals created — expected goals measure the quality of the chances a team makes. That is the clearest case for their favouritism, because at home they turn pressure into real chances. The other recent games are less flattering: at Lille on Saturday they drew 1-1 with 0.78 expected goals for and 2.17 against.

Nice lost 2-1 at Lyon on Sunday, and a stoppage-time equaliser from Wahi was ruled out by centimetres [8]. The expected goals were almost level, 1.61 to 1.60, so the performance was closer than the score. Their two away games in the last five, at Auxerre and at Lyon, both ended in defeat, while both home games since September ended in wins. Brest have had six days of rest, Nice five.

A settled defender gone from the Brest line

Brest lose Raphaël Le Guen to a broken leg. He had played all 450 league minutes this season, so this is a starter taken out of the middle of the defence. Julien Lachuer said only Le Guen and Brendan Chardonnet should miss the visit of Nice through injury [1], and at Lille the central pair was Gautier Lloris and Lucas Tousart [5]. That is the likely shape again.

Giovani Versini stopped training on Wednesday and is doubtful, though his coach has not fully ruled him out of the squad, according to Ouest-France [2]; a separate report from Morning Foot said he had returned to training [3]. Nice's absentee list runs to five names, but the minutes are thin: Mendy has 124 this season, Abdi 47, Sanson three. Keito Nakamura was sent off at Lyon, a red card Le Progrès reported as costing Nice dear [10]. Nice coach Olivier Pantaloni has flagged Brest striker Ludovic Ajorque as the threat to neutralise, according to Morning Foot [4].

Why the draw stays live

Brest's home form is the reason they are favoured: 3.82 expected goals against Angers is the standout number of their season so far. But the -0.25 line is honest about the gap, and Nice's away defeats at Auxerre and Lyon are the counterweight. With Le Guen out of the Brest back line and Nice missing mostly fringe players by minutes, the teams are closer than the one-point table gap suggests. Brest are narrow favourites, the 2.75 goals line points to a controlled game, and the draw is a real outcome.
