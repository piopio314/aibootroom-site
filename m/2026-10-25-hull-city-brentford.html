A top-six side with a hole in the middle

Brentford are sixth in the Premier League with 10 points from seven games and travel to Hull City on Sunday. They will do it without Yehor Yarmolyuk and Vitaly Janelt, both sent off in the second half of the home defeat to Liverpool [2]. Two central midfielders missing before a game you are expected to win is the sort of detail that shapes a Sunday afternoon.

Hull are ninth with nine points and have not won in five: two draws and three defeats. The run reads worse than it is, because all three of those defeats came away from home — at Fulham on Saturday, at Newcastle, and in the League Cup at Sunderland. At home they drew 2-2 with Everton and created 1.33 xG.

What Hull carry into this one is a public calling-out. Sergej Jakirović told his players that their first half at Fulham was one of the worst halves of football he had seen since taking charge, and one player said he totally agreed [8]. Hull Live's line is that they simply must respond in front of their own crowd on Sunday [12].

Hull's problem is creating, not just losing

The Fulham numbers are the real worry. Hull created 0.16 xG at Craven Cottage — almost nothing — against a season average of 1.11 per game. That makes Saturday an extreme even by their own standards, but it also shows where the pressure sits: the attack, not only the results.

Brentford's numbers are mixed in a different way. They have scored 10 goals in their last five, including a 3-0 win over Chelsea with 2.65 xG, but they have also conceded 1.62 xG per game across those five while creating 1.27. Four draws in seven league games fits that: a real advantage in points, not control of matches. Both teams had eight days since their last game. The clubs last met in February 2020, when Brentford won 5-1 at Hull in the Championship.

Two suspensions, and a striker short of goals

The absences are certain rather than doubtful. Yarmolyuk and Janelt were both dismissed in the second half against Liverpool and are suspended for the trip to Hull, as the BBC reported [2]; Brentford confirmed they will be without two players for Sunday [1]. Their central options are thinner than in the start that took them to sixth, and the supply line matters: Igor Thiago has one goal and one assist in seven Premier League games this season, according to Sky Sports [5].

On the Hull side, Hull Live reports an update on a striker after a significant injury concern, described as a timely boost before the Brentford match [10]. Jakirović also arranged a midweek run-out against second-tier opposition so players could press a case for Sunday [11]. One Hull supporter told BBC Sport he is not panicking yet, and sees the Brentford suspensions as a reason for hope [13].

Clear favourites, not a rout

With the margin removed, the win chances are Brentford 54.8%, draw 23.9% and Hull 21.3%. Brentford are more than twice as likely to win as the home side. The handicap shows how strong that is and where it stops: the fair line sits at +0.72 goals, so Brentford are rated about 0.7 goals the better team. That is a clear gap, not a rout — which fits four draws in seven.

The goals line is 2.75, with Over at 51.8% and both teams to score at 56.3%, so the market leans only slightly towards an open game. Brentford's win chance rose 1.5 points in recent days, and the -0.75 away handicap moved 1.7 points in the same direction — consistent with the two suspensions being treated as coverable rather than a reason to bring Hull closer.

The same story from two angles

The football and the price point the same way. Brentford are the better team by the table, by results and by the handicap, and losing two midfielders has not changed that view. Hull's problem is creation: 0.16 xG at Fulham was an extreme, but their season average is modest too, and a response game against a side that concedes chances is their best route back. Brentford are clear favourites on Sunday; what the price does not say is that they will find it easy.
