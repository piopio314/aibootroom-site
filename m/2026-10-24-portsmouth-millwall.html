Same points, same problems

Twelfth plays thirteenth at Fratton Park in a Saturday lunchtime kick-off [12], and the table offers no separation at all. Portsmouth and Millwall both have 15 points from 11 games, both have won four, drawn three and lost four, and both have a goal difference of zero. A win settles the argument between two clubs whose seasons are running in parallel.

The tension is not in the league positions but in the squad lists. Portsmouth are missing at least 10 players through injury, and John Mousinho said this week that he hopes to have bodies back for this fixture, according to portsmouth.co.uk [1]. Millwall's problem is concentrated in one place: goalkeeper Filip Marschall is out for an extended spell with ankle ligament damage [6].

So the game becomes a test of depth rather than of starting quality. Portsmouth have the home crowd and a habit of staying in matches. Millwall have the fresher legs and, on the underlying numbers, the better chance creation. Neither side can call on its first-choice eleven.

One defeat in seven, one comeback

Portsmouth have lost once in their last seven Championship matches [2]. That run includes a 3-2 home win over Sheffield United and a 1-0 win at Norwich, and it continued on Tuesday when Terry Devlin equalised late at Wolves for 1-1. The xG in that game was low — 0.78 for Portsmouth, 0.95 for Wolves — but the pattern of the run is a team that keeps the game alive to the last minutes.

Millwall's chances are better than their results. They created 1.87 xG against Lincoln and came back from two goals down to draw 2-2 [9], and they made 2.68 xG in another 2-2 with West Ham. Across the last five, Millwall average 1.68 xG for and 1.32 against; Portsmouth average 1.36 and 1.0. Even so, Millwall have had seven days to prepare for this lunchtime kick-off while Portsmouth had four.

Who is actually available

Portsmouth's absentee list is long and it takes real minutes with it. Bishop has played 572 minutes across 10 appearances this season, Arthur 447 minutes at the back, and the list continues past them. Mousinho had already planned for this game: he rested Pack, Swanson, Kavuma-McQueen and Milovanovic at Wolves, according to portsmouth.co.uk [3], and Thomas Waddingham could return on Saturday [4].

Millwall's goalkeeper situation is the single clearest problem. Marschall's injury against Lincoln means a fifth change of goalkeeper this season, reported by portsmouth.co.uk [7], with Jensen also out. Further forward, Arconte (3 goals in 638 minutes) is doubtful with an ankle problem and Coburn (2 goals in 380 minutes) is out, so the attack behind that 1.68 xG average may be missing both of its main finishers.

Our read: fitness decides it

This fixture pulls in two directions. Two clubs with the same record, the same points and the same goal difference, and a market that rates them level. Portsmouth have the home ground and the better recent habit of not losing, but they also have the shorter week and the longer injury list. Millwall create the better chances and have had seven days to prepare, but they may travel without both first-choice forwards and with another new goalkeeper.

Level in the table, level in the market, and availability decides it. That is why Portsmouth's 36.1% reads as a favourite's rating and not a confident one — a home side patched together, against an away side that makes chances but may not have the players to finish them.
