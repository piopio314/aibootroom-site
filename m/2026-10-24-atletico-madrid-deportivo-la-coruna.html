A run ends, another test begins

Atletico Madrid have won five of their last six, and the run was interrupted on Tuesday night in Stuttgart, where they drew 1-1 and finished with ten men [1]. The next fixture comes quickly: Deportivo La Coruna at the Metropolitano, four days after a Champions League away trip, in front of a home crowd that expects the run to restart.

Deportivo arrive in better condition and in a stranger mood. They have not won in five La Liga games — three draws and two defeats — but those draws came against Levante, Betis and Getafe, matches they were in until the end. They have also had eight days to prepare, exactly twice Atletico's four rest days, which is the one thing clearly in the visitors' favour.

The subplot is José María Giménez. The centre-back spent more than a decade at Atletico and faces them for the first time in a Deportivo shirt; according to Mundo Deportivo, he asked that no fear clause be included in the move [6]. He has also said his Atletico chapter is over [7], which makes this a football problem rather than a sentimental one — the away defence will be organised by the man who knows Atletico's forwards best.

Deportivo's numbers are better than their results

Atletico's last five matches produced 1.65 xG for and 0.94 against, with Stuttgart the one game where the balance flipped against them (0.91 xG to 1.63). In the league the record is cleaner — seven wins, one draw, one defeat, 22 points, second behind Barcelona, according to onefootball.com [5], with a +11 goal difference.

Deportivo's five winless games tell a different story from the table. They average 1.27 xG across them and have scored five goals from that; at Real Sociedad they created 2.13 xG and still lost 3-2. Ninth on 11 points, they stay in games without closing them out — which is why they are priced as long outsiders here rather than as a side sliding away.

Giménez against his old club

There is one reported absence for Atletico: a media report from www.livescore.com says a defender is set to miss this game and should return a week later at Valencia [4]. For Deportivo, the tactical question is whether Giménez, who knows Atletico's patterns better than anyone on the pitch, can hold a back line that will be tested repeatedly.

At the other end, Julián Álvarez scored his first goal of the season in Stuttgart [2]. Álex Grimaldo said afterwards that Álvarez is Atletico's best player and had been creating chances for weeks before the goal [3]. A striker off the mark matters in a game where the price asks Atletico to win by more than one goal.

Favourites, but by how much

Atletico are 74.3% to win, the draw 16.4% and Deportivo 9.4% — roughly eight times the visitors' chance. That is consistent with second against ninth, five straight league wins against none in five, and a home ground where Atletico have already beaten Real Madrid this season.

The handicap is Atletico -1.75 — Deportivo start 1.75 goals ahead — with the fair line at -1.67. In practice the line asks Atletico to win by two goals or more, while their recent wins have mostly come by a single goal, including 2-1 and 1-0 scorelines. The goals line is 3.0, with Over at 49.2% and both teams to score at 46.7%.

Atletico's win chance has slipped 1.5 points and the Over 2.6 points, while the fair goals total has come down from 3.07 to 2.97 and the fair handicap from -1.78 to -1.67. The market still makes Atletico clear favourites, but it expects a tighter, lower-scoring win — consistent with a rested opponent and a fixture history of tight scorelines.

Control, not a rout

Atletico are clear favourites and the football agrees: the league position, the five straight league wins, the chance numbers of the last month. The caution is in the margin. A handicap of -1.75 and a goals total trimmed towards 2.97 both describe a controlled home win rather than a high-scoring one, and Deportivo's 1.27 xG average gives them a way to stay in the game. Our read: Atletico more likely than not to win, less likely to win big.
