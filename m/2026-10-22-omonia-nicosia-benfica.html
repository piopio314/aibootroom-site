Benfica take a perfect record to Nicosia to face unbeaten Omonia

Two perfect starts, one clear favourite

Thursday in Nicosia brings together two clubs with six points from two Europa League matches. Benfica have beaten AC Milan 2-0 away and Celtic 4-1 in Lisbon [3][4]. Omonia have won both of their league-phase games too, and arrive unbeaten in five in all competitions [8].

The points are level, but the table is not: Benfica sit third of 36 with a goal difference of +5, Omonia sixth with +4. Both are seeking to keep a perfect record in the third round [5], which makes this an unusual meeting between two sides who have not yet dropped anything.

The scale of the opposition is the story. Benfica's six points came against two of the biggest names in the competition; Omonia's came against Celta Vigo and Celje. Hugo Martins, the AEL Limassol coach, said Omonia can surprise at home but called Benfica clear favourites [10].

Omonia's wins cost more than they look

Omonia's last five read well: four wins and a draw, 13 goals scored and 3 conceded. They beat Celta Vigo 1-0 at home, while their win at Celje was far more comfortable.

Monday brought a 2-2 draw with Olympiakos Nicosia, level only after a stoppage-time equaliser [7]. That leaves three days between games for the home side, while Benfica have been resting since 15 October.

What Benfica's numbers say

Benfica average 2.07 expected goals across their last five matches and concede 1.04. The 4-1 over Celtic came with 3.22 xG and a 3-0 lead at half-time [4]; the 2-0 over Vitória SC came with 2.76 against 0.43. Their one defeat in that run was 1-3 at Porto.

One result needs context: the 2-0 win in Milan was earned with only 0.64 xG against 0.96, so Benfica can take a big away win without controlling the chances. They have had seven days to prepare, against Omonia's three.

Echeverri starts, Barrenechea stays home

Marco Silva confirmed in a press conference that Claudio Echeverri will start Thursday's match [1]. That settles one attacking question a day before kick-off. Portuguese outlet O Jogo reports that Benfica travelled without three players who stayed behind to prepare, with another also absent from the buildup [2].

Enzo Barrenechea is the absent midfielder who matters most: 11 appearances this season, 254 minutes, one assist and a rating of 7.04. Omonia's absentees — Eiting and Kitsos with knee injuries, plus Akintola — have not played this season, so their XI should look close to the side that has won four of five.

About one and a third goals better

The win chances, taken from the 10 bookmakers we compare with the margin removed, make Benfica 70.4% favourites, the draw 17.7% and Omonia 11.9% — nearly six times smaller than the visitors' chance. The handicap gives Omonia a 1.25-goal head start, and the fair line sits at about +1.36, so the market rates Benfica roughly one and a third goals the stronger side. A single-goal Benfica win would not settle that handicap in their favour. The total-goals line is 3.0, with Over at 49.6% and both teams to score at 52.0%.

The week's moves were small but pointed the same way: Benfica's win chance is up 0.8 of a point, the away handicap is up 0.9 of a point, and Over 3.0 is up 0.5. That is consistent with a squad announcement naming Echeverri as a starter and with the absentees being read as rotation rather than a weakened XI [1][2]. It also fits the goals picture — Benfica have scored 12 in five, and Omonia have scored in each of their last five.

The win and the margin are different questions

Benfica are clear favourites because their six points were earned against Milan and Celtic, and because their chance creation is on another level from Omonia's. Omonia's own six points are real, but they are built on efficiency: 0.32 xG in a winning performance is hard to repeat against a side creating more than two expected goals a game.

Even so, this is not a formality. The home side are unbeaten in five, have the crowd in Nicosia and scored twice on Monday, and Benfica have already shown they can win away without dominating. The market separates the two questions cleanly: Benfica are more likely than not to win, but the margin the price expects is around one and a third goals, so a narrow victory would still leave the handicap open.
