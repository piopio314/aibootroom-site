A first home night, and a long way up the ladder

Lincoln Red Imps are the reigning champions of Gibraltar, and Thursday is their first home match of this Conference League campaign, alsoldelacosta.com reported [2]. The visitors are Twente, an Eredivisie side; Twente are 78.5% to win, the draw is 13.8% and Lincoln are 7.7%, so Twente's chance is about 10.2 times Lincoln's. According to VI, Lincoln sit about two hundred places below the lowest-ranked Eredivisie club on the Euro Club Index [8], a gap reflected in those probabilities. Twente coach John van den Brom hopes to build on the 1-0 win in their previous European fixture, twenteinsite.nl reported [6], and twenteinsite.nl also reported that the Dutch side skipped their training session in Lincoln's stadium on the eve of the match [7].

The gap has a football reason. Lincoln lost their opening league-phase game 1-2 at Brann and created only 0.64 expected goals, while Twente beat Thun 1-0 in their opener and are making chances at about two expected goals a game. Even so, Twente arrive after a 1-2 home defeat to Utrecht on Sunday, which is the one part of the picture that does not fit a calm away win.

The table adds weight to the occasion. In the 36-team league phase Twente sit 14th with three points and Lincoln 27th with none, so the home side are chasing their first point of the campaign in front of their own crowd. For Twente, this is the fixture they are expected to take three points from before harder nights arrive.

Twente's numbers are better than their results

Twente's last five matches average 2.01 expected goals created and 0.77 allowed. That is the shape of a team controlling games: the 1-0 win over Thun came with 2.32 xG created and 0.2 allowed, so the scoreline was far closer than the play. The defeat to Utrecht was different — Twente had slightly the better of the chances, 1.36 to 1.25, and still lost 1-2, which is the result the away price has had to absorb.

Lincoln's domestic form is good: three wins, a draw and a loss in five, with 10 goals scored and four conceded, including home wins of 2-0 over Europa and 4-0 over Europa Point. But the level jumps sharply here, and the Brann match showed how sharply: 0.64 xG created and 2.3 allowed. Both teams had four days between matches, so neither side arrives fresher than the other.

Pavón's hope, and Twente's short stay

Twente coach John van den Brom said he hopes to build on the 1-0 win over Thun [6], and the Dutch side skipped their training session in Lincoln's stadium on the eve of the match [7]. UEFA has appointed Matteo Marcenaro as referee; according to voetbalnieuws.nl, he has officiated only one match involving a Dutch club [10].

Lincoln's coach Juan Manuel Pavón spoke about respecting Twente while still hoping for something from the night [1]. The squad carries its own stories: Lee Casciaro, a 45-year-old policeman, is in his second consecutive European campaign with the club [4], and the goalkeeper Mark Hamm is Dutch, having joined after a LinkedIn conversation last season [3]. Dutch media report that Lincoln are rated considerably stronger than the Gibraltar national team [9].

Almost two goals better, and the line keeps rising

The win chances put Twente far clear, and the handicap says how far. The fair line is now 1.93 goals in Twente's favour, up from 1.83 in recent days, so Twente are rated almost two goals better than Lincoln. Read in plain terms: a two-goal Twente win would be about par on that line, a three-goal win would beat it. That is why the +2 on Lincoln has drifted this week, with the handicap moving 2.6 points toward Twente. According to VI, Lincoln sit about two hundred places below the lowest-ranked Eredivisie club on the Euro Club Index [8], which is the kind of gap the line is trying to hold.

The goals line is 3.25 and Over is now 51.0%, up 3.5 points in recent days, with the fair total pushed from 3.16 to 3.3 goals. Both teams to score sits at 48.0%, which fits a Lincoln side that scores at home domestically but created little in Europe. The away price also shortened by 1.6 points to 78.5%. That movement is consistent with Twente's chance numbers — created 2.01, allowed 0.77 — more than with Sunday's result.

The favourite tag is right, the certainty is not

Twente are strong favourites for reasons that show up in the football: 2.01 expected goals created and 0.77 allowed across five games, against a Lincoln side that made 0.64 at Brann and gave up 2.3. The market has moved in the same direction, shortening Twente's price, lifting their handicap line from 1.83 to 1.93 and pushing the goals line up to 3.25.
