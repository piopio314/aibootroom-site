One team falling, one team standing

Udinese have lost five Serie A games in a row and sit 16th. The side arriving at their stadium on Sunday night is unbeaten in the league and sits fourth. That gap — a team leaking chances against a team scoring freely under a new coach — is what this match is about.

Milan's start under Ruben Amorim is the reason for the short price. Yahoo Sports reports that Amorim is unbeaten in his first seven Serie A games at the club [3], and Milan have taken 15 points from seven matches, with four wins and three draws. Udinese have four points from the same number of games, and they have conceded 13 goals across their five defeats.

One result cuts against all of this. Udinese won 3-0 at San Siro in April, the only Udinese win in the last five meetings between the clubs. Milan had won the previous four, three of them by two goals or more, so April stands out as the exception — and it is the reason this is not a foregone conclusion.

Udinese's chances against, Milan's chances for

The five defeats read worse when you look at the chances. Udinese have conceded 2.42 expected goals per game across the run, and Sunday's 0-3 at home to Lecce was as one-sided as the score: 0.54 xG created, 3.47 allowed. Even their better performances have not paid — against Cagliari they built 2.13 xG and lost 0-1.

Milan's numbers point the other way. They average 2.07 xG for and 0.93 against over their last five in all competitions, with 11 goals scored and five conceded. The 3-2 win over Atalanta came with 2.58 xG, and the 4-0 win at Salzburg with 3.45. Both teams had seven days between matches, so neither side arrives tired or unusually sharp.

An injury report and a boycott

Udinese's team news is mixed. milanreports.com reports that David Alaba is set to miss the match through injury, days after his first Serie A appearance for the club [4] — a senior defensive option missing for the visit of a side averaging more than two expected goals a game. On the positive side, ysscores.com reports that Nicolò Zaniolo has been cleared to return to training after a muscle injury [5], which gives the home coach another option going forward.

The bigger story around Milan this week was off the pitch. The club said in an official statement on acmilan.com that it backed their fans' boycott of the trip over away ticket prices [1], and ESPN reported that Milan said they will retaliate against clubs charging excessive fees [2]. Milan will play in front of a far smaller away support than usual.

What the bookmakers make of it

The market, with the margin removed, makes Milan 58.9% to win, the draw 23.5% and Udinese 17.6%. That is a clear favourite rather than a close call, and it matches the table: fourth against 16th, a side with three draws and no defeats against one with five defeats in a row. The handicap says the same thing in goals. Udinese start with a 0.75-goal lead, and the fair line has moved from 0.74 to 0.87 in Milan's favour in recent days — the market rates Milan close to, but short of, a full goal the better side.

Our read

Milan are clear favourites and rated about nine-tenths of a goal the better side, and there is a football reason for both: Udinese have lost five in a row while conceding 2.42 expected goals a game, and Milan have scored 11 goals in their last five. The price reflects that gap honestly. What it does not carry is April, when Udinese won 3-0 at San Siro with a performance nobody saw coming. Milan are more likely than not to win at Udine — which is not the same as certain.
