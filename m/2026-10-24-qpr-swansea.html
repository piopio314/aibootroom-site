Swansea Lead the Table, but Loftus Road Favours QPR

A table-topper the market will not call favourite

Loftus Road gets a table-topper on Saturday, and the market will not call that table-topper the favourite. Swansea arrive top of the Championship with 23 points from 11 games and seven wins. QPR are ninth, on 17 points, and their last match was a 0-3 defeat at Birmingham.

That scoreline dropped QPR from fifth to ninth [4]. Before it, they had lost only one of their first ten league matches, according to Football League World [5]. Even so, the market has spent this week making them stronger, not weaker.

The visitors have their own bruise. Swansea's four-match winning run ended with a 1-0 home defeat to West Ham on Saturday, a result that cut their lead at the top to a single point [6]. A first wobble for the leaders, a heavy away defeat for the hosts: that is the tension behind a home price that sits closer than the table suggests.

The chances say Swansea, the results say neither

QPR's last five read LWDDD, and the numbers underneath are gentler than the results. At Birmingham they had 1.15 xG to the home side's 1.30 — the chances were close, the score was not. The best display of the run was the 4-1 home win over Derby, where they created 1.52 and allowed 0.86. Across the five they average 1.42 xG for and 1.26 against.

Swansea average 1.76 xG for and 1.34 against over the same span, the better chance-making of the two. The 2-1 win over Norwich (2.36 to 0.68) and the 3-1 over Burnley (2.63 to 1.73) show what first place is built on. But their last two games both finished 1-0, one each way, and they come to London off a blank at home. Both clubs have had seven days since their last match — and recent visits here have gone badly for QPR: Swansea won 2-1 at Loftus Road in April, and by the same score a year earlier.

Four out for QPR, one piece of relief

QPR's team news is the part that looks hardest to carry. Nicolas Madsen, Rumarn Burrell and Tariq Lamptey are out until after the November international break, and Ilias Chair has been ruled out of this match, according to Yahoo Sports [1]. Chair is the usual attacking midfielder, so the home side must find its chances another way.

Amadou Mbengue has also suffered a major injury expected to keep him out for a long layoff, reported by inkl.com [2]. There is relief elsewhere: Richard Kone has been given the all-clear after an injury scare and is expected to be available, according to West London Sport [3]. Swansea's likely change is at right-back, where BBC report Lissah is favourite to start after suspension, with Ronald possibly dropping to the bench [9]. Reports linking manager Vitor Matos with Crystal Palace have brought no approach, according to fansnetwork.co.uk [8].

A quarter of a goal, and a line that moved

Across the 10 bookmakers we compare, with their margin removed, QPR are 40.4% to win, the draw 28.1% and Swansea 31.5%. The handicap line puts that advantage in another way: QPR are rated a quarter of a goal the better side, so the market has a narrow home win in mind rather than a comfortable one. Goals are close to level — Over 2.5 is 48.6% and the fair total sits at 2.45 — and both teams to score is 54.6%.

The week's movement went towards the home side. QPR's win chance rose by almost three points to 40.4%, Swansea's fell by three points, and the fair handicap line moved from -0.07 to -0.17 goals in QPR's favour — despite QPR's 0-3 at Birmingham (westlondonsport.com) [4] and four absentees (sports.yahoo.com) [1].

Our read: the venue against the table

The table says Swansea, the home ground says QPR, and both are describing the same afternoon. The leaders have the better season and the better chance quality, but they arrive after their run ended and after a blank at home. QPR have the venue, a squad missing its creative core, and a price that has shortened anyway. Our read: this is a game the market expects to be settled by a single goal, with QPR narrowly the more likely to find it. Swansea's chance of leaving west London with three points is real, but smaller than a league position eight places higher would suggest — and two recent visits here ended 2-1 to the visitors, so the caution has history behind it.
