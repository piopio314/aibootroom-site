A table that flatters the hosts

Fortuna Sittard sit sixth in the Eredivisie with 15 points from nine games, four points and three places above Excelsior. They host them on Sunday afternoon. The table says the home side should be comfortable. The chances they create say something different: 0.77 expected goals per game across their last five matches.

Excelsior's problem is easier to see. They have not won in five league games (LLDLD) and have conceded 15 goals in that run, three per match. They lost 0-4 at Heerenveen last time and 1-5 at home to Groningen the week before. Only two points came from those five fixtures.

So Sunday brings together a team that cannot stop conceding and a team that struggles to create. That is the tension of the afternoon: Fortuna are the better-placed side and the favourites, but they are not a team that blows opponents away. Excelsior, meanwhile, have scored six in five — they are not silent either.

A point rescued by the goalkeeper

Fortuna drew 2-2 at NEC Nijmegen on Sunday, and Anthony Descotte opened the scoring in the 18th minute [1]. The shape of the game, though, was one-sided. NEC out-created Fortuna 3.06 to 0.45 in expected goals, and Mattijs Branderhorst saved a penalty in the closing stages according to L1 Nieuws [2]. That is a point built on goalkeeping and finishing, not on control.

The same pattern runs through the whole run of results. Fortuna conceded 4.13 expected goals in the 1-5 home defeat to Ajax on 12 September, and they allow 1.97 expected goals per game. Excelsior's numbers point the same way: 2.17 expected goals against per game and no clean sheet in the winless stretch. Fortuna have had seven days since their last match, Excelsior nine.

Branderhorst and the small margins

Fortuna's most important player right now may be their goalkeeper. Branderhorst saved that late penalty at NEC, and he told L1 Nieuws he has mixed feelings about the result given his own decisive contribution [3]. With a side that gives up around two expected goals a game, the goalkeeper is not a detail.

At the other end, Descotte's early goal at NEC was one of the few clear chances Fortuna have produced in recent weeks. L1 Nieuws notes that the league campaign continues at home on Sunday [5]. For Excelsior the issue is collective rather than individual: 15 goals conceded in five games, and no settled answer at the back.

Our read

The market has this about right: a small home edge, a high goal line, and no clear gap in class. The favouritism rests on Excelsior's defensive run and on the table, not on Fortuna's attacking output — 0.77 expected goals per game is why the home chance is 39.4% and not far higher. Both teams give up good chances, which explains a line of three goals. Fortuna are the favourites, but only just, and a side that creates that little may have to grind it out rather than control it.
