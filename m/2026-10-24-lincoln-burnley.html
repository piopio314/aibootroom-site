Burnley favoured at Lincoln, but the line says almost level

Bottom-three Burnley, and still the favourites

Burnley arrive at Sincil Bank on Saturday in 22nd place, with one league win in eleven games. Lincoln are 18th, three places and four points better off. The lower of those two teams is the one the market makes favourite, and the reason is not hard to find: Burnley's 4-0 win at Watford this month is the standout result either side has produced this season.

Both clubs are chasing the same thing. Lincoln have not won at home in the league this season, and their last home game was a 0-6 defeat by West Ham on 14 October. Burnley have gone a full year without a home league win, a run extended by Saturday's 1-2 defeat to Wolves [2]. Something has to give this weekend, and history offers no guide: this is Burnley's first visit to Sincil Bank since 1999 [10].

The pressure sits on the away dugout. Nicky Hayen has won one of his first eleven games, and according to burnleyexpress.net he is still expected to be in the dugout on Saturday [1]. A manager working under that weight, with his team in the bottom three [3], is the backdrop to a match Burnley are priced to win.

Lincoln's results are worse than their chances

Two of Lincoln's recent defeats came in games they controlled. They out-created Sheffield United 1.39 to 0.68 in October and lost 0-3, and out-created Swansea 2.11 to 0.92 in September and lost 1-2. The West Ham night was different: Lincoln created 0.39 expected goals and allowed 3.23. Friday's draw at Millwall followed the pattern of the defeats rather than the wins — 0.75 created, 1.87 allowed — a point that came from finishing, not from control.

Burnley's five games read the same way: 1.25 expected goals created per game, 1.83 allowed. The Watford win, 1.94 to 0.64, is the only one of the five where they clearly won the chance count; the home draws with Charlton and Derby and the Wolves defeat all had them second-best on xG. Burnley have had eight days to prepare, one more than Lincoln's seven, and both teams have been open: Lincoln's last five games produced 17 goals, and Burnley's exactly the same.

Fifteen out, and a captain handled with care

Burnley's team news is the biggest number of the week. The early list for the trip to Lincoln names 15 players ruled out and one fitness doubt, reported by burnleyexpress.net [4]. Hayen will select from what is left, and even a returning captain is being treated with care — Josh Laurent has urged caution over Josh Cullen's imminent comeback, per the same outlet [5]. The mood around the club is not calm either: the outlet also reports police clarifying their involvement in a confrontation over a fan banner at Turf Moor [6].

Lincoln have had the better week. Deji Elerewe is available again after the club won their appeal against his red card at Millwall, reported by staceywest.net [8]. They go into Saturday still looking for that first home league win, and the club prepared with a behind-closed-doors game against a Premier League side, reported by uk.sports.yahoo.com [9].

A clear favourite, a near-even line

Across the ten bookmakers we compare, with the margin removed, Burnley are 41.7% to win, Lincoln 31.8% and the draw 26.4%. That is a clear lean to the away side, well beyond Lincoln's share, and it fits a team that has produced a 4-0 away win and five draws that keep the season alive. The handicap tells a different story. Lincoln get a head start of 0.25 goals, and that line lands at 51.8% — close to a coin flip. The market rates Burnley only about a fifth of a goal better, even though they sit three places and four points behind Lincoln in the table.

The goals line is just as balanced: a fair total of 2.53, with Over 2.5 at 50.8% and both teams to score at 55.7%. Both sides concede steadily and both have produced 17 goals in their last five, so the line is where the recent form points. Burnley's win chance sits at 41.7% against a Lincoln side that lost 0-6 at home last time out and a Burnley side that has won once in eleven — the market still leans away, but only just past a coin flip.

Our read

Put the two market numbers together and the game makes sense. Burnley are favourites because of one afternoon at Watford and because Lincoln's home record includes a six-goal defeat. But the same evidence cuts the other way: one win in eleven, a year without a home league win, fifteen players missing and a manager under pressure. Lincoln, meanwhile, have out-created the opposition twice in their last five games and lost both — a team whose position is worse than its football. The read: Burnley's advantage is real but thin, and the most likely shape of this match is a draw or a single-goal result either way.
