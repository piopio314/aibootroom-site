A derby that squeezes both ways

Valencia go into this regional derby 19th in La Liga with 6 points from nine games: one win, three draws, five defeats and a goal difference of -9. OneFootball reports that Javier Aguirre's side need a win at Mestalla to ease the pressure after that start [1], and the same outlet describes this as a regional derby with plenty riding on it [11]. For the home crowd, this is not a neutral afternoon.

Villarreal come south from a different kind of week. They led 2-0 at Anfield on Tuesday and lost 4-3 [8]. Their manager refused to single anyone out afterwards, calling it a joint collective effort and saying he did not think it was anything other than football, as reported by the Liverpool Echo [9]. That is a calm message after a painful night.

The table says the visitors are seven places higher, 12th with 9 points. It does not say they are flying: four defeats in nine league games. So the derby sets a team that needs a result and has had a week to prepare against a team with better chances created, a better recent record in this fixture, and a long journey in their legs.

Two draws have stopped the fall

Valencia's last two league games were draws: 2-2 at Racing Santander with 1.80 xG to 1.28, then 0-0 at home to Athletic Club with 1.41 xG to 0.59. Only two goals scored and two conceded in that pair. OneFootball reports that Aguirre has steadied the team and that the injury list is easing [3]. That is the base they will try to build on, and it is why this is not treated as a mismatch.

Over the last five matches Valencia average 1.10 xG created and 1.30 allowed. The 2-3 home defeat to Real Sociedad stands out: they allowed 2.65 xG that day. Villarreal, by contrast, average 2.02 xG created and 1.26 allowed across their last five. They made 2.38 xG at home to Elche last weekend and drew 0-0. Chance creation is not their problem; finishing has been. Villarreal also have only 4 days between Anfield and kick-off, while Valencia have had 7 since Athletic.

Returns, not absences, shape the home side

Valencia's news this week is about players coming back. Copete could feature seven months after the serious knee injury he suffered against Villarreal, according to OneFootball and El Desmarque [5][6]. Umar Sadiq, out since an injury in September, is also nearing a return, per Premium Times [4]. A centre-back returning against the opponent he was hurt by would lift Mestalla, and Aguirre needs defensive options after conceding in four of the last five league games.

Superdeporte reports that Aguirre's main concern before this match is the defensive help from his wingers [7] — a detail that matters against a side creating two expected goals a game. On the other side, Aguirre also spoke warmly about Harvey Elliott's work ethic and sharper physical shape during his loan spell, according to This Is Anfield [2].

A quarter-goal game, about three goals

The win chances, with the margin removed, put Villarreal at 44.0%, Valencia at 30.1% and the draw at 25.9%. So the visitors are clearly more likely to win, but more than a quarter of the weight sits on the draw. The handicap line makes the same point in goals: Valencia receive a quarter of a goal, with the fair line reading +0.27 from their side, so the home side is given a start rather than the line reading as a full rating of Villarreal's edge.

The total-goals line is 2.75, with Over at 49.6% and both teams to score at 58.4% — the market expects chances at both ends rather than a cagey 0-0. The week's moves are small, four tenths of a point on either win chance, consistent with two pieces of news pulling in opposite directions: Valencia's two unbeaten games and extra rest against Villarreal's quality and their 3-4 at Anfield. Villarreal have won two of the last five meetings and drawn two, including 0-2 at Mestalla last October and 2-1 in February. That record fits the away favouritism, not just the table.

Our read

Valencia have the need, the home crowd and three extra days of rest; Villarreal have the chance creation, the recent record in this fixture and the better position in the table. Neither side is convincing enough for the market to make this anything more than a quarter-goal game. The football and the price agree on the shape of it: a tight derby with goals in it, where the visitors are more likely than not to win, and a home side that can take something if it defends the wide areas better than it has been doing.
