Bottom of the table, and still the favourites

Estoril go into Sunday's home game with Moreirense bottom of the Primeira Liga — 18th of 18, two points from eight matches, no win. A home fixture against a side that has lost three of its last five is exactly the match a team in that position needs. It is also, according to the Portuguese press, the match that will decide who coaches them next.

A Bola reports the board is increasingly inclined to keep faith with Sérgio Raimundo and sees this game as the real test [1], while O Jogo reports a decision on the permanent job is due by Wednesday [2]. The club has already made one change this month: Vasco Matos's departure was confirmed a week ago [4]. So the interim coach names his team on Sunday knowing the answer arrives days later.

Moreirense are 12th with eight points, six more than Estoril, but they arrive after a 0-1 cup defeat at AVS SAD, Rodrigo Pinho scoring in the third minute [7]. Coach Vasco Botelho da Costa said it 'may seem almost polemical' but he had to be very satisfied with the performance [8]. And this fixture has been tight for two years: the last five meetings gave Moreirense two wins and three draws, the latest 1-0 in April, and the one before that a 3-3 at Estoril.

Chances created, points lost

Estoril's last five in all competitions read D-L-L-L-D. Their season averages are 0.98 expected goals created and 1.44 allowed per game, which does not, on its own, point to a clear home favourite. The most recent league match argued the other way: at Academico Viseu they created 1.36 expected goals to the hosts' 0.73 and lost 2-1.

The cup offered little relief. Estoril needed a late goal to force extra time, then penalties, to get past lower-league Salgueiros after a 1-1 [5]. Moreirense's run is L-L-D-W-L, and the one win was 3-1 at home to Maritimo with 2.0 expected goals; away at Vitória SC they drew 1-1 with 0.52. Both clubs played a cup tie in the last week, so rest is close to even — seven days for Estoril, eight for Moreirense.

A new centre-back pair for the visitors

Estoril's attack could be thinner. A Bola reports that forward Begraoui picked up a muscle injury during the international break [3] — a problem for a side with three goals in their last five matches.

The clearer news is in the away defence. Record reports Maracás is absent and Gilberto Batista will partner Kevyn in a new centre-back pairing [9]. Dinis Pinto, who missed the cup game with physical complaints, is following a recovery plan and is doubtful [10], though A Bola reports he is aiming to be fit [11]. Carlos Macedo will referee, with Bruno Esteves on VAR [12].

Half a goal, and a line on the fence

With the margins removed, Estoril are 50.6% to win, the draw 26.0% and Moreirense 23.5%. The bottom side is rated more than twice as likely as the side six points above them. The handicap says it another way: the line is half a goal, so Estoril are rated just the better side by the margin of half a goal, and that fair line has edged from -0.52 to -0.54.

The goals line sits almost exactly on two and a half — Over 2.5 at 50.3%, the fair total up from 2.45 to 2.51. That is consistent with two defences that give up chances: Moreirense allow 1.58 expected goals per game, Estoril 1.44. The week's moves were small and pointed the same way: Over 2.5 up 1.7 points, both teams to score 'yes' up 1.5, the draw's chance down half a point. Neither the Maracás reshuffle nor the Begraoui report moved Estoril's win price.

Our read

No win in eight, an interim coach whose future is decided this week, a forward reported injured — that is one side of Sunday. The other is a home team that out-created its opponent in its last league match, against a visitor that has lost three of five, is out of the cup, and must field a centre-back pairing that has not played together. The market has weighed the second list more heavily and set Estoril as clear favourites, half a goal the better side. That price rests on the chances, not on the table: whether Estoril finally turn them into a first win, or into a fourth draw in five meetings, is the open question.
